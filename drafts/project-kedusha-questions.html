Questions for Yaakov

To turn the draft into a story. Last year's spread worked because one person was talking the whole way through, and we currently have no voice like that. What we need most is a scene and a few real numbers; everything else is bonus.

The one thing we're missing: a scene

  • Where were you when Project Kedusha stopped being an idea? What was the conversation, and what made it obvious?
  • What happened when the first printed copy arrived? Who did you hand it to, and what did they say?
  • One Kedusha.com conversation that surprised you, anonymized, in whatever detail you can share. A single real exchange is worth three paragraphs of description.
  • Did anyone say no? Gathering other organizations' resources into one volume can't have been simple. A moment of friction, even one that ended well, is what makes an article read as true.
