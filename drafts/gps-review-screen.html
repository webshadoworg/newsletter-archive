Don't quote these

Same screen I run on everything before drafting. This book is the worst case in the library for the problem: a line we want and a line that would do damage, fifteen lines apart on the same page. Two chapters are the source of almost all of it.

Never quote

p353 — the "YOUR SINS" anaphora. Three consecutive paragraphs: *"YOUR SINS are what prevent you from feeling completely connected to Hashem…", "YOUR SINS cause you to think that perhaps you are not the person Hashem really meant to call 'My beloved precious son!'"* This tells a man his sins are the reason he doubts he is beloved. It's the exact inverse of the series thesis, and the most damaging passage in the book.

p362 — the labelling passage. You *"have now become: 'a smoker,' 'an alcoholic,' 'a drug addict,' 'a compulsive gambler,' a בעל תאוה…"* — and p369's *"He is … the new YOU."* Fixed-identity labelling of the person rather than the behaviour, which flatly contradicts Chapter 7's own argument.

p365 — "You become a living, breathing hypocrite." Second person, addressed to him.

p369 — the chapter's close. *"Right now you can't pull yourself up …. You are powerless …"* followed by *"Looks like you need an: EXTREME SPIRITUAL MAKEOVER!"* Powerlessness, then an extreme makeover as the answer, and no first step anywhere on the page.

pp. 206–220, "Building Muscle" — the free sample. Addressed throughout to a person who eventually wins.

Two more that aren't on my formal list but shouldn't go in a GYE email: p355's *"whether he is bringing a sacrifice in the Bais HaMikdash or walking into the gas chambers of Auschwitz"*, and p366's Itchy Yitzy cycle, which writes relapse out in graphic bleeding-and-infection detail.

The page-number traps

This is why I won't cite a bare page number from this book, and why nobody else should either:

  • p360 holds the best sentence in the two chapters — and runs straight into pp361–362, the identity-loss

and labelling material. Quote p360, stop at the page turn.

  • p369 holds a quote I'd build a piece around *and* the powerlessness close, about fifteen lines apart.

Citing "p369" without naming the paragraph is a coin flip.

  • p353 is where the chapter's best *idea* is sourced — the yetzer's real aim is קטנות הדעת, from

נתיבות שלום חלק א׳ פרקי מבוא דף י"א — and it's also where the "YOUR SINS" block lives. Take the idea from p351, which carries the same source line with none of the anaphora.

  • Concept Two's premise (your discomfort is the goal, contentment is the danger) is one step from "so

feel worse." The rail is p404. They travel together or not at all.

Credit where it's due

Two things these chapters get right that most of this genre doesn't:

  • Nothing in Chapters 25–26 says "you were given this test so you could pass it." Not once. That's the

failure mode I flag most often, and this book avoids it.

  • Nothing in them is graphic about the aveira itself. Both are conspicuously abstract — "certain

specific sins (thoughts or actions)" — using smoking, drinking and itching as stand-ins.

The real limitation

Chapters 25–26 are diagnosis only. Chapter 26 ends at *"You are powerless … and — boy — does that hurt!"* and points at the next section. It never tells the man he is still beloved *while* he is failing, and it hands him no next step. So it describes him accurately and then leaves him standing there.

That gap is the reason our series exists. If we take the diagnosis, the answer has to be in the same piece — not one section later.
