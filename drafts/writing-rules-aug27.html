1Two or three sentences a paragraph, not five

Both of tonight's big cuts were 5 sentences to 2, and nothing was lost.

wasWhich puts kvetching in a rather different light. A running complaint about the traffic, the job, the tone somebody used with us, feels like nothing. It's a mood, not an aveirah. But every kvetch is a small announcement that things are not good, and a man who has been making those announcements since morning is a man already looking for some relief. That is the mood the whole thing waits for, because the relief on offer is giving in.
nowKvetching is a small announcement that things are not good, and makes us feel like we need relief. And that invites acting out.

2Delete the sentence whose only job is to introduce the next sentence

You killed every one of these, in both sections:

DeletedWhat it was doing
“Which puts kvetching in a rather different light.”announcing a turn instead of taking it
“And notice when bikkurim happens.”telling the reader to notice
“Why should a list on a piece of paper do anything at all?”inventing the reader's scepticism
“That's worth more to us than it looks.”claiming importance instead of showing it
“Because of where the pull actually comes from.”promising an explanation instead of being one
“From here we see that…”you replaced it with “Bottom line:”

Rule: open the paragraph on its content. If the first sentence can go without losing a fact, it was scaffolding.

3Delete the closer that sums up

You cut “and it reads less like a blessing than an instruction,” and cut “A list you rattle off is a routine. A list you have to think about does something to you.”

The article now ends on the member's own words: “it gives me the strength to get through everything.” A real voice beats a neat line.

4One example, not three. One contrast, not a rhythm

Gone: “the traffic, the job, the tone somebody used with us” (three items doing one item's work), and “It's a mood, not an aveirah” (the not-X-but-Y rhythm, which by then had run several times in the piece).

5Plain words, and no vocabulary borrowed from another field

wasThe bad feeling is the delivery mechanism.
nowFeeling bad is the opening it needs.

You said “delivery mechanism is weird.” It was also wrong on its own terms: a delivery mechanism carries something, and a bad mood doesn't carry the taavah, it lets it in.

6Never cut information. Only texture

The one thing you put back. I tightened “who gets credit for them so that it's more detailed” down to “who to credit for them” for rhythm, and lost the reason the elaboration is in the exercise at all.

Rule: before a cut, ask what the words are carrying, not how they sound.

7A parallel to a mekor is “an idea we see,” not “the same point”

wasThat's the same point bikkurim is making.
nowThat's also an idea we see with bikkurim.

You walked back your own sentence, unprompted, and it was the right call: mikra bikkurim is not a gratitude exercise with a why attached.

Same paragraph, companion rule: describe a study in its own terms, then draw the parallel in our voice. I wrote that the participants were asked “who to thank.” They were asked for causes. Thanking is ours. You took “who gets credit.”

8Name the actor

wasa happier person is much harder to catch
nowmuch harder for the yetzer hara to catch

Unstated agency is a defect, and worst in the sentence that carries the point.

9Never hand the reader a verdict on himself

“a person would be turned corrupt by it” became “would be harmed by it”. The Hebrew literally says turned into a corrupt person, and our reader is that person.

“the remorse should be done in a focused way” became “feel remorse as best you can,” which releases a reader who can't produce a feeling on command.

10Your register stands. I shouldn't formalise it

Comma splices are speech, not errors. “Bikkurim isn't brought at the end of the harvest, it's the reishis.” Printed as sent. At 5:47 I turned one of yours into a full stop, which was me tidying your voice.

A plain question is not the AI tell. You put “Why does gratitude help?” at the head of the same paragraph I'd stripped a question from. Three words, a question the reader is actually asking, answered in the same breath. The tell is the manufacturing, not the question mark.

Italics: you removed the ones I added on house convention. Ask, don't infer.

One bold run per section, on the mechanism the piece rests on, so a skimmer who reads one thing gets the argument.

11Two paragraphs may not open the same way

“Notice what they were asked to write” and “And notice when bikkurim happens” shipped two paragraphs apart. I didn't see it because each was defensible alone.

Any device used twice in one piece is a tic. Before publishing I now list my own paragraph openers and closers and count them. Same failure mode as the em dash.

And one thing to do every time

Cut whole units that repeat, not just words. At 4:03 you deleted an entire Lubavitcher Rebbe letter because it said what the letter above it said and dragged a side issue in. Redundancy at the paragraph and source level costs more than any sentence.
