The sentence a reader does not already believe

“To this day, every Jewish marriage is built on a law we learn from Avraham buying a burial plot.” That is not a devar Torah flourish, it is the actual halacha: kiddushin by money is learned from Avraham paying Efron for a grave. The claim that follows is that marriage was defined off a burial on purpose, because the part of it that counts is the part that is underground.

Sarah dies, Avraham buys a cave, and the Torah goes straight into Yitzchak and Rivka. Why learn how to start a marriage from the story that ended one? Because every relationship has a layer above the ground, built out of feeling, and a layer beneath it that feeling did not build and cannot remove. Avraham bought a cave to say his bond with Sarah had not ended, since it had never begun. Then the last section turns the whole thing onto the reader and Hashem.

You cannot destroy a well

Well Therapy. theyeshiva.net item 916, essay, Nov 23 2012. Sent by Chayim, Nov 1, as a rewrite. · my rank 3 of 7

The sentence a reader does not already believe

“Just as you cannot create a well, you also cannot destroy one.” You can block it or divert it, and the spring is still down there. So when “a voice says the relationship is dead”, the claim is not try harder. The claim is that dead is not one of the things it can be.

Yitzchak, Yaakov and Moshe all met their wives at a well. Why a well and not a river? Because a well is the one body of water that takes real digging and still owes you nothing: you do not make a drop of it, you only uncover what was already there. Jacobson reads marriage off that shape. The bond is pre-existing, so our work is excavation, not construction, and excavation is the one job that cannot fail for good.

Whether your wife laughs at your mistake or calls it abusive was decided long before you made it. And the reason a man cannot believe his wife loves him is usually that he does not like himself. In his words, “sometimes when you hate yourself, you can’t appreciate that somebody else loves you”.

From a marriage seminar he ran in Monsey. A wife puts the rubbish right against the door so her husband cannot miss it, and “he climbed over the garbage bag” and went to shul. She tells it as a joke. Then the turn: giving the benefit of the doubt is not a decision you make in the moment, it is downstream of what you already believe about yourself.
