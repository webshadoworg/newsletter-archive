Section Five — the six Concepts, corrected

pp. 371–466. This is the "program" you remembered, and it is a real ladder: six Concepts in a deliberate order. The summary that circulated had four of the six wrong — because the scanned PDF has two of its three blocks in reverse page order, so reading it front-to-back reads the back half backwards.

What the earlier summary got wrong

It said Concept One was "essence over pleasure / Jumping the Gun" — that's Concept Five. It said Concept Four was olam habinyan vs. olam hachurban — that's Concept Two, p404. Its Concepts Two, Five and Six are all really pieces of the single Concept Six. Only its Three and Six were right.

Two knock-on effects: it folded three "Concepts" out of one, and it missed Concepts One and Four entirely — and Concept One is the most on-theme idea in the section.

The real six

One — "This is NOT the real me!" (p385) Split your external image from your internal essence. The neshama is angelic, the body animal, and since Adam the yetzer sits *inside* consciousness as a snake — so the sins belong to the snake, not to "you." It pre-empts the obvious bechira objection with a boxing mashal: your weak resistance is itself the snake's sabotage. Three proofs you're still pure, including *"it only hurts to fall from a HIGH place."*

Two — "Recognize that YOU cannot continue like this!" (p393) The friction you feel now is the goal, not a problem; the danger is contentment. The Kobriner: the lost Jew is the one who משלים עם מציאותו הרוחנית — the man in the pit who stopped screaming. That inner scream is your אתחלתא דגאולה. Then the safety rail on p404: this has to come from עולם הבנין, never עולם החורבן, or the yetzer converts it straight into יאוש. Never use the premise without the rail.

Three — "Digging deep and really changing" (p407) Change the mindset at its origin, not the act. Stopping the act is the *first* step, not the last. So remove the trigger — and this is where the book gets operational in a way nothing else in it does:

"You may even think that you are doing great and putting up a decent fight against the Evil Inclination. However, it is the Yetzer Horah itself who set the parameters of your fight! … It's happy that you have the computer and you are fighting over how many times you watch inappropriate content. So even your FIGHT is being controlled by IT! … For once, it's time for YOU to control the parameters of the fight!"

p417 · mekor: נתיבות שלום חלק ב׳ קמ"ז

That's the argument *for* filters and gedarim, made without shaming the reader for needing them. It's the only place in the book that speaks directly about a computer.

Four — "Reclaiming the right perspective" (p421) Materialistic desire shrinks the mind into קטנות המוחין until, like two coins held over the eyes, petty pleasure blots out a million-dollar world. The sharp move is on p428: the small-mindedness was the yetzer's actual objective — the sins were only the means of getting you there. So don't fight temptation on its own level; rise above it.

Five — "Discovering REAL happiness" (p439) The soul is a בת מלך, so worldly pleasure can't satisfy it and the emptiness is a hunger signal *from* the soul. Pouring in more pleasure deepens it. Happiness comes from *achieving*, never from *receiving* — and the mark of a happiness-producing act is that it was hard and didn't feel good.

Six — "How YOU can bond with the Almighty?" (p455) The whole point of the 613 is dveykus, and then the inversion: the *less* you're in the mood, the more the act is purely His will — so difficulty is a better connection, not a worse one. Then ups and downs as the design, and this, which names the shame instead of stepping around it:

"After every 'up' comes a 'down' and after every 'down' comes an 'up.' There is no way around this. We are 'programmed' to think that we can only try and connect to Hashem when we are on an 'up,' and so we fall apart and disconnect when we are on a 'down.' We feel 'low' and dirty. We feel embarrassed and ashamed. But most of all … we feel alone."

p462 · mekor: נתיבות שלום חלק ב׳ עמוד שמ״ט

The Hebrew at that same locus is the cleanest statement of it:

יש דבקות מתוך גדלות, כאשר יהודי מרגיש את גדלות הבורא ומתענג על ה׳, ויש דבקות בה׳ דוקא כאשר יהודי נמצא בשפל המצב! וזהו תכלית קבלת התורה, שישראל יהיו תמיד דבקים בה׳ כחשיכה כאורה.

Nesivos Shalom II p. 349

And p464 adds yesod hasimcha, a separate citation from the Purim maamarim (נתיבות שלום עניני פורים מגילת אסתר - מגילה לנמצא בהסתר פנים אות ד׳):

יסוד השמחה הוא: להאמין כי בתוך כל ההסתרים הקדוש ברוך הוא אתו ואני ה׳ השוכן אתם גם בתוך טומאתם!!!

Nesivos Shalom, Purim — Megillas Esther, §4

What sits just before this section

Chapters 25 ("Losing Altitude," p343) and 26 ("Confusion," p357) are the diagnosis, and Chapter 26 is the addiction chapter proper. It concedes the failure in advance, which almost nothing in this genre does:

"even if you make a clear decision that you truly want to improve, your inner resolve and willpower are simply not strong enough and you will continue to stumble over and over again."

p360 · mekor: נתיבות שלום חלק ב׳ עניני פסח מאמר י"ב אות ד' עמוד ע"ר

That removes the "you just didn't want it enough" accusation in one sentence. And p369 — *"You try to find ways to blame outside circumstances … You feel like a loser"* — names his inner life without moralising about it. Naming "you feel like a loser" as a symptom of the condition rather than a verdict is exactly the move our reader needs.

But those two chapters also hold the worst material in the book, sometimes on the same page as the quotes above. See the next page before drafting from them.
