“Project Kedusha” - A New Year of REAL Change

“I’m struggling with you-know-what”
“I think my husband is having a hard time”
“I’ve been clean for two years and slipped last night, is it over?”
“My chavrusah confided in me and I don’t know what to say to him”

Every day, questions like these are asked by Yidden around the world.

A father in Lakewood: “My son saw something on his phone, what do I do?”
A rebbi in Flatbush: “I need help with a Talmid”
A mother in Yerushalayim: “We can’t afford a filter”
A bochur, alone, at two in the morning: “I gave my word to my Rav and broke it again”...

Take the father in Lakewood. Eleven at night, standing in his kitchen, holding a phone that isn’t his, having just seen what his son saw. By morning he will have to speak to him. He won’t call the neighbors and he won’t wake his Rav, so he does what everyone does. He opens a browser and starts typing. A thousand answers come back, none of them written for him.

Kedusha is one of the greatest challenges facing Klal Yisroel today, and one of the hardest to even speak about.

Twenty Years on the Front Lines

For 19 years, Guard Your Eyes has been on the front lines of this struggle. It was founded by Yaakov Nadel and Yechezkel Stelzer, with the guidance of Rabbi Dr. Abraham Twerski zt”l and, yibadel lechaim, Rav Aharon Feldman shlita. The battle began with the internet, then smartphones, then social media, and now AI. As the technology changes, so must our response.

Through it all, GYE’s strength has been helping those already struggling, from typical teenage nisyonos through full-blown addiction, with tools built on Torah sources and the latest behavioral science. The framework readers met in these pages last year still holds: three pillars, motivation, planning and connection, held together by mentors, forums and a community who understand exactly what you’re going through.

And we never stop innovating.

As the world grows comfortable talking with AI, a door opened. Today, when a new member reaches out, GYE welcomes him. No forms, no passwords, just a conversation. With our proprietary AI, trained on Torah sources, mental-health professionals, and our most experienced mentors, he feels understood from the first minute. And behind the scenes stand real people, ready to step in.

“A man will type at two in the morning what he could never say out loud at two in the afternoon. Our only job is to make sure that what answers him is Torah, and that a human being is standing right behind it.”

Thank God, tens of thousands have broken free. Behind every number: marriages rebuilt, self-respect restored, fathers present again for their children.

Before the Crisis Starts

But helping people in crisis was never the whole story. Strengthening people before a serious struggle begins matters just as much, and GYE has been there from the earliest days. Back in 2013 we helped build the VCF filter, which evolved into GenTech. We developed Webshadow for parent-child accountability, published filter guides and prevention tips for parents, and ran community Filterthons.

That work continues today with our recently launched Project STEP. Many people want a filter and never get one. They aren’t tech-savvy, they’re worried it will block too much, or they can’t afford it. Project STEP walks them through the whole process, with sponsors covering the cost for families in need.

We’ve also taken these solutions directly to the people. For adults, the Kedusha Challenge: 30 days, run by a Rav in his own kehilla over WhatsApp, with a short dvar chizuk and one practical challenge a day. It ran in over 40 shuls last Shovavim. For teens, Esa Einai, a 50-day Shovavim program now used by thousands. Boys told us it was the strongest winter z’man they had ever had.

A Shulchan Aruch for Kedusha

Centuries ago, a Yid seeking practical halacha had to search across countless seforim: Bavli, Yerushalmi, Rishonim. Many didn’t know where to turn until Rav Yosef Karo wrote the Shulchan Aruch. Lehavdil, that is our challenge today.

Klal Yisroel is blessed with remarkable resources, built by many different organizations. Filters. Kosher devices. Education. Recovery. And it is overwhelming. Where do you start? What’s right for my child? For my community? Which is exactly where that father in the kitchen was standing.

“We stopped asking what else GYE should build. The question became: why is there no single place a Yid can go with any question in this area and simply get an answer?”

That question became Project Kedusha, and it has three parts. The Kedusha Guide, in print. Kedusha.com, online and powered by AI. And a hotline, for those without internet.

Not a Pamphlet. A Book.

The Kedusha Guide is close to 100 full-color pages, the first of its kind: the best resources in the Jewish community gathered into one volume, in six sections: Kedusha Education, Chizuk, Struggles, Safeguards, Screen Addiction, and Kosher Entertainment.

For parents: how to have the conversation, for every age. For chizuk: daily videos, shiurim and seforim. For those who struggle: the complete GYE framework, and therapy referrals. For protection, every filter, every kosher phone, all 70-plus TAG offices, even the devices people forget: the Xbox, the smart TV, the Tesla. And for the family, kosher entertainment for every age, so parents finally have an answer to “I’m bored.”

Wherever you see the GYE badge, it’s ours. Everything else is the work of others. Because this isn’t about one organization. It’s all of Klal Yisroel’s solutions, in one place.

The response has been extraordinary. Rav Yosef Elefent of the Mir called it a game changer. The goal is thousands of copies, through shuls, schools, and events across America and Eretz Yisroel. Because when a book sits on a shelf at home, or on a table in shul, it does something profound. It normalizes the conversation. No searching. No shame.

You Can Just Ask

A printed book is fixed the day it leaves the press, and technology changes daily. Which is why we built Kedusha.com: everything in the Guide, alive online, updated continuously with our partners at Mishor in Lakewood. When a new filter launches, a new device appears, a new challenge emerges, Kedusha.com already knows.

Or you can do something no book has ever done.

The father in the kitchen types one sentence: “I found my son watching things he shouldn’t.” What answers him is not a page of search results. It responds with warmth, then does what a wise mentor would: it asks how old his son is, and what device. Only then does it point him to the right resources, and to how the conversation in the morning should sound.

A rebbi with a talmid who’s struggling. A man who can only bring himself to type, “I need help with… you-know-what.” A mother looking for kosher videos. Each guided to exactly what they need. Anonymously. Instantly. Without judgment. Every conversation is reviewed anonymously by our team, so the site learns, and shapes the next edition of the Guide.

And for those without internet, the same intelligence is a phone call away: 866-KEDUSHA. Ask your question, get your answer.

Every Jewish Bookshelf

Remember those questions? The father asking about his son’s first phone now has an address. The rebbi searching for resources now has a library. The mother who couldn’t afford a filter has Project STEP. And the bochur, alone at two in the morning, is not alone anymore.

Guard Your Eyes has always been identified with crisis, and crisis is hard to speak about; many hesitate to reach out because of stigma and shame. Project Kedusha changes that. GYE becomes part of a much broader conversation: raising healthy families, education, wise technology choices, and yes, helping those who struggle.

Which is why the Guide was not written only for the person in the middle of a struggle. Every family in Klal Yisroel is already making these decisions: the first phone for a bar mitzvah bochur, the laptop a school now requires, the smart TV a neighbor is giving away. Most of us decide alone, quickly, and hope for the best. Suddenly the Kedusha Guide belongs on every Jewish bookshelf — not because of a crisis, but because everyone is navigating technology.

Every family deserves a trusted place to turn. Project Kedusha. One small step for countless individuals. One giant upgrade to Klal Yisroel’s Kedusha.

How to get the Kedusha Guide

The Guide is available now. Shuls, schools and organizations can request copies in quantity; an individual copy can be ordered link or number: need from you. Everything in it, kept current, lives at kedusha.com.

None of it runs on its own. GYE’s annual campaign, dates and goal: need from you, is what puts a Guide on the shelf of a home that would never have thought to ask for one, and keeps a mentor on the other end of the line at two in the morning.
