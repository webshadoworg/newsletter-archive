A · Why the Days After Rosh Hashana and Yom Kippur Can Be Challenging  × ruled out By @HashemHelpMe · 394 words · Ran in GYE’s own Sukkos issue, Oct 5 2025. Named author.

Out. #848: we do not re-print what we already printed.

This is the piece that answers the question the issue is already asking. Your forum column is three men arguing about what happens after a good stretch. He explains the mechanism: it is not a kedusha problem, it is that the nervous system goes looking for relief after intense days, and that is why Motzai Yom Tov is when men fall. Short, named, practical, and it points forward at Motzai Simchas Torah, which is still ahead of the reader.

By @HashemHelpMe

Quite a few people reached out, reporting that Motzai Rosh HaShana, Tzom Gedalya, and Motzai Yom Kippur were very challenging times, and some chevra unfortunately fell. The understandable shocked reaction of ”How could this happen right after the Yomim Noraim?!” demoralizes many otherwise successful guys. This ”phenomena” has been written about on the forums in the past. Chazara is good, especially knowing that soon there will be a Motzai Simchas Torah.

Contrary to what most people consider as fact, the vast majority of guys who struggle with pornography and masturbation do not have a ”kedusha problem”.They have a self-regulation problem. From an early age they trained themselves to use sexual release as a way of relieving any and all emotional discomfort/pain. Therefore, it is extremely understandable that following intense emotional days, one has stronger urges to act out - to seek relief/release from all the tension. Long days of serious davening, introspection and focusing on what we need to fix, coupled with physical exhaustion and change of daily schedule, create the ”perfect” recipe to desperately need some sort of ”escape” to self-regulate. Fasting also adds to the discomfort and distress that a normally balanced fellow is trying to cope with.

Knowing this in advance is helpful for a few reasons.

First and foremost, to remove the panic and shock of ”Why am I getting these urges after spending so much time serving Hashem so beautifully on these special days?” That panic just exacerbates the situation even more. We all know that when our muscles tighten up, a specific ”muscle” was used in the past to release all that tension.... So simply understanding that what one is experiencing is normal and expected, has a calming effect.

Secondly,if you know you will need to de-stress, plan in advance how to do that in a kosher healthy manne r, instead of resorting to unhealthy and forbidden actions.

Lastly, knowledge of this relationship between intensity and urges, will hopefully help you realize when you are ”overdoing it”, and help you stop ”overheating” with stress in the first place.

Anyone who is reading this obviously is working on himself, and therefore must be proud, and must know that Hashem has immense hana'ah from his actions, even if they are imperfect.

May we all be zoche to a gmar chasima tova and a meaningful Yom Tov!

B · Our Past Sins Become Our Schach  × ruled out The Alter Sadigerer Rebbe, son-in-law of the Beis Ahron of Karlin · 178 words · Same issue, Oct 5 2025. No author stated; it is a GYE write-up of the Rebbe’s vort.

Out. #848, same reason as A.

The best thematic match to the esrog piece on the page. Refael says the thing you fall to is not who you are; this says the fallen thing itself becomes the merit. Two pieces, one idea, and it is the only candidate that is actually about Sukkos.

Chaza”l say that the Schach should be from ”Pesoles Goren Ve'Yekev”. Why do Chazal use the Lashon of ”Pesoles”? Couldn't they have found a nicer way to describe the material we use for this holy Mitzvah?

Says the Alter Sadigerer Rebbe(son-in-law of the Beis Ahron of Karlin), that Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur are times of Teshuvah Me'Yirah-Repentance from Fear, and Chazal say that when we repent from fear our sins become like ”mistakes”(zedonos na'asin ki'shgagos). However, Sukkos is a time of Teshuvah Me'Ahavah-Repentance through love and rejoicing. This is a much higher level of Teshuvah, and Chazal say that the sins become like merits!

This, says the Sadigerer, is the secret of the Schach. We davka take the ”Pesoles”—the spoiled, the sins of our past—and through Teshuvah Me'Ahavah on Sukkos, we uplift them into merits. Hashem's divine presence hovers over us in the Sukkah davka in the form of our past sins, which we have merited to uplift through our repentance with love!

F · The aveirah you already know you are going to do  ← my pick guywithalife, same post as C, his first section · 347 words · Supplied by Chayim, #818, Sep 23. Never used. Posted Sep 22 this year.

You are right and I picked the wrong half. This one has the point. It names a thing most readers have felt and never heard said, and it sits next to your forum column without repeating it. That column asks whether you can promise never again. This one asks what to do when you already know the promise is not true.

A machshava I had over Yom Kippur was one I have experienced many times over the span of many Yom Kippurs.

So here I am, sitting nicely by my seat, walking the walk, talking the talk. (In other words, davening.)

So I'm trying to get things under control, and I start by telling Hashem how I'll be better next year.

I'll daven more, be a better person, no porn, a better husband, won't talk lashon hara. No more lying, better sedorim, more respectful of talmidei chachamim, I'll have hakaras hatov. More bitachon, and the list goes on and on.

As I am putting my upcoming year in order, a particular aveirah which I know I will have the ability to do creeps into my mind. And as I try to commit to being good, this thought of this aveirah moves- in what feels like a permanent move- to the front of my mind. It sits there taunting me, like ”Lol! You're saying you doing tshuvah, but you KNOW you're gonna be rocking this aveirah later, don't you!”.

It ruined everything, at first. I could not think straight, couldn't progress with any of the moves I'd planned on showing to Hashem, just because I felt like such a fake.

This happens to me every single freaking year. Obviously, it pisses me off at the very least, as it hinders any real tshuva I try to put forth. I feel like a faker with it, and like it all means nothing.

This year, however, I answered back.

After Arbit, as I walked home, I thought simply: What if I commit to do everything well this year aside from this aveirah? This way, I won't feel like a liar, and at the same time, I'm really committing to have the best year possible barring the one inevitable. (NOTE: I wasn't saying I was gonna do that aveirah, but rather that I am not making any sort of promise on that, period.)

Doing this definitely made me feel better AND pray better, and it felt like a good strategy for me.
