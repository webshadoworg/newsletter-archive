Part 1 · Shoftim · August 15, 2026

Focusing on the Positive During Elul

It’s easy to greet Elul with the familiar assignment: go through everything you’re missing and get to work on it. But as many of us know, that negative approach can backfire, and leave us feeling even more stuck.

This week, we’re bringing a refreshing approach to the month of Elul: four ideas from the Lakewood Mashgiach, Rav Nosson Meir Wachtfogel zt’l.

1. Add, don’t subtract. Elul, the way most of us were taught it, is the month you go to war with what’s wrong with you. The Mashgiach says it’s the opposite: “עיקר העבודה באלול צריך להיות בעשה טוב דוקא, ולא בסור מרע!” (the main avoda of Elul has to be in aseh tov specifically, not in sur me’ra). These are the days of רצון ורחמים, he explained: נפתח המוח ומתרחב הלב (the mind opens, the heart widens). This is the one month a year a person can carry more than he usually can. So you don’t spend it tearing yourself down. You spend it building.

2. Let it be sweet. He put one condition on the building, and it’s the hinge the whole idea turns on: “מתיקות בלימוד, אחרת האדם נהפך למתאוננים! ומחפש הנאות במקום אחר חלילה” (there has to be sweetness in the learning). Without it, a person turns into a complainer, and goes looking for pleasure somewhere else, chas v’shalom.

[Rav Michel Yehuda Lefkowitz zt’l heard this in a single word of the Gemara. Hashem created the yetzer hara, and created the Torah as its תבלין: its spice. Not a רפואה. Not a cure. A cure works whether you enjoy taking it or not. A spice only works if you can taste it. Torah without sweetness, he said, does nothing to protect a person from the yetzer hara. And Chazal’s advice for finding that sweetness: learn what your heart wants, where your heart wants to learn it.]

3. You are not what brought you low. What we’re actually up against isn’t appetite. It’s humiliation. “עיקר כח היצר להשפיל את האדם, ולשכח ממנו את מעלתו שהוא בן מלך” (the yetzer’s whole strategy is to bring a person low, low enough that he forgets he’s a בן מלך, a son of the King). That forgetting is what opens the door to things a בן מלך would never touch. The Mashgiach points to Yosef HaTzaddik at the single hardest moment of his life, and his answer: “לי יש שייכות לאבות” (I belong to the Avos; what could I possibly have to do with this). Then the line that makes the whole idea land: “וכשנזכר ומרגיש שהוא בן מלך, ממילא מתנהג אחרת!” (the moment a person remembers and feels that he’s a בן מלך, he behaves differently). No willpower required. That, said the Mashgiach, is the avoda of אני לדודי ודודי לי.

4. There’s no map, only sweat. When people asked him outright for the derech of Elul, he told them he had none to give: “אין דרך מסוימת ברורה לכולם, אלא כל אחד צריך לעמול” (there’s no one clear path for everyone; every person has to work out his own). He held onto one promise from his own rebbeim instead: whoever labors, exerts himself, and doesn’t quit, gets somewhere in the end. Nobody arrives on yichus. A person’s madreiga is measured by his toil, not his lineage. He’d say it in Yiddish: א טראפען שוייס ווערט ניט פארלאָרן. A drop of sweat is never lost.

Here’s why these four ideas aren’t background for a GYE member. They’re the whole fight, seen from the inside.

Add, don’t subtract is close to everything we already teach. Filling your day, on purpose, with productive and positive things: that’s the green circle. It isn’t a nice extra next to the real work. It’s one of the strongest defenses there is. A day that has structure in it, and something good in the gaps, is a day that’s much harder to lose. And because there’s no single derech for everyone, the circle is yours to design. For one man that means mornings: up on time, a seder that starts when it says it starts. For another it means the night, where the trouble usually lives, and what he needs is a fixed routine and a phone that’s somewhere else entirely. In yeshiva it’s mostly the sedarim. Outside, it’s a set learning time, work you’re actually moving forward in, exercise, a hobby that isn’t a compromise, it’s a shield. A man with good things filling his life is a much harder man to knock down.

You are not what brought you low is the one worth underlining twice. Anyone who’s been in this fight for a while knows exactly how far down it can take him, and knows that most of the falling happens after he’s already decided he’s finished. The Mashgiach is telling you that the shame isn’t the aftermath of the fall. The shame is the weapon that caused it. Which means remembering who you are isn’t comfort you allow yourself once things are already fine. It’s the avoda itself, in real time, mid-fight.

And it helps to know where the King is standing this month. All year He’s in the palace: getting in takes permission, protocol, a reason to be there. In Elul, in the Baal HaTanya’s mashal, the King is out in the field. Among ordinary people, on their ground, in his work clothes, no appointment needed, and He receives every single one of them with a smile. Nobody has to get cleaned up first to come over. אני לדודי ודודי לי isn’t a slogan for the season, it’s a description of exactly where you’re standing right now, in the month the King came out to the field to be with you.

There’s no map, only sweat is for the week that feels like it went nowhere. A man who’s fallen many times can be completely convinced that all the effort added up to nothing, and there’s usually no result on the day itself to prove him wrong. Hold onto the sweat anyway. It was never lost. The toil is the madreiga.

ONE AREA TO BUILD THIS WEEK

Pick one area of your life to build. One part of the day that could use your attention and a bit of planning. Your learning. Your mornings. The night hours. Your davening.

Choose the one that you think will be geshmak to improve, and plan what you want to improve during this Elul, and then choose the next steps.

Part 2 · Ki Seitzei · August 22, 2026

I’m tired of feeling guilty, how do I do Teshuva?

For many of us, the most unsettling part of Elul and the Yomim Noraim is a question we cannot answer cleanly. How do we do teshuva for a behavior that is ingrained, that we feel stuck with? We may be making real progress. But we cannot say when we will stop slipping, or when we will finally be free of it.

For most things, teshuva is straightforward: you feel remorse over what happened, you tell Hashem what you did and that you are sorry, and you commit not to do it again. But as we all know, when a behavior is ingrained and out of control, and we are not sure how to get out of it, guilt does not make it easier to stop. It makes it harder.

In a fascinating piece in Shaarei Teshuva (1:11), Rabbeinu Yonah gives us the answer. He says that when you’re doing teshuva for ingrained behaviors that happen frequently, wait with the guilt, and focus first on what you practically need to do to change:

אך האיש המתיצב על דרך לא טובה תמיד, וגבר על חטאיו דורך בכל יום, ושונה באולתו ושב במרוצתו גם פעמים רבות, וכל עת אוהב הרע, ומכשול עונו ישים נוכח פניו, רוצה לומר התאוה והיצר, וחפצו ומגמתו, אשר לא יבצר ממנו כל אשר יזם לעשות - ראשית תשובת האיש הזה, לעזוב דרכו ומחשבתו הרעה, ולהסכים לקיים ולקבל עליו לבל יוסיף לחטוא, אחרי כן יתחרט על עלילותיו הנשחתות, ויתודה לשוב אל ה׳

In other words, it’s premature to focus on remorse for this behavior before you’ve succeeded in stopping. The focus should not be on the emotional part of teshuva, but on the practical. Yes, it might take time, change doesn’t happen overnight, but if you’re actively working on stopping, experimenting to figure the right strategy for you, and learning from mistakes and improving your plan, then you’re already doing everything you need to be doing for teshuva right now.

And notice where Rabbeinu Yonah takes it next, in the same breath: “אחרי כן יתחרט... ויתודה” - afterwards he does regret it, and afterwards he confesses. Nothing is being written off. The order is being changed. Those parts of teshuva are real, they are coming, and they should be done. They get built on top, once the ground underneath is holding. Why leaning too hard on the past tends to backfire on this particular struggle is worth its own article, and we will come back to it later in this series.

One thing worth being blunt about, because it is where this gets real. Working on stopping is not something that happens in your head. It is not deciding again, more sincerely this time, and it is not feeling worse about it in shul on Rosh Hashanah. It is what you do differently on an ordinary Tuesday night. Which hour of the day the trouble lives in, and what is different about that hour. Which device, in which room, with what on it. Who you call before instead of after. What goes into the gap when the boredom shows up.

So the line that matters is not between a man who has stopped and a man who has not. It is between a man who is actively working on this, trying things, adjusting, learning what works for him, and a man who has quit working on it, who is coasting, or resigned, or waiting to become a different person.

Which leaves two people reading this. If you are actively working on this in reality, then you are already doing it, and Elul is for sharpening what you have. If you have not been doing anything about it, this is the month to start. Not with a resolution about the rest of your life. With one thing you are going to actually do this week.

HOMEWORK

We all go through different periods with this. Ups and downs. Stretches where you are focused on it, and stretches where you drift, or give up, or just stop putting in the effort.

For an ingrained, addictive behavior, teshuva is putting in the effort. Working on yourself. Working on the next step, whatever yours is right now. You cannot guarantee the result, but if you are actively working to change your mindset and your behavior in order to free yourself and ease the grip this has on you, that is teshuva.

So the homework is one question: what is your next step, and when this week are you doing it?

Part 3 · Ki Savo · August 29, 2026

How Teshuva Can Backfire

Last week in this series we quoted Rabbeinu Yonah that when doing Teshuva for addictive behaviors, the first step should be how to break free. Charata (remorse) over the past can wait until we’ve reached stability with avoiding the old patterns.

Now suppose you’re already clean for 3-6 months? Is it time now to focus on the past? And if yes, how should it be done?

In this week’s newsletter we bring you some surprising guidance on this topic from Gedolei Yisrael.

1. Thinking about the past should be done on rare occasions

Rav Nosson Meir Wachtfogel zt’l, the Lakewood Mashgiach, quoting R’ Yeruchom Levovitz zt’l (Leket Reshimos, Elul):

כל השנה אפילו שהאדם נכשל צריך לחשוב הלאה על העתיד, ולהתקדם ולא לחשוב על העבר, רק יום אחד בשנה מותר לאדם לחשוב על העבר - ביום הכפורים... וביום זה מזכירים בוידויים כ”כ הרבה נפילות עם פרטים פרטים, ואלמלי קדושת היום ששומרת על האדם היה נהפך למושחת. וגם זה מותר רק מחמת החידוש של תשובה, אבל כל השנה המהלך הוא להמשיך הלאה ולהתקדם, ולא להתעסק עם העבר, וזה חולשה של בני אדם לחיות עם העבר.

All year long, even when a person has fallen, he has to think forward, about the future, and keep moving ahead, and not think about the past. Only one day a year is a person permitted to think about the past, and that is Yom Kippur... And on that day we list in the vidui so many falls, in detail after detail, and were it not for the holiness of the day protecting him, a person would be harmed by it. Even that is only permitted because of the special power of teshuva. But all year long, the way is to keep going forward and to keep moving ahead, and not to occupy yourself with the past. Living with the past is a human weakness.

The Baal HaTanya (Tanya Ch 26) teaches that feeling bitter about the past needs to be confined to scheduled slots of time, when you have a clear head to do proper Teshuva. Because if the feelings of bitterness come up at random times, like during learning Torah, davening or even neutral times like work – it will only drag you down, and serve the Yetzer Hara’s agenda. Even the scheduled slots should be focused: You sit down, feel remorse as best you can, and then have full Bitachon that Hashem has forgiven you, and there’s nothing left to be bitter about.

2. When it comes to Kedusha-related behaviors, there are even more restrictions

The Steipler zt’l wrote:

מכתבך קבלתי והנני משתתף בצערך ומקוה שהשי”ת יעזרך במשך הזמן להנצל מחטאות נעורים ר”ל. במה שנוגע על העבר אין לחשוב לעת עתה כלל, ועיקר העיקרים הוא לעסוק בתורה... עוד עיקר גדול שלא להיות עצב ולא לחשוב אודות העבר כלל עד אחר נשואין שאז יש ליתן לב לתקן העבר.

I received your letter, and I share in your pain, and I hope that Hashem will help you over time to be saved from the sins of youth. As for what concerns the past, there is no need to think about it at all for now, and the most essential thing of all is to be occupied with Torah... Another great fundamental: not to be sad, and not to think about the past at all until after marriage, at which point it is worth turning your attention to setting the past right.

Why wait so long? Because for a bochur, thinking about what we did in the past, even in the context of Teshuva, has a risk of getting us stimulated and triggered, which then puts us at risk of relapsing!

Similarly, the Lubavitcher Rebbe zt’l wrote in two separate letters:

...ובהנוגע לתיקון חטא הידוע, הנה צריך להרבות באותיות התורה ובאותיות התפלה... ולהסיח דעתו מהנ”ל, אפילו מתיקונו, עד לאחר הנשואין בשעה טובה ומוצלחת.

...And with regard to the tikkun for the known sin: he should increase in Torah and tefillah... and take his mind off the above, even off its tikkun, until after his marriage, in a good and successful hour.

יסיח דעתו מענין זה לע”ע, היינו לא רק מהענין בעצמו אלא גם מהתבוננות בגודל החטא ובנחיצות התיקון. ויסיח דעתו מכ”ז לגמרי. ויתקע דעתו בחוזק בלימוד ובתפלה. מובן שכל הנ”ל צ”ל בשמחה (אף שמבואר בספרים באופן אחר, הנה דרך זה יניח כעבור איזה שנים, ועתה יסיח דעתו מכל הענין ויתחזק לעבוד את ה’ מתוך שמחה דוקא).

He should take his mind off this matter for now. That means not only off the matter itself, but also off contemplating the gravity of the sin and the urgency of putting it right. He should take his mind off all of it completely, and fix his mind firmly on learning and on tefillah. It goes without saying that all of the above has to be done with simcha. (Even though the sefarim explain it differently, he will set this approach aside after some years; for now he should take his mind off the whole matter and strengthen himself to serve Hashem specifically out of joy.)

Igros Kodesh vol. 8 pp. 284-5 and vol. 4 p. 364.


Bottom line: Focusing on the past needs to wait until you’re sure you can do it without getting triggered by it, and even then, it should only be done on Yom Kippur or other special occasions. Teshuva itself is covered by working on breaking free, and doing other positive actions, especially an increase in Torah learning.

(Just to be clear, working on stopping does require thinking about recent slips so that you can learn lessons from them. But that type of thinking about the past is more technical. It’s about the circumstances that led to the fall, and a down to earth problem-solving approach: what time did I slip? What device was it? What triggered it? What mistakes led to it? How can I improve my plan? The focus is not on evoking deep remorse, and not on replaying exactly what the slip entailed.)

Part 4 · Nitzavim-Vayeilech · September 5, 2026

Who Told You There Is No Tikkun?

This week the series continues with comforting words from a number of sources: any spiritual damage caused by the addictive behaviors that so many of us struggle with can be fully corrected.

One note before we start. As we have said, the subject of tikkunim, and actually doing them, is generally only recommended once you feel the behavior is no longer out of control. Until then the focus is on stopping, and on planning how to stop. So we are not getting into specific tikkunim here, only into the principle that a tikkun is possible and that it is doable, which is worth knowing for whenever the time comes.

1. The Shelah: there is nothing that teshuva does not repair

Start with the earliest source on it. The Shelah HaKadosh, close to four hundred years ago, opens by saying that he is writing to save the man who reads that passage and takes from it that there is no teshuva:

מתחילה אבוא להציל ממיתה נצחיית את הקורא המאמר הזה, שאין תשובה למי שחטא חטא זה, ואז החוטא יתייאש ויאמר כאשר אבדתי אבדתי, ויעשה משאלות לבו ויאמר נואש. אלא באתי לעורר החוטא שיקום בזריזות לעורר תשובה, ואל יתמהמה רגע אחד, כי אין דברי המאמר הזה כפשוטו שלא יועיל תשובה, כי אין דבר בעולם שלא יתוקן בתשובה, אפילו מי שכפר בעיקר.

First I come to save from eternal death the man who reads this passage and takes from it that there is no teshuva for one who sinned this sin, and then the sinner despairs and says, I am lost anyway, and does whatever his heart wants and gives up hope. Rather, I have come to rouse the sinner to get up quickly and awaken teshuva, and not to delay a single moment. Because the words of that passage cannot be meant the way they sound, that teshuva will not help. For there is nothing in the world that is not repaired by teshuva, even one who denied the ikar.

The Midrash Talpiyos, R’ Eliyahu HaKohen, the baal Shevet Mussar, says it harder. On the pious men who are machmir to go around saying there is no tikkun for this, he rules that they are not chassidim, pious ones, but chaseirim, ones who are lacking, and that their punishment is heavier than the punishment of the people they are talking about.

אלו החסידים המחמירים לומר אין תיקון לזרע לבטלה, כאשר שמעתי מפיהם - הם המרבים פושעים בישראל, ומרחקים לבני אדם מתחת כנפי השכינה. שכל מי שיש בו עון זה ושומע מפיהם שאין לו תקנה, הולך ועושה מה שלבו חפץ, באומרו וכאשר אבדתי אבדתי. ואלו הגורמים כך - אינן חסידים כי אם חסרים, וענשם כבד משלהם.

These pious men who are strict to say there is no tikkun for zera levatala, as I have heard from their own mouths - they are the ones who multiply sinners in Israel, and they push people away from under the wings of the Shechina. Because anyone who has this sin and hears from them that he has no remedy goes and does whatever his heart desires, saying, I am lost anyway. And those who cause this are not pious ones, they are ones who are lacking, and their punishment is heavier than theirs.

Shelah HaKadosh, Shaar HaOsiyos, Kedushas HaZivug (Hilchos Biah, os 24). Midrash Talpiyos, anaf zera levatala, os 7. Both are quoted in the commentary printed in most editions of the Keser Rosh, where a typo runs them together as one source.


2. The Steipler: the seforim told you half of it

The Steipler is answering a man who had read in the seforim that this aveira seizes his zechuyos. He tells him first that nothing of the mitzvos themselves is taken away from him, not at all and not in the slightest. What gets seized is the flow of help those mitzvos generate, and only for a time. Then he says what the seforim left out:

הספרים הקדושים מתוך כוונה לעצור בעד החוטא כתבו בביאור עצום איך שע”י זה נחטפים זכויותיו, וקיצרו לבאר צד השני. דהיינו, אע”פ שכמה פעמים נכשל, מ”מ אם לעומת זה הרבה פעמים מנצח ומתגבר על התאווה, אז באותו פעם שמתגבר על התאווה הבוערת בו באופן נורא, הרי הוא ממשיך אור הקדושה על עצמו ועל העולמות באופן קדוש מאוד מאוד, וחלק גדול מאוד מהניצוצות הקדושות הרי הוא מוציאם ומחזירם לקדושה, ואין לשער גודל רוממות קדושת זה האדם הכובש את תאוותו בזמן תוקפו, והוא אז בבחינת יוסף הצדיק. והכלל, אדם שיש לו מלחמת היצר ופעמים מנצח פעמים נכשל, הרי כמו שהחטא נורא ואיום, לעומתו זכותו הוא נורא ואיום מאד, ויזכה עי”ז לתקן בחייו את מה שפגם ולהחזיר לצד הקדושה כל הניצוצות הקדושות שנעשקו ע”י עוונותיו.

The holy seforim, meaning to stop a man from sinning, wrote in enormous detail about how his zechuyos are seized through this, and they were brief in explaining the other side. Namely: even though he has stumbled many times, nevertheless, if against that he wins many times and overcomes the taava, then at that moment when he overcomes a taava burning in him in a terrible way, he draws a light of kedusha onto himself and onto the worlds in a very, very holy way, and a very large part of the holy sparks he takes back out and returns to kedusha. There is no measuring how high the kedusha is of this man who conquers his taava at the hour of its full force; he is then in the aspect of Yosef HaTzaddik. And the rule is this: a man who has the war of the yetzer, and sometimes wins and sometimes falls, just as the sin is awesome and terrible, correspondingly his zechus is awesome and exceedingly terrible, and through this he will merit to repair in his lifetime what he damaged, and to return to the side of kedusha all the holy sparks that were taken away by his aveiros.

Kereina D’igrasa vol. 1, 105-106.


3. Two examples of a tikkun

To conclude, two examples of a specific tikkun, from two different directions. The first, in the words of the Baal HaTanya:

אך החיות שבטפות זרע שיצאו ממנו לבטלה, אף שירדה ונכללה בשלש קליפות הטמאות - הרי זו עולה משם בתשובה נכונה, ובכוונה עצומה בקריאת שמע שעל המטה, כנודע מהאר”י ז”ל, ומרומז בגמרא: כל הקורא קריאת שמע על מטתו כאלו אוחז חרב של שתי פיות, להרוג גופות החיצונים שנעשו לבוש לחיות שבטפות, ועולה החיות מהם.

But the energy in what went out from him levatala, even though it went down and was absorbed into the three impure klipos, that energy comes back up out of there - through proper teshuva, and through כוונה עצומה, intense kavana, in the Krias Shema said at bedtime. This is known from the Arizal, and it is hinted at in the Gemara: whoever says Krias Shema on his bed is like a man holding a double-edged sword, to kill the outer bodies that became a garment for that energy, and the energy rises up out of them.

The second runs in a different direction: Torah. The Steipler comes back to it again and again in his letters, and this is from the same letter quoted above:

עיקר התיקון לחטא זה הוא התורה ובפרט יגיעת התורה היינו כשלומד גם בשעה שאין לו תענוג מהלימוד ולומד רק משום שכן צוה השי”ת כגון שחוזר הסוגייא הרבה פעמים או שמייגע מוחו להבין היטב ד”ת. הכלל מי שמייגע בתורה לש”ש ודאי שסוף סוף יזכה לתקן את אשר קלקל.

The main tikkun for this sin is Torah, and in particular יגיעת התורה, toiling in it. That means learning even at a time when he takes no pleasure in the learning, and learning only because that is what Hashem commanded - reviewing the sugya many times over, or wearing out his mind to understand a matter of Torah properly. The rule is: whoever toils in Torah lishma will certainly, in the end, merit to repair what he damaged.

R’ Aharon Leib Shteinman zt”l put it simply:

מה שנוגע על העבר ודאי אתה מתחרט, ועיין בכתר ראש (אות קלג) דמבואר שלימוד התורה מכפרת על העבר אם עושין תשובה בחרטה.

As for the past, of course you regret it - and see the Keser Rosh (os 133), where it is explained that learning Torah atones for the past, if a person does teshuva with regret.

(This is based on the Zohar as explained by the Vilna Gaon and cited by R’ Chaim of Volozhin in Kesser Rosh)

Tanya, Likutei Amarim ch. 7; the Gemara is Berachos 5a. Kereina D’igrasa vol. 1, 105-106. R’ Aharon Leib Steinman’s letter is printed at the front of Zos Brisi; R’ Shlomo Wolbe notes that the Chazon Ish showed him this same piece.
