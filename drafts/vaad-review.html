What’s missing · ranked

You asked. These are the questions a reader has to answer for himself before he can say yes, in the order they matter. The first three are the ones I would not send without.

  1. When it meets, and for how long

    Which day, what time, and how long a meeting runs. “Meets once a week” is the commitment being asked for, and it is the first thing anyone weighs before saying yes. Right now a reader has to sign up to find out what he is agreeing to.

  2. Phone, video, or in person

    Not stated anywhere. It also decides whether “fully anonymous” reads as true - video would quietly contradict it. If it is a phone line, saying so is itself the reassurance.

  3. How long the group runs

    Twelve weeks? A year? Open-ended? Nobody commits to something with no end in sight. A fixed run is much easier to say yes to, and people usually stay past it.

  4. A date, and a reply-by date

    “Right after Sukkos” and “right after Yom Tov” are the same thing said twice, and neither is a date. Same for “let us know soon”. If spots close, the date they close is what makes anyone act this week instead of next.

  5. How small is small

    “A small group” is carrying a lot of weight. A number - eight or ten men - is the reassurance that it will not be a crowd he has to perform in front of.

  6. Who each group is for

    Are groups split by age or situation - bochurim, married men, older - or is it one room for everyone? “Guys” implies men, but not who he would be sitting with.

  7. What happens after he clicks

    Does he get an email, a phone call, a form? One clause would remove the fear that pressing the button commits him on the spot.

  8. Which GYE material

    “Going through the GYE material together” is the one vague phrase in otherwise concrete copy. The Handbook? A specific programme? One or two words fixes it.

Decisions and open ends

Two brachos, back to back

Your copy closes “K’siva va’chasima tova to you and your family.” and you then said to sign it גמר חתימה טובה. I did both rather than delete a line of yours, so the email now carries both. My recommendation: drop the K’siva line and let גמר חתימה טובה be the close - we are past Rosh Hashanah, so K’siva va’chasima tova is last week’s bracha. Say the word and it goes.

It is signed by an organisation, but written by a person

The copy is first person singular throughout - “I wanted to let you know”, “I’d like to invite you” - and then signs off “Guard Your Eyes”. A name under it would make the whole letter land differently. Your [Your name] blank is still open unless the bracha was meant to fill it.

The sign-up button is login-walled

Unchanged from your v1 file and worth deciding before this sends. app.guardyoureyes.com/toolbox/tools/vaad returns 200 but is a single-page app - opened with no session it bounces to guardyoureyes.com/component/gyeuser/login. A reader who is not already signed in hits a sign-in screen before he sees one word about the Vaad. The return= does round-trip, so he gets there eventually if he perseveres.

Merge tag and platform

I rendered your [Name] as Mailchimp’s *|FNAME|*, because Mailchimp is what your test send proved for the High Holiday email. If this one goes out another way, tell me and I will swap the tag.

Preheader

There is none in the HTML, deliberately - Mailchimp deletes hidden preheader divs on import, which is what broke the opener. The preview line above is what Gmail will actually scrape from the first sentence. If you want to control it, it goes in the ESP’s Preview text field and I will write you a line.
