6 questions

Filled dot = what I’d pick. If you agree with all six, say “go with your picks” and I build it. Otherwise just give me the numbers you want changed.

Question 1 — logo

Small logo at the top plus an initial avatar, or the logo is the avatar?

  • Small centered logo + lavender circle with the agent’s initial — what page 1 shows
  • Logo becomes the avatar, no separate logo — Option E on page 3

Why: the person should be the hero. Every real notification email I looked at (LinkedIn is the closest match) keeps the logo small and lets the sender’s face and name dominate. Logo-as-avatar looks good but makes the email lead with an institution.

Question 2 — subject

Which subject line?

  • “{name} replied to your conversation” — 34 chars as Chaim, 37 as Yehoshua
  • “{name} just messaged you” — 23 chars, 26 with a long name

Why: see the lock-screen preview on page 1 — the first one clips for agents with longer names, the second fits every name. It also covers the case where an agent writes first rather than replying, so one line works for both. If sends ever get queued, drop “just”: {name} messaged you.

Question 3 — preheader  ·  the one I’d push on

Does the agent’s real first line go into the preview text in v1?

  • Yes — first line of the reply, 90-char cap
  • No — fixed generic line for everyone in v1, real first line only once a member has actively chosen it

Why: the argument for showing the real line is “the member chose the detailed version.” But in v1 nobody has chosen anything — detailed is the default and opting out means knowing the option exists and asking staff. So everyone gets the agent’s raw words on their lock screen by default. The ~250-char snippet inside the email is fine either way, because reading it means opening the email. This is one conditional, not a redesign.

Question 4 — from name

Pick one and freeze it.

  • GYE Support — 11 chars
  • GYE Team — 8 chars
  • GYE Personal Support — 20 chars

Why: you were right to flag the third one — “Personal Support” reads like a helpline to anyone glancing at an inbox list. I’d also change the role line in the body to match, so the email says “GYE Support team” under Chaim’s name.

Question 5 — avatars

Will staff ever have real profile photos?

  • Yes, soon
  • Not soon / probably never

Why it matters: it decides question 1 permanently. Real photos make the person-forward layout much stronger and rule out logo-as-avatar. No photos means the avatar is a letter forever, which is the weakest version of the person-as-hero idea and the main argument for Option E.

Question 6 — not a design question

Who watches help@gye.org, and how often?

  • Needs a name

Why: the email tells people to use the button, but some will hit reply anyway and that reply lands in a real inbox. Someone has to read it and move it into the support system by hand. If nobody does, a member writes something vulnerable and gets silence — worse than a bounce. Volume should be low, but this is the one manual dependency in the whole design.

Already decided, not asking again

  • Send from help@gye.org, no no-reply@ anywhere visible
  • Replies land in help@gye.org, get read, and are hand-carried into the support system. Never bounced. The email nudges people to the button but never claims replies go unseen
  • Links point at app.gye.org
  • bounces@mail.gye.org in the Return-Path
  • No new sending subdomain — gye.org has reputation already, warm-up from zero at launch is the worst timing
  • Snippet cut at the last sentence end before 250 chars
  • No rating widget. Asking someone to rate a supportive message 1-3-5 is jarring
