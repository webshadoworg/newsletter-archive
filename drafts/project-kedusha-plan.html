The Plan

You said this needs some planning. Here it is on one page: what the article argues, who says what, what is still missing, and the four decisions only you can make. Written Aug 28, 2026.

The argument, in five moves

The whole piece now runs on one idea, yours: proper protection feels like too much of a tircha, so it does not happen.

1. The story. A fifteen-year-old, sobbing. His father’s office. No filter on the computer. The father never decided that was acceptable; he never decided anything about it at all.
2. The condition. Everyone knows they should have better protection. By the third question it stops being a decision and becomes a project, and a project needs an evening nobody has.
3. Why it is genuinely hard. The answers keep changing. Filters expire quietly, kehilla standards move, AI changed the question in a season.
4. The admission. GYE is the foremost address for a Yid already struggling. GYE was never the expert in protecting a family that is not struggling. That is a research problem, and research is Mishor’s expertise. Hence the partnership.
5. The close. Find out in ten minutes instead of losing a night. Information should not be the barrier standing between a Yid and the solution he needs.

Every section serves one of those five. If a paragraph does not, it comes out.

Who says what

Four quotes woven into the prose, not four portrait sections. That is the change you asked for. Every one is my wording, in yellow, waiting for the real sentence.

  • You open the article, with the fifteen-year-old and the office. This is the one quote the piece cannot run without.
  • R Lazerson (Mishor) carries the technology: why a printed page is stale by the time it is bound.
  • Yaakov Nadel carries the turn: GYE was never only for people who fell. He also gets the nineteen years around him in prose.
  • Chaim (hotline) carries the evidence: the calls are not what people assume, and half of them are about what to buy.

That is four voices in about 1,480 words, which is roughly the density of both Mishpacha spreads we have on file. A fifth would start to crowd it.

3. The title. The Tircha of Doing the Right Thing is a placeholder that names the problem. Options if you want Project Kedusha in it: Project Kedusha: The Answers Keep Changing, or Everyone Knows. Nobody Knows Where to Start.
