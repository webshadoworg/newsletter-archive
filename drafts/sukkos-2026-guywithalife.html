Section 1 · 347 words · Run it

The Yom Kippur machshava

This is the piece. He names a thing most people feel on Yom Kippur and never say: the one aveirah that sits at the front of your mind while you are promising to be better, and makes the whole vidui feel like a lie. Then he does something about it rather than being consoled about it. It is specific, it is his own, and it is the right week for it.

A machshava I had over Yom Kippur was one I have experienced many times over the span of many Yom Kippurs.

So here I am, sitting nicely by my seat, walking the walk, talking the talk. (In other words, davening.)

So I'm trying to get things under control, and I start by telling Hashem how I'll be better next year.

I'll daven more, be a better person, no porn, a better husband, won't talk lashon hara. No more lying, better sedorim, more respectful of talmidei chachamim, I'll have hakaras hatov. More bitachon, and the list goes on and on.

As I am putting my upcoming year in order, a particular aveirah which I know I will have the ability to do creeps into my mind. And as I try to commit to being good, this thought of this aveirah moves- in what feels like a permanent move- to the front of my mind. It sits there taunting me, like "Lol! You're saying you doing tshuvah, but you KNOW you're gonna be rocking this aveirah later, don't you!".

It ruined everything, at first. I could not think straight, couldn't progress with any of the moves I'd planned on showing to Hashem, just because I felt like such a fake.

This happens to me every single freaking year. Obviously, it pisses me off at the very least, as it hinders any real tshuva I try to put forth. I feel like a faker with it, and like it all means nothing.

This year, however, I answered back.

After Arbit, as I walked home, I thought simply: What if I commit to do everything well this year aside from this aveirah? This way, I won't feel like a liar, and at the same time, I'm really committing to have the best year possible barring the one inevitable. (NOTE: I wasn't saying I was gonna do that aveirah, but rather that I am not making any sort of promise on that, period.)

Doing this definitely made me feel better AND pray better, and it felt like a good strategy for me.

Section 2 · 177 words · Your call

The sports bar

The best writing on the page and the riskiest setting. A frum reader watching himself be recognised by a stranger as one of his own kind is a shame nobody in our archive has described, and he is honest that it surprised him. Against that: it is set in a sports bar, the glance is described, and the man is sketched by his colouring. Editorial, not mine.

I was in a sports bar the other day with a friend who frequents it often.

All was well, we were having a nice time chatting with some regulars that I'd met before, watching sports, etc.

A middle aged, white as flip, probably of Irish descent, owns some sort of business type guy comes over to us. He's good friends with my pal, and I've met him before. As we're chatting about nothing and everything, a girl passes by. As I haven't been in control of myself for the most part recently, I sneak a glance at her. Not for long, but a glance nonetheless. The guy, sees me look, winks at me, and says, "Good Stuff, huh?". Gives me a smile, like, "you and me both".

I felt shame like I don't remember feeling ever. I regretted looking SO badly! To be in cahoots with a dude like that?

I have no point, only to note that I don't remember this sort of feeling with lust, an area which I usually think I've felt it all.
