My recommendation

1

The seven falls are the rising - not despite them

“שבע ייפול צדיק וקם” is read by almost everybody as a consolation: he falls, and nevertheless he gets up. Rav Hutner says that reading is the foolish one. The seven falls are the substance of what makes him a tzaddik. And the moment you feel the storm of the yetzer inside you is the moment you most resemble the gedolim, not the moment you least do.

בזמן שהנך מרגיש בקרבך סערת היצר, דע לך שבזה אתה מתדמה אל הגדולים הרבה יותר מאשר בשעה שאתה נמצא במנוחה השלמה שאתה רוצה בה... החכמים יודעים היטב שהכוונה היא שמהות הקימה של הצדיק היא ה’שבע נפילות’ שלו

Source Rav Hutner, Pachad Yitzchak - Igros u’Ksavim §128, p. 217 [V], on Mishlei 24:16. He opens the letter in English: “Lose a battle and win the war.” · Chiddushei HaRim - why B’nei Yisrael (שרית, you struggled) and not B’nei Tuchal (ותוכל, you prevailed): the Torah names us for the effort [S - cite as heard from rabbanim] · Rav Wolbe, Igros u’Ksavim II §282 - אל תתבייש בכח זה, don’t be ashamed of this koach; it is the same koach you will build a home with [V]

Where Its own part, and it is the natural closer for the series - it is the only idea here that ends on the reader being closer in than he thought, rather than on something to do. It also now sits right next to part 4, which ends on “a person can fall a thousand times and still be a tzaddik”: Rav Hutner is the mekor that explains why that is true rather than just asserting it.

2

Today you were born - the fresh start, and the fly at the door of the heart

Rabbeinu Yona raises the struggler’s own objection in his own words - how can I have the nerve to come back, I did it and repeated it countless times, I come before Him like a thief caught in shame - and his answer is that the thought itself is the maisis, sitting like a fly at the doorways of the heart and renewing the accusation every single day. Then: throw it all down and make yourself as if you were born today.

ביום ההוא ישליך כל פשעיו אשר עשה, ויעשה עצמו כאילו היום נולד, ואין בידו לא זכות ולא חובה, וזה היום תחילת מעשיו

Source Rabbeinu Yona, Yesod HaTeshuvah, opening [V] · Rav Mattisyahu Salomon, Matnas Chaim, Yamim Noraim p. 73 - Shaarei Teshuvah’s long detailed derech is for a man already on the path; one who went off it throws it away and starts fresh [V]

Where Purpose-built for the Rosh Hashana issue (Sept 12). It is the same author as part 2, so the series would be closing on the voice it opened its central idea with, and Rav Mattisyahu is a third framing of the two-track fork the unplaced row above opens on. One overlap to know about: Rabbeinu Yona’s “how can I have the nerve to come back” objection is close to what part 4 just answered. His answer is different - the thought is the maisis - so it does not repeat, but the opening lines would have to.

A

The lashon hara analogy - your own, and it never made it in

Your words, Aug 10: “Doesn’t mean that you’re never gonna do it ever. That’s the same question as lashon hara. ‘I’m never gonna speak lashon hara again’ - you can try your best. But if it’s happening frequently right now and you don’t even know what to do to stop, then the work to do is to figure it out and actively work on it.” It answers the reader’s core objection by showing he already lives with exactly this in a different mitzvah, without ever concluding he is outside teshuva - and it diagnoses somebody else, not him.

Source Yours. No mekor needed and none should be attached.

B

Toxic guilt, defined - and the diet mashal

The definition from GYE’s own article is quotable as it stands: “shame becomes toxic when you think your setbacks are caused by something you can’t control and something that won’t change.” That is a test the reader can run on himself in one sentence. The diet mashal that opens it is the same device as the lashon hara analogy in a warmer register.

Source GYE’s toxic-guilt article (you sent it Aug 10, full text in raw/gye-toxic-guilt-article.md). House material, so not a mekor - but for the definition we are quoting ourselves, which is fine. The mekoros underneath it: Rabbi Dr. Twerski on guilt vs shame, and Nesivos Shalom, Parshas Noach - guilty feelings as a gift, a tikkun for breaking free [V].

Where Was to open part 2. Never used - part 2 became yours.
