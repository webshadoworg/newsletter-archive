I am an esrog. And so are you.

By @RefaelEY


According to a well known midrash (Vayikra Rabbah 30:12-14), the four species hint at four kinds of people in Klal Yisrael. Naturally the first thing I ask myself is where I sit in that table. After so many years of falling it is fairly reasonable that I should feel a little like an aravah, no? True, I learn Torah and I do chassadim, but falling in something this serious, and this many times, makes me feel a little like a rasha. A sort of dry, beaten aravah.


But wait. The midrash goes on to say that the four species also hint at four central limbs of the body, limbs they resemble in their outward shape. So if the esrog hints at my heart, let us look inside for a moment: what do I really want in the depths of my heart? What would I do if there were a magic button here next to me that I could press and be free of these exhausting battles from now until forever?


What would I do if at that moment I were at my very best? At the full strength of body and soul, not tired, not under a pull, at my clearest, in the middle of an uplifted Neilah. Do I press it or not?... Obviously I press it! So there it is. That is my heart. That is my inner self. What I am struggling with must not be the thing that defines my “I”, my identity.


The aravah, on the other hand, hints at the mouth. It expresses my outward actions. What actually happens in practice, min hasafah ulachutz, from the lip outward. True, my actions still need fixing. And only Hashem and I know (and a few of my friends from GYE) how hard I keep trying to strike at this taavah and to beat this aravah. Again and again and again...


But me? I am an esrog! And you too, you are an esrog!


Esrog

The kind of person: Torah and good deeds (taste and smell)

The limb: Heart

What it means for us: Our true inner will. "וְעַמֵּךְ כּוּלָּם צַדִּיקִים", and your people are all tzaddikim.


Lulav

The kind of person: Torah learning only (taste without smell)

The limb: Spine

What it means for us: Standing upright spiritually. Staying on a path that matches our real values.


Hadas

The kind of person: Good deeds only (smell without taste)

The limb: Eyes

What it means for us: Our good deeds, and the positive way we look at others and at ourselves.


Aravah

The kind of person: Neither Torah nor good deeds (no taste and no smell)

The limb: Mouth (lips)

What it means for us: The outward actions that need fixing. What happens to us "min hasafah ulachutz". The place where we struggle.


Vayikra Rabbah 30:12-14

It was impossible to imagine being clean forever

By @vehkam

From almost never to never

@MECHAZAIK ASKED

Hey Chevra,

Maybe the oilam can help me here. Bh the journey has been going pretty good recently but how is it I see some guys are at 2, 3, even 4 years straight clean?! How do you guys do it?! I can go 3-4 months ok, but I feel the fall is always in my mind potentially around the corner.

What happens after 3-4 months is, and I’m still figuring myself out so I’m not sure... but I think I start to get complacent and feel like i’m pretty good and ok and i’m not so “addicted” or “hooked in” so “it wont be so bad” if i just fall once. i’ll tell myself I deserve it since its been so long... obviously thats all the yetzer hara and shtusim but i get to this place where i dont feel that initial “omg I must stop and run away from this stuff” feeling and before that happens i’m trying to figure out how to get into a “never again this is not who I am” mindset instead of “almost never but only once every few months”.... its almost as if i dont want to completely leave go. Have any thoughts how i can change this mindset from almost never to never?


@JacktheJew replied:


I think there may be an important nekudah to consider here:


Do YOU want to do it?


What happens with us a lot of the time is that we say to ourselves, “I don’t want to do it! I want to change! I want to never do this again!” This brings on a constant struggle. We see it as: The Yetzer Hara wants us to do something, we don’t want to do it, and we fight against it. It’s basically stretching a rubber band. Hence, what I understand to be your question:


How is it possible to just fight that off forever?


I think, that If we are able to step outside of ourselves and look a little more objectively at things, we would see something that is initially very scary to accept.


We do want to do these things. Our actions, the fact that we allow ourselves to wind up back here time after time, shows that we actually DO want it. We are not in a true state of pure wanting to do the Ratzon Hashem. It’s not just the Yetzer Hara that we are fighting. When we are triggered, WE want to do it. And, by the way, the more we even try bringing the Yetzer Hara into this equation, the more cover we are giving ourselves, taking away how much we want to do it deep down.


The truth, therefore, is that the only way to actually stop is to not wait for that blast of motivation, (Usually heard, seen, or experienced in pretty close proximity to a fall, BTW) that will change us over into people who want tahara.


Instead, we have to learn how to do the work of changing ourselves from something that we want.


If someone adamantly insists, “I hate unhealthy food! I don’t want it anymore! I just LOVE Celery!”


How long can he hold out for? His insistence that he doesn’t want junk will conflict with his body’s reality that he does, until he runs out of willpower.


He can only hope for success by admitting the truth to himself instead of (literally) sugarcoating it.


Even if he can recognize and admit that, if our friend the dieter spends his whole day fighting off thoughts of Oreos, Bloomeos, or whatever more Yeshivish equivalent there is to Oreoes without being the Paragon of Taanugei Olam Hazeh that those things are, he will not succeed either. The same problem mentioned before: There is a limited amount of willpower a person has to put in.


The solution, therefore, must be to not be dealing with these thoughts and problems for months until I snap.


That takes ideas and practice of how to healthily deal with urges.


@chosemyshem replied:


One very specific and practical eitzah. Get involved in something that keeps you motivated and going in a healthy way.


Helping others is particularly powerful.


@amevakesh replied:


Not taking anything away from what the Oilam is saying, but I have found that one of the most powerful way of going from almost never to never was reaching out to someone. Ideally someone that’s been clean for a significant amount of time, that has experience mentoring, and that cares. Almost all of the people that are clean for years (I only know of 1 exception), made that first phone call, and made it their business to stay in touch with them, until a real relationship developed. Hatzlacha!
