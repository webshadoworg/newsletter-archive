The message count — in, as of Jul 29

Mendy was right and I was wrong about this

My objection was “the count is permanently 1, because there is one email per message.” That was wrong, because it counted the wrong thing. The number is unread messages in the conversation, not messages in this send. Ignore four emails and the fifth honestly says five. It climbs on its own, with no batching and no extra architecture — and a rising number is real information: it means this person has stopped opening them.

Two things carried over from the objection, both cheap:

  • Hide it at 1. “1 message waiting” is weaker than saying nothing, and hiding it kills the singular/plural conditionals in one move. One condition instead of three.
  • Not “awaits your response.” Same number, same urgency, but a fact instead of a debt. These are often people not answering because it already feels like too much, and an email that opens by telling them what they owe is the one they leave unread. Final wording, set by Mendy Jul 30: “{count} new messages from {name}.”

And the person stays the headline, so the email still sounds like Chaim rather than a ticket queue. The count sits under his line as a small pill.

“Open conversation” solves the plural for free — it reads correctly at 1 unread or 5, so the button needs no conditional to match the pill.

Open on the wording: Chaim is named three times in the top four lines now, and “from Chaim” is wrong if the thread was handed to a second agent. Both noted on page 1.

The thing the count unlocks — worth revisiting later

One email per message, to somebody who is not opening them, is how a sender ends up in spam. Once a real count exists, GYE can send fewer emails instead of more: after two unopened, drop to one a day — “3 new messages from Chaim.” Same urgency, a third of the volume, better deliverability. Not v1, but the count is the piece that makes it possible.

Button label — settled on “Open conversation”

Mendy, Jul 30. The instinct to move off Read & reply was right. “Read & reply” asks for two things and one of them is work — for a member who is avoiding the conversation, that is a reason to close the email. A button that only promises to show you something is easier to press, and the reply follows once they are in the chat.

v1 currently shows “Read & reply.” Say the word and it becomes “View Message” — I did not want to add a seventh question over one label.

Brand colors — settled

Both tiles use one purple and one yellow, exact inversions of each other: #624b78 and #f9d772. #624b78 appears 70 times across GYE’s existing branded emails.

Rebuilt as PNG at 110×114 from one shared letterform mask, so they are sharp on retina at any size we use. A vector from the designer is still worth having before the tile ships larger than ~45px.
