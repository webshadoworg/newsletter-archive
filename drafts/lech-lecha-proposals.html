“And Hashem said to Avram, ‘Lech lecha, go for yourself, from your land, from your birthplace, and from your father’s house, to the place that I will show you.’” (Breishis 12:1) Why didn’t Hashem tell Avraham where he was going? He’s told to leave everything familiar and set out into the unknown, but not told the destination. Why keep it a mystery? Rashi, quoting Chazal (Bereishis Rabbah 39:9), explains that Hashem deliberately withheld the destination l’chabeiv aleha b’einav, to make the land precious in his eyes, and to reward him for each step of the journey. In other words, the not-knowing was the point. Hashem was rewarding Avraham for the walking itself, not only for the arriving. That’s the message folded into the command. The purpose of Avraham’s journey was never just to get from point A to point B on a map. If it were only about ending up somewhere, Hashem could have named the place. Lech lecha is literally “go to yourself.” The Sefas Emes and other mefarshim read it exactly this way: the journey Avraham was sent on, and the one planted in each of us, is a journey inward, a lifelong process of becoming. The destination is who you are growing into, which is why it can’t be marked on a map in advance. And if the whole of life is a journey of becoming, then falling along the way isn’t a detour off the path. It’s part of walking it. Shlomo HaMelech says, “Sheva yipol tzaddik v’kam”, “the tzaddik falls seven times and rises” (Mishlei 24:16). Notice what the pasuk is telling us: a tzaddik is not someone who never falls. The Vilna Gaon and others point out that the tzaddik is defined davka by the rising, he is a tzaddik because he gets up, again and again, not in spite of the falls but through them. The seven falls are inside the definition of the tzaddik, not a stain on it. This is the whole hashkafa of our avodah. The most important thing was never a spotless record. It was staying on the journey, honoring the process, and getting up every time. “It’s not how hard you fall, but how high you bounce.” Lech lecha. Keep going to yourself. The train is always arriving.

What is wrong with it

"not in spite of the falls but through them." That makes the fall the engine of the rise, and it is not a thing we can say. You or a rav. Separately: the Sefas Emes attribution does not hold, the text points the other way, and the Vilna Gaon line could not be found in any printed source. The midrash he cites is better than he claimed, it says step by step at our own posuk.

Lech Lecha!

A great deal is said about the greatness of Avraham Avinu's "lech lecha." Much less is said about the "lech lecha" that Yitzchak Avinu made. What does that mean? As is well known, Avraham's middah is chesed, while Yitzchak's middah is gevurah, its opposite, so that Yitzchak was forced to go and move a certain distance away from his father Avraham's style of avodas Hashem in order to lay down his own derech in avodas Hashem. (There is of course a significant difference between the two cases: Avraham moved away sharply and completely from the avodah zarah of his father Terach, while Yitzchak only turned to a different style of avodas Hashem.)

Rav Dessler asks why Yitzchak did not continue in his father's derech in avodas Hashem, and answers that "then his avodah would have been a mere repetition of his father's avodah." Hashem's hanhaga directed matters so that Yitzchak's middah would be entirely different, "so that each one of the holy Avos would bring down into the world a new aspect of avodas Hashem" (Michtav MeEliyahu vol. 2 p. 164).

This comes to expression in a particular way at the beginning of Parshas Toldos, where we are told of Yitzchak and Rivka davening to be granted children. On one hand Rashi writes (Bereishis 25:21) that Hashem answered Yitzchak's tefillah specifically and not Rivka's, because "the tefillah of a tzaddik the son of a tzaddik is not comparable to the tefillah of a tzaddik the son of a rasha." But on the other hand Rashi notes (there, posuk 20) that the reason the Torah mentions again that Rivka was the daughter of Besuel and the sister of Lavan is "to tell her praise, that she was the daughter of a rasha and the sister of a rasha and her place was a place of reshaim, and she did not learn from their deeds."

So which is better, a tzaddik the son of a tzaddik, or a tzaddik the son of a rasha?

Rav Dessler explains that each of them has his own maalah. A tzaddik the son of a rasha is worthy of praise for not having learned from his father's bad deeds and for having chosen a good path. But by a tzaddik the son of a tzaddik there is a different danger, that the whole thing will exist in him only as an external habit.

We all know how hard it is, for example, to put deep daily kavanah into the nusach of a tefillah we have been used to saying since childhood. When a tzaddik the son of a tzaddik works to turn these things from an external habit into an inner kinyan, "like a man who discovers it all afresh," he merits a great maalah (ibid. vol. 2 p. 205, vol. 3 p. 124).

And how does all of this connect to us?

Sometimes we too are asked to make a kind of "lech lecha." Many of us grew up in a home of shomrei Torah umitzvos and were raised on various yesodos in avodas Hashem. But if we want to be honest about our own personal avodas Hashem, and in particular about the struggle that brought us here, it is not always enough to act out of habit. Sometimes we need to stop for a moment and look inward, to check what will really bring us to the result we want. (Sometimes it is also worth taking advice from people with experience.)

If what we grew up on works, that is excellent. We will carry on using it with all our strength. But if not, perhaps we need to examine other possibilities. Perhaps Hashem's will from us is "lech lecha," to what suits the structure of your own nefesh, to tools and understandings with which we can really reach the Promised Land. When this is done properly, it is not, chas v'shalom, a throwing off of ol. On the contrary, it is a true kabbalas ol, in an inner way and out of real reflection, rather than out of external habit.

Lech lecha!

For further study:

The Shulchan Aruch paskens that mitzvos require kavanah (O.C. 60:4). And the Mishnah Berurah writes (there, s.k. 10) that where it is an act people are only ever accustomed to do for the sake of a mitzvah, such as taking a lulav, the act itself proves that he had intent to do a mitzvah, and b'dieved he has fulfilled his obligation, even if he did not explicitly have in mind to fulfil a mitzvah.

However, the words of the Gra"ch of Brisk are well known: that in order to fulfil the obligation of tefillah, a person must be aware that he is standing and davening before Hashem, because that is an inseparable part of the definition of the act of the mitzvah of tefillah. In the Gra"ch's sharp language: "If his heart is not clear, and he does not see himself as standing before Hashem and davening, this is not an act of tefillah, and he is in the category of one who is mis'asek... and it is as though he skipped those words" (Chiddushei Rabbeinu Chaim HaLevi, Tefillah 4:1).

If so, perhaps it was not for nothing that Yitzchak's maalah was specifically in the matter of tefillah for children. Yitzchak, whose middah is gevurah, a middah of drawing inward, works to reveal the inner dimension inside the external habit on which he was raised, and merits children who are the revelation of the father's inner self and the depths of his heart (Tzidkas HaTzaddik 63), and all of this through tefillah, whose entire essence is the inner avodah of the heart and not just outward mumbling.

לֶךְ לְךָ!

הרבה מדברים על מעלת ה'לֶךְ לְךָ' של אברהם אבינו. פחות מדובר על ה'לֶךְ לְךָ' שעשה יצחק אבינו. במה דברים אמורים? כידוע, מידתו של אברהם היא מידת החסד, ואילו מידתו של יצחק היא מידת הגבורה המנוגדת לה, כך שיצחק נאלץ ללכת ולהתרחק במידה מסוימת מסגנון עבודת ה' של אברהם אביו, כדי לסלול את דרכו שלו בעבודת ה' (כמובן, קיים הבדל משמעותי בין המקרים: אברהם התרחק בצורה חדה ומוחלטת מן העבודה זרה של תרח אביו, בעוד יצחק רק פנה לסגנון שונה של עבודת ה').

הרב דסלר שואל מדוע לא המשיך יצחק בדרך עבודת ה' של אביו, ומשיב ש"אז הייתה עבודתו חזרה בעלמא על עבודת אביו". הנהגת ה' כיוונה את הדברים שמידת יצחק תהיה שונה לגמרי, "כדי שכל אחד מן האבות הקדושים יוריד לעולם בחינה חדשה של עבודת ה'" (מכתב מאליהו ח"ב עמ' 164).

עניין זה בא לידי ביטוי מיוחד בתחילת פרשת תולדות, שם מסופר על יצחק ורבקה המתפללים להיפקד בבנים. מצד אחד כותב רש"י (בראשית כה כא), שה' נענה דווקא לתפילת יצחק ולא לתפילת רבקה, וזאת מפני "שאין דומה תפילת צדיק בן צדיק לתפילת צדיק בן רשע". אך מצד שני מציין רש"י (שם פסוק כ), שהטעם לכך שהתורה מזכירה שוב שרבקה היא בת בתואל ואחות לבן, הוא "להגיד שבחה, שהייתה בת רשע ואחות רשע ומקומה אנשי רשע - ולא למדה ממעשיהם".

אז אם כן, מי עדיף - צדיק בן צדיק, או צדיק בן רשע?

הרב דסלר מסביר שלכל אחד מהם יש מעלה: צדיק בן רשע ראוי לשבח על כך שלא למד ממעשיו הרעים של אביו ובחר בדרך טובה. אולם אצל צדיק בן צדיק קיימת סכנה אחרת - שהדברים יתקיימו אצלו רק כהרגל חיצוני.

כולנו יודעים עד כמה קשה, למשל, להכניס כוונה יומיומית עמוקה לתוך נוסח התפילה שהורגלנו לומר מילדותנו. כאשר צדיק בן צדיק עובד כדי להפוך את הדברים מהרגל חיצוני לקניין פנימי, "כמו איש המוצא את הכל מחדש", הריהו זוכה למעלה גדולה (שם ח"ב עמ' 205, ח"ג עמ' 124).

ואיך כל זה קשור אלינו?

לפעמים גם אנחנו נדרשים לעשות סוג של 'לֶךְ לְךָ'. רבים מאתנו גדלו בבית של שומרי תורה ומצוות, והתחנכו על יסודות שונים בעבודת ה'. אבל אם אנחנו רוצים להיות כנים לגבי עבודת ה' האישית שלנו, ובפרט לגבי ההתמודדות שהביאה אותנו לכאן, לא תמיד מספיק לפעול מכוח ההרגל. לפעמים אנחנו צריכים לעצור רגע ולהתבונן פנימה כדי לבדוק מה באמת יוביל אותנו לתוצאה הרצויה (לפעמים כדאי גם שנתייעץ עם אנשים בעלי ניסיון).

אם מה שגדלנו עליו עובד - זה מצוין! נמשיך להשתמש בו בכל הכוח. אבל אם לא, אולי עלינו לבחון אפשרויות נוספות. אולי רצון ה' מאתנו הוא 'לֶךְ לְךָ' - אל מה שמותאם למבנה הנפש שלך, אל כלים ותפיסות שאיתם נוכל באמת להגיע אל הארץ המובטחת. כאשר הדבר נעשה בצורה נכונה, לא מדובר כאן חלילה בפריקת עול, אלא להיפך - בקבלת עול אמיתית, בצורה פנימית ומתוך התבוננות, ולא מתוך הרגל חיצוני.

G-d My Sister, G-d My Wife "A sound! My beloved knocks! Open your heart to Me, My sister, My wife, My dove, My twin (5)." In these stirring words, King Solomon describes the Jew both as G-d's spouse and as G-d's sibling. There are times when the Jew is situated in the holy-land, inspired and motivated to live a spiritual and G-dly life. Like in a good marriage, the Jew is excited about G-d, yearning to be close to Him and fulfilled by having a relationship with Him. But then come the days when you enter into a psychological "Egypt," where your inner spirituality is numbed, as you are overtaken by self-centered lusts, beastly cravings, negative impulses, and enslaving addictions. Your marriage with G-d seems all but dead. The key to survival at those moments is to remember that G-d is not only a spouse but also a sibling. We are sacred and G-dly not just because we feel it and we love it, but because a person is inherently a sacred creature, and G-dliness is intrinsic to the human being's very composition. Whether I'm in the mood for it or not, when I behave in a moral and spiritual way, I am being loyal to my true self. You are holy not because you feel holy, but because you are essentially holy – this is one of the most fundamental ideas of Judaism, expressed in the first narrative about the first Jew. When the Russian winter threatens to freeze our souls, it's time to recall the warmth provided by G-d as a member of the family. It's time to remember the intrinsic bond that exists between you and your sibling that will never fail And just with our earthly marriages, we are ready to leave Egypt, and restore the "husband-wife" connection, in which our relationship with Hashem fills our nervous system with bliss and inner wholeness and serenity. Jacobson, "My Sister Or My Wife?"

theyeshiva.net, published Oct 30 2025. Full essay, his subheads kept. · source

Rated above everything else on the marriage list. Zero overlap in 151 campaigns, and it is the only candidate we have written for a marriage that is flat rather than one that is fighting.

A Self-Absorbed Husband? This week's Torah portion, Lech Lecha, relates how a famine breaks out in the Land of Canaan, and Abraham and his wife Sarah head down south to Egypt. As they approach Egypt, Abraham voices his fears to his wife that the Egyptians, notorious for their immorality, might kill him so that they may lay their hands on the most beautiful Sarah. "Please say that you are my sister," Abraham pleads with his wife, "so that they will give me gifts for your sake and my life will be spared [2]." This is a difficult story to digest. Abraham, the founder of Judaism, considered one of the most spiritual humans of all time, the person who gave the world the gift of Monotheism and taught humanity the value of kindness, seems to be all-consumed by the fear for his life, and totally unconcerned with the fate of his wife. What is even more disturbing is Abraham's interest that "they give me gifts for your sake," while his wife would be enduring abuse and humiliation. No less absurd is the fact that the Torah finds it necessary to begin the biography of the father of the Jewish people with this episode, as though signifying that it contained the fundamentals of Jewish faith and practice... Two approaches can be found among the commentators. The Ramban (Nachmanides, circa 1194-1270) writes that Abraham performed indeed "a great sin, inadvertently." The Zohar explains (Tazria 52a), that Abraham, who knew Sarah's superior spiritual quality, was certain that no harm would befall her. He was only fearful about his own fate. Yet, as in every story of the Torah, this narrative contains a profound psychological and spiritual message, articulated by the Chassidic masters. [3]. A Tale of Two Loves What is the difference between the sibling relationship and the spouse relationship? A spouse you choose; siblings you don’t choose. Your connection with your brothers and sisters is natural and innate. The bond between siblings is constant and immutable. Whether you love your brothers or not, he will always remain your brother; you are eternally connected by genes, culture, and soul connection. Conversely, the bond with a spouse is subject to change and fluctuation; today you are married, but in ten years from now you may sadly be divorced. Yet paradoxically, the love of a sibling – even at its best -- is calm and placid; the love of a spouse, on the other hand, is capable of becoming fiery and passionate. Because the love of a sibling is inborn and natural, it can never fully die, but we also don't get too excited about it. It is part of who we are. The love of a spouse is something created anew as a result of two separate individuals coming together at a later stage in life. The distinctiveness , rather than the sameness, of the two individuals linked in marriage is what gives the relationship its intensity and drama, feelings that cannot be found even between close siblings. Yet this same quality is also the reason some marriages are short-lived. Passion can flourish, but passion can fade away. And when the marriage does fail, you fall back on the innate bond that exists among family members, who are, hopefully, always there for you. Tough Times The story of Abraham and Sarah is also allegorical. When one is situated in the Holy Land, a term symbolizing a psychological state of serenity and spirituality, he is her husband and she is his wife. They care for each other and look out for each other in a way that only a husband and wife can. Those are the days when you wake up in the morning and say, "Thank you G-d for giving me such a special person in my life." But then a famine may erupt, starving your heart and dulling your senses, you end up in "Egypt," which in Hebrew means "constraints" and "limitations." You lose your passion for your spouse, barriers between you are constructed, and your love becomes a challenge. At these moments, one must remember that his wife is, in essence, also a sister and that her husband is also a brother. Even if you don’t feel the connection, you remain connected innately; even if you don’t experience the romance consciously , you remain linked essentially . Because the shared bond between a wife and her husband is not only the result of a created union at a later point in their lives, rather the spouse relationship is innate and intrinsic, in the words of the Zohar, "two halves of the same soul [4]." A marriage, in the Jewish perspective, is not only a union of two distinct people; it is a reunion of two souls that were one and then, before birth, separated. In marriage, they are reunited. The relationship between spouses goes beyond feelings. We crave to always be husbands and wives, but sometimes -- for our marriages to survive and thrive -- we must become brothers and sisters. Whether you feel it or not, your wife is one with you, always [5]. Do not allow the loyalty and trust to wane on both sides. Even if there are arguments, difficulties, and hardships, maintain loyalty to each other, like healthy and functional siblings. Abraham and Sarah taught us that when the relationship becomes challenging, you cease to be husband and wife; now you become brother and sister. You fall back on the innate, intrinsic oneness that binds you in an eternal link. This, in fact, brings an awesome benefit to a husband. When you are there for your wife, even when you're not in the mood for it, an extraordinary energy of love is later returned to you. That's why Abraham told Sarah that by saying that she was his sister, he would not only survive but would also receive special gifts. Marriages Today We have been in "Egyptian" exile for a long time. The above-mentioned secret has saved many of us and our marriages during challenging times. But now, as we prepare to return to our eternal homeland, we are empowered and summoned to cultivate a new posture, one of redemptive consciousness. When the brother and sister can openly embrace each other as husband and wife. Perhaps it is what is happening now for many couples. Yes, we have mastered the art of intrinsic oneness. But we want something more. We want the openhearted bliss that comes from two people experiencing full trust and safety with each other. That requires the work of exposing our vulnerabilities, pain, and fears, so we can also discover the internal yearning for authentic connection and attachment with our soulmate. === POST 1 - "a month ago" (so roughly mid-September 2026, before Sukkos) ===

"Starting to detail my journey. Hoping this will be of benefit to others, and a way for me to get chizzuk from myself and others.

To make a very long story short: I have been struggling with inappropriate content for over 25 years. I was first exposed when I was around 11 and have been battling since. I've had various successes over the years, but every few months or so, I inevitably stumbled.

I don't think I ever really believed that I had it within myself to stop. For so many years, I thought the solution was simply making sure I didn't have access, and that as long as I had the best filters, I would be fine.

This didn't work out too well.

I tried every filter out there, and while I was fully on board with filtering everything, I never really tried to stop testing the filters. I'm pretty computer savvy, and I was eventually able to skirt most filters in some form, only to then go out and get a different filter or multiple filters at the same time. I tried guessing PIN codes hundreds of times and playing with settings, etc. I would go months without access, but I also spent a lot of that time trying to get access.

Even when I couldn't get access through filters to images, etc., it wasn't enough. I discovered all kinds of ways to get stimulating content, like audiobooks, shopping websites, and other things. I always felt like I was chasing something to numb myself when I was feeling overwhelmed, down, or triggered.

This all changed recently. There was a glitch in my filter, I got some access, and I was discovered by my wife (not the first time). I never came so close to losing everything. It is still an extremely excruciating process of rebuilding trust and connection with her.

But I'm done with my old ways of just filtering. I am doing something different.

I am being radically open with her (as incredibly painful as that has been). I have a mentor I check in with every day before I go to sleep. I am admitting to myself that I CANNOT DO THIS ALONE.

This has been the most painful and strangely relieving realization over the past few weeks.

I've made a list of boundaries, green circles, red circles, a plan, etc. I'm fully invested and hope to post my progress here, mostly for myself.

It's been 29 days so far, and I'm proud to say I haven't crossed a single boundary. No filter poking. No self-touch. No using my computer to distract or numb myself in any form.

I'm feeling my strength build day by day. B"H.

I'm looking forward to entering the succah as a new person, and experiencing the words שהחיינו וקיימנו והגיענו לזמן הזה with new purpose and meaning.

Thank you for reading."

Why newcomers refuse the 12 Steps

Rafael's Hebrew gilyon, section 3. Hebrew below the translation. · source

The best-written thing in the batch. Nine English campaigns have mentioned the 12 Steps and every one is an advert. Not one explains why it works.

It Doesn't Sound Logical!

One of the things that holds new members back from joining the 12 Step program is the feeling that "it doesn't sound logical." A member who is clean today more than ten years described what he thought when he first heard about the program:

"The way things are now, I am trying with all my strength to stay clean, and even so I fall quite a lot. So if I surrender and give up the fight, and 'hand it over to G-d,' obviously the collapse will be total and will go completely out of control. That is basically releasing the brakes entirely."

Only after another two years of suffering, when no single solution worked for any length of time, did he come through the GYE website to the Step program, discover that it was not quite what he had thought, and he has been clean from then until today.

It is not only new members who are sceptical about the program. You can hear therapists express themselves this way too:

"It is not logical that a group of addicts who get together succeed where even the best professionals have failed. Addiction is such a deep problem. It does not sound reasonable that they really stay clean for years. And that whole spiritual business of theirs sounds really strange."

Sometimes feelings like these come from a superficial acquaintance with the program and its concepts, but there is also truth in them. It indeed is not always logical, and that is exactly the point we want to take up this time.

If we look honestly at the road we have walked until today, and at the various plans we have had over the years for how to stay clean long term, we will be able to admit that "it sounds logical, but it doesn't work." We do not actually manage to carry out our wonderful plans. That is exactly the reason that brought us here. We came to a clear recognition that we are powerless and cannot solve the problem, not by our own strength and not by the strength of our brilliant plans. If a logical plan were enough to beat an addiction, many of us would have come out of it long ago.

In the Step program they offer us the opposite: "it works, but it doesn't sound logical." And here we are asked to choose. Will we insist on clinging to our own logic at any price, however painful? We may know deep down that our ideas do not work, but it is hard for us to give them up, both because they sound so good and because we have invested so much in them.

The Step program is a results-oriented program, not a program of theories. People who have suffered so much and have already come "to the edge" cannot afford to amuse themselves with beautiful ideas. One of the sharper expressions in the Step program suggests to new members that they "take the cotton wool out of their ears and put it in their mouth." That is, stop broadcasting their own ideas, and start listening to those members who have been clean for years, in an attempt to understand what worked for them and to do the same.

True, our head tells us that we are cleverer than them, and it may even be correct, but it is not helping us stay clean the way they do. So we need to check, not what sounds sophisticated and logical, but what actually manages to produce the kind of results we have never managed to achieve. We have had our fill of what sounds logical and does not really work.

Don't worry. Nobody is asking us to throw away our intellect. We will go on using it, just in a slightly different way. We will come to the Step meetings and make a comparison: we will check where our own plans lead us, and where the Step program leads the members who take it seriously. And not only in the matter of taavah, but in every area of our lives.

It may not sound logical, but it works!

The Hebrew, verbatim

זה לא נשמע הגיוני!

אחד הדברים שמעכבים חברים חדשים מלהצטרף לתוכנית 12 הצעדים, הוא התחושה ש"זה לא נשמע הגיוני". חבר שנקי היום מעל עשר שנים סיפר, שכאשר שמע לראשונה על התוכנית, המחשבה שלו הייתה:

"במצב הנוכחי אני מתאמץ בכל הכוח להישאר נקי, ולמרות זאת אני נופל די הרבה. אז אם אכנע ואוותר על המאבק, ו'אמסור את זה לאלוקים' - ברור שההידרדרות תהיה מוחלטת ותצא לגמרי מכלל שליטה. זה בעצם לשחרר לגמרי את הבלמים!"

רק אחרי שנתיים נוספות של סבל, כששום פתרון אחד לא עבד לאורך זמן, הוא הגיע דרך אתר GYE לתוכנית הצעדים, גילה שזה לא היה בדיוק מה שחשב, ונקי מאז ועד היום.

לא רק חברים חדשים ספקנים לגבי התוכנית. אפשר לשמוע גם מטפלים מתבטאים כך:

"לא הגיוני שחבורת מכורים שמתכנסת יחד מצליחה במקום שבו אפילו מיטב אנשי המקצוע נכשלו. הרי התמכרות היא בעיה כל כך עמוקה. זה לא נשמע סביר שהם באמת נשארים נקיים לאורך שנים. גם כל הנושא הרוחני הזה שלהם נשמע ממש מוזר".

לפעמים תחושות כאלה נובעות מכך שמדובר בהיכרות שטחית עם התוכנית ועם המושגים שלה, אבל גם יש בהן מן האמת. זה אכן לא תמיד הגיוני, ולנקודה הזו בדיוק אנחנו רוצים להידרש הפעם.

אם נתבונן בכנות בדרך שבה הלכנו עד היום, ובתוכניות השונות שהיו לנו לאורך השנים איך להישאר נקיים לאורך זמן, נוכל להודות ש"זה נשמע הגיוני, אבל זה לא עובד". אנחנו לא באמת מצליחים לבצע את התוכניות הנפלאות שלנו. זו בדיוק הסיבה שהביאה אותנו לכאן. הגענו להכרה ברורה שאנחנו חסרי אונים ולא יכולים לפתור את הבעייה - לא בכוחות עצמנו, ולא בכוח התוכניות המבריקות שלנו. אם תוכנית הגיונית הייתה מספיקה כדי לנצח התמכרות - רבים מאתנו היו יוצאים ממנה כבר מזמן.

בתוכנית הצעדים מציעים לנו משהו הפוך: "זה עובד, אבל לא נשמע הגיוני". וכאן אנחנו נדרשים לבחור: האם נתעקש להיצמד להיגיון שלנו בכל מחיר, כואב ככל שיהיה? אנחנו אולי יודעים בתוך תוכנו שהרעיונות שלנו לא עובדים, אבל קשה לנו לוותר עליהם, גם בגלל שהם נשמעים כל כך טוב, וגם בגלל שהשקענו בהם כל כך הרבה.

תוכנית הצעדים היא תוכנית מכוונת תוצאות, לא תוכנית של תיאוריות. אנשים שסבלו כל כך הרבה והגיעו כבר "עד הקצה" לא יכולים להרשות לעצמם להשתעשע ברעיונות יפים. אחד הביטויים החריפים בתוכנית הצעדים מציע לחברים חדשים "להוציא את צמר הגפן מהאוזניים, ולהכניס אותו לפה". כלומר, להפסיק להשמיע את הרעיונות שלהם, ולהתחיל להקשיב לאותם חברים שנקיים לאורך שנים, בניסיון להבין מה עבד להם ולנהוג באותו האופן.

נכון שהראש שלנו אומר שאנחנו יותר חכמים מהם, ויכול להיות שזה אפילו נכון - אבל זה לא עוזר לנו להישאר נקיים כמוהם, ולכן אנחנו צריכים לבדוק - לא מה נשמע מתוחכם והגיוני, אלא מה מצליח להביא תוצאות כאלה שמעולם לא הצלחנו להשיג. אנחנו כבר שבעים ממה שנשמע הגיוני אבל לא באמת עובד.

אל דאגה. לא דורשים מאתנו לזרוק את השכל. נמשיך להפעיל אותו, אבל בצורה קצת אחרת. נגיע למפגשים של תוכנית הצעדים, ונערוך השוואה: נבדוק לאן התוכניות שלנו מובילות אותנו, ולאן תוכנית הצעדים מובילה את החברים שלוקחים אותה ברצינות. ולא רק בעניין התאווה, אלא בכל תחומי חיינו.

זה אולי לא נשמע הגיוני, אבל זה עובד!
