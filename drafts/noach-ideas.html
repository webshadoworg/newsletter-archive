A

התיבה הרעה — the ark you seal yourself into

Mendy, #990 · Rafael’s Hebrew Noach 5787, section 1 · my rank 1 of 3

RECOMMEND for the dvar Torah slot

A teiva is good or bad by its context. Hashem told Noach to build it, and then told him צֵא מִן הַתֵּבָה, get out. The good teivos are the beis medrash, any positive activity, and a filter. The bad teiva is the one you seal yourself into alone, which רק נראית ’תיבת נוח’. למעשה, היא בכלל לא ’תיבה’. היא ’טביעה’ — it only looks like Noach’s ark; it is not an ark at all, it is a drowning.

Sources
R’ Chaim Vital, שערי קדושה ח”א שער בUNVERIFIED Taava rooted in יסוד המים. Not verified. Sefaria rejects the book-level ref; Sha’arei Kedusha is a complex-structure text there. The idea is standard, the citation is what is open. Needs the sefer or a working ref before it prints in his name.
Rambam, Yesodei HaTorah 4:1VERIFIED The four yesodos, cited correctly.
צֵא מִן הַתֵּבָה, Bereishis 8:16VERIFIED Exact.
Against the archive

NEVER RUN Grepped all 151 archived campaigns for teiva and ark material. Zero hits.

Why it is worth it

It names the filter as the teiva, and that is the hinge of the whole issue. D below is a man who has just filtered his computer and is asking what to do now the battle has moved into his head. The dvar Torah and the forum section would be answering each other across the page. Neither English candidate does that. It is also the only one of the three that leaves the reader with something to do.

What is wrong with it

The pun dies in English. תיבה and טביעה share nothing in English; ark and drowning are not a pair. That paragraph needs a different English hook and that is a copy decision, not mine. Rafael’s CTA furniture (the Israeli number, the gmail address) comes out.

B

The Lower the Draw, the Higher the Flight

Chayim, #984 · 386 words · my rank 2 of 3

Runnable, after a new title and one positioning sentence

The keshes is an archer’s bow. The further back you draw the string, the further the arrow flies. ירידה לצורך עליה: the pull-back is what makes the launch. Closes with a guard paragraph that this is not “falling is good” and never a reason to let go.

Sources
Bereishis 9:13VERIFIED אֶת קַשְׁתִּי נָתַתִּי בֶּעָנָן. “The Torah literally calls it Hashem’s bow” is exact: קַשְׁתִּי, possessive.
ירידה צורך עליה, “the chassidishe seforim”VERIFIED Likkutei Torah in seven places, Avodat Yisrael, Beit Aharon, Sod Yesharim, Sha’arei Avodah. Chassidus is where it lives. Good attribution.
Ramban, Bereishis 9:12WRONG CITE Not cited by the draft, and it is the best-known meforash on keshes-as-bow — running the other way. Citing Chizkuni: the bow is shown inverted, so nobody reads arrows as being shot from heaven, and “the bow has no rope upon which to bend the arrows.” On this pasuk the classical reading is a bow deliberately undrawn and unstrung.
Against the archive

NEAR MISS No keshes, rainbow, bow, arrow or ירידה לצורך עליה dvar Torah anywhere in the archive. One passing sentence of the idea in 2025-12-19 Mikeitz, inside a Chanukas Habayis message. Not a blocker.

Why it is worth it

New to this list, correct on its pasuk and its attribution, and it already carries its own guard against the misreading.

What is wrong with it

The title is the rationalization in six words. Lifted out, which is how a headline and a subject line travel, “the lower the draw, the higher the flight” says a deeper fall buys a higher ceiling. That is the 11 PM thought verbatim. The body spends its climax arguing against its own title, and a title does not get walked back.
Also: “a fall is not the end” is GYE’s position. “The lower the descent, the higher the ascent” is a larger claim.
Fix is a title built on the guard rather than on the draw, plus one sentence placing the bow as a mashal rather than as the pshat in the ois. Both in the author’s words, not mine.

C

Every Generation Has Its Tzaddik

Chayim, #982 · about 500 words · my rank 3 of 3

RECOMMEND against. This list has read it.

Rashi’s two readings of בְּדֹרֹתָיו; the objection that a tzaddik learning eighteen hours a day could not be nothing in an earlier dor; the mashal of a tzaddik coming to town who “keeps Shabbos,” then 1900s America; and the close that the people on GYE are the צדיקים תמימים of our day.

Sources
Rashi, Bereishis 6:9VERIFIED Reported correctly. יש מרבותינו דורשים אותו לשבח... ויש שדורשים אותו לגנאי (Sanhedrin 108a).
Bereishis 6:9, the pasukUNVERIFIED The Torah reads בְּדֹרֹתָיו, plural, “in his generations.” The draft puts “in his generation” inside quotation marks with the cite. The plural is Rashi’s dibur hamaschil and in several meforshim it is the peg the drush hangs on. Minor, but it is a quoted pasuk carrying a citation.
The Chazon Ish sentenceUNVERIFIED “That wasn’t the nisayon of his time and place.” A contestable claim about a named recent gadol, from an unnamed author, and a diminishing one. Nothing is quoted in his name so it is not an invented quote, but it is the kind of sentence that draws letters. The 2024 version made the same point about “Europe of old,” with no name attached.
Against the archive

RE-RUN 2024-09-27, sent to this list for Noach 5785. Its second section is headed “The True Tzadikim of Our Generation.” Same Rashi, same eighteen-hours objection, same tzaddik-comes-to-town mashal, same 1900s America, same five-item list, same GYE close. Beat for beat, in the same order. A rewrite, not a resend, but every move in it is a move the list has already read.
Stated plainly: there is no Noach 5786 campaign in the archive — the files jump Bereishis Oct 17 2025 to Lech Lecha Oct 31 2025. So I cannot rule out that it also ran last year. The ESP can.

What is wrong with it

This version’s tzaddik does not include the man who fell. The 2024 list had five items and the fifth was “one who picks themselves up after falls and continues fighting.” This draft drops it: the definition is the one who does not look, and the chizuk lands only on those “quietly fighting.” For a reader who fell this week, and that is a large part of this list, it reads as a line he is on the wrong side of.
Second week running that the dvar Torah candidate is a prior piece reworked. Open either way: who wrote this, and is it its own author revisiting himself? That is a different call from a re-run.

Why it is worth it

The best forum item offered in weeks, and it is the question the issue is already about. It also contains the section’s title, in @theboywholived’s own words: “the hard part isn’t getting a filter on your computer. It is filtering my own brain.”

What is wrong with it

Two of the replies tell him to fight thoughts and two tell him not to. This is invisible until the four sit next to each other.
• @theboywholived: “work on eliminating each thought. Don’t allow it any room.”

• @BenHashemBH: “you don’t want to make too big a deal out of the bad thoughts, that makes them stick around.”
In the order supplied a reader meets suppression first, stated most forcefully, and it is the approach the other two are warning him off. A man who then tries to eliminate each thought and finds they multiply has been handed the standard failure.
Recommendation only: @theboywholived’s real contribution is his honesty, not his method. Keep the two honest lines, drop the eliminate-every-thought paragraph, let the method come from @chosemyshem and @BenHashemBH with @noilad closing. That also solves him appearing in two consecutive issues.
