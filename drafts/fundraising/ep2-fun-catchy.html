The one line that made this email possible

Chapter Two ends with him looking out the window, sipping coffee, and saying this:

For almost twenty years... they’ve kept building. And you make that possible.

[Beat.]

Please don’t.

That is already a donor ask, written from the other side. It is the single best fundraising line in the whole series and it costs us nothing to use, because he said it, not us. Both options are constructed to arrive at it and then answer it.

Option B: the lines I wrote for him, needs your approval

Rule on file is that nothing goes in his mouth without you approving it. Option B cannot send until you read these. Verbatim script is in black, mine is in red.

I have held this position since the beginning of time and I have never once had to put anything in writing.

For the record, my system was excellent. So I went everywhere. Into their pockets. Into every quiet moment. And I did it without ever raising my voice, because eventually, I learned... I didn’t need to scare anyone. Shame worked. Isolation worked. Hopelessness worked.

Three tools. No overhead. Best century I ever had.

Then a small operation in Brooklyn took an interest in my methods. So they connected people. Removed the shame. Studied why people fall... and how they rise.

I would like to be precise about the damage. A man who used to sit alone at two in the morning with nowhere to go now has a number to dial, 1-877-GYE-4-YOU, and he does not even have to give his name. He is answered in seconds, at any hour, and nobody is waiting ahead of him. Do you understand what that does to shame? It needs a locked door. They took the door off.

I adapted, naturally. I always adapt. Every time I got smarter... so did they.

So I reviewed the file, and I have located the real problem. It is not the organization. Organizations run out of money. The problem is that somebody keeps paying for this one. That somebody is you.

For almost twenty years... they’ve kept building. And you make that possible.

Please don’t.

Sincerely, Your Yetzer Hara

Two judgement calls inside that. “Best century I ever had” keeps him out of the twenty-years number, which the film owns and which I did not want a joke standing next to. “They took the door off” is the one image in either option that is not in the script; it is there because shame is the tool he names first and the hotline is the answer to it, and the letter needed him to say why out loud.

He is signed “Your Yetzer Hara” and the email says we are forwarding it unedited. Nobody real is quoted anywhere in either option, and nothing is attributed to Rabbi Nadel except his own sign-off.
