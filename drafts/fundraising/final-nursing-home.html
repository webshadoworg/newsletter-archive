This is our last email before Sukkos, and we wanted it to be in their words, not ours.

“I still remember the last time I fell. It was Purim night, after learning a whole seder in my kollel and feeling so good and shtark. I got home, and ten minutes after I got home...

“I cried that night into my pillow. When will this end? Will this go on forever? Where will I end up? I honestly did not want Moshiach to come, because I knew I couldn't greet him like this.”

A 33-year-old yungerman wrote that to us on his 90th day clean. Nobody around him knew. His email continues:

“A few days later, out of desperation, I decided to check out your website. I never knew that you help with such stuff, I thought you were just some sort of internet filter. I read some posts on the forum, and I was shocked that there is a whole world out there working together to solve these problems. I decided to sign up. My hands were literally shaking, I couldn't believe what I was doing.

“The hardest thing I ever did was call someone up over the phone. I was SOOO embarrassed. But it probably was the smartest thing I EVER did.

“B"H I hit 90 days. It's pretty incredible. I never thought I could make it this far. And I know I can continue. I can beat this.”

He is one of countless ehrliche people who thought it would never end.

A 40-year-old fellow wrote to us:

“I came here as a lost soul. I was broken and destroyed. I thought that I would still be acting out when I was in a wheelchair in a nursing home. I thought I was a sick man who would never be cured.

“B"H I'm 66 days clean and doing really well. You have literally saved my life.”

And one message from a 30-year-old who wrote to us after he donated to this campaign:

“I'm a recent member, and I can't explain it, but I feel better than I've felt in as long as I can remember. And that includes getting married, which was not that long ago.

“I carried this with me through all my kabbalos, through rabbeim I hinted to but never told, thinking it would go away with marriage. I don't have to hope anymore that I can ‘at least live the last 20 years of my life, when I'm too old to have these taavos, and until then just hide it and hope no one finds out.’

“I had an extra life handed to me.

“Ok, I've gotten myself worked up. I'm gonna go donate again. Ahhh, this is life. I wanna dance, cry, and just soak up the wonderful world we live in.”

Each of these Yidden was sure he was the only one, and that he would carry his secret alone forever.

Where would each of them have turned if GYE had not been there?
