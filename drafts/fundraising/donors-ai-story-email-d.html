Then artificial intelligence grew up. Here is what we did with it, and what we did not.

Dear *|FNAME|*,

Last year, we thought we had reached the ceiling. We had built everything the technology of the day allowed a small organization to build.

Then, in the months since our last campaign, artificial intelligence grew up.

Chazal say, מיניה וביה אבא ליזיל ביה נרגא, the handle of the axe comes from the forest itself (sanhedrin 39b). The same technology that has made this nisayon harder than it has ever been landed in our hands as a tool. We spent this year learning to master it. Here is what that looks like for one Yid who reaches GYE today.

He arrives at midnight. A year ago he met a registration form. Tonight he meets a conversation. It asks what brought him here, answers his questions, and gives him honest feedback on the spot. By the time he is signed up, we already know why he came and what he hopes for.

He needs a plan, now. Planning was always the pillar most people never got to. It meant courses and worksheets, and a man in the middle of a struggle rarely sits down to worksheets. Now he talks it through. He says where he is stuck, and he walks away with a real plan for the next hour and the next ninety days. Every one of these tools is trained on twenty years of GYE’s work and reviewed by our own team. Many members tell us it feels equivalent to a real mentor.

He has no internet. He may be holding a phone that cannot open a browser. He can dial 1-877-GYE-4-YOU, talk it through, and be put through to a person or leave a message for one. (Separate from 1-866-KEDUSHA, which you read about on Monday. That line answers a family’s questions about filters and phones. This one is for the man himself.)

Chazal also say, אין הקומץ משביע את הארי, a handful does not satisfy the lion. However large the team, no human being is there at midnight, and at six the next morning, and again on a lunch break. A struggle does not keep office hours. For the first time, support does not either.

A 24-year-old married member wrote to us about the new site:

“It’s clear that it was made with so much wisdom and care, designed for fighters by people who GET it. You’ve not only given me my life back, but continue to enhance it and help me grow.”

None of this replaces human support, and we remain fully committed to it. It is a complement.

The research is now saying the same thing. A 2026 randomized trial found that a human coach produced broader gains in motivation and resilience than a chatbot, and a review of 35 studies found the best results when AI worked between sessions and a person stayed in the picture. Some of the gains vanished once the human support stopped.

There are things only a person can do. A sensitive question, a delicate situation at home, a judgment call about which strategy fits this man’s life and not the one in the manual. Truly understanding someone’s situation. And the thing no tool can give: the sense that another person cares about you and that you are not alone. Connection is one of GYE’s three pillars, alongside motivation and planning, and it is the one that turns a good week into lasting freedom. A member with a mentor does not want to let someone down. He keeps going after a fall, because someone is waiting to hear from him.

So this year we invested in people too. We set up a case management system, so every member is followed up with and nobody falls between the cracks. We have full-time support staff answering the phones and the messages. We have a large team of volunteer mentors, and we are launching a mentor training program so they keep sharpening their skills, the women’s pilot first, the men’s to follow.

A 22-year-old bochur wrote to us this past March:

“I’ve worked with a mentor over the phone for over 6 months and I’ve been clean since then. I have not relapsed at all.”

What comes next. In the coming year we are building dedicated platforms for teenagers, for parents, and for spouses, each with the same two layers behind it: tools that answer at any hour, and people who care.

Our budget carries both. The mentors and the staff, and the new tools that stand in for them between calls. Last year you carried it. We are asking you to carry it one more year.
