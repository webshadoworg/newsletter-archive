“Ask It Anything”: GYE Launches an AI Hotline for Kedusha Questions, No Internet Required

By Rabbi Yaakov Nadel

Try this. Pick up any phone and dial 1-866-KEDUSHA (533-8742). A voice answers. It is not a person, and it is not a menu. It is an AI, built by Guard Your Eyes, and it will talk with you about any kedusha question you have.

Ask it something real. “My daughter wants to upgrade her phone for her bas mitzvah.” “Which filter works on a work laptop without blocking the sites I need for parnassa?” “When should I speak to my boys about kedusha?” It asks about your family and your community’s standards and gives you an answer drawn from nearly 200 vetted resources. Free, anonymous, any hour. No internet needed.

The hotline is one of three parts of Project Kedusha, launched this Elul by Guard Your Eyes, and featured in Mishpacha Magazine last week.

Why you need this

Almost every frum home today has questions about keeping its devices protected, and most of us have no idea where to get a straight answer.

  • How do I protect my smartphone properly, without breaking the apps I need?
  • Which kosher phone makes the most sense for me, and which one for my teenager?
  • What is the best first phone for a child, and at what age?
  • Is there a kosher AI service my kids can use for school, or that I can use for business?
  • What filter works on a work laptop, and who will help me set it up?
  • How do I lock down the smart TV, the Xbox, the car screen?
  • When and how should I speak to my boys about kedusha?

Until now, getting a clear answer to any of these meant asking around, calling three organizations, and doing your own research. The information existed. It was scattered across dozens of providers; it changed every few months, and nobody had put it in one place.

Now you get the answer instantly by calling the hotline or asking at Kedusha.com. And it is current. GYE has partnered with Mishor in Lakewood, under Rabbi Leizerson, to keep every recommendation up to date as new kosher phones, filters, and AI tools come out, with the insider’s view of what actually works.

Three ways in

So we built it. Project Kedusha brings close to 200 vetted resources from dozens of organizations under one roof.

1-866-KEDUSHA, the hotline you just dialed. The full library by voice, for anyone, and especially for those without internet.

Kedusha.com, the same library online, kept up to date, with an AI chat trained on our own database. Type a question and get instant, personalized guidance.

The Kedusha Guide, in print. Close to 100 full-color pages in six sections: Education by age, Chizuk, Struggles, Kedusha Safeguards, Screen Addiction, and the Kosher Aisle of safe entertainment for children. The Safeguards section alone compares every filter in America and Israel side by side, every popular kosher phone by region and community standard, and the devices people forget: the Xbox, the smart TV, the Tesla.

Wherever you see the GYE badge, it is ours. Everything else is the work of others, and that is the point. This is all of Klal Yisroel’s solutions in one place.

Rav Yosef Elefant of the Mir called it “a game changer.” Harav Simcha Bunim Cohen of Lakewood: “Now that AI has come up, which is another tool of the Yetzer Hara, Project Kedusha is teaching us how to use it.” Harav Uri Deutsch, Mara D’asra of Forest Park: “I was very moved and extremely impressed by this new effort, both the printed and the electronic resources, to be mechazek what we know is the nisayon of Klal Yisroel in our time.”

A public service of Guard Your Eyes

Project Kedusha is not a new organization. It is a public service of Guard Your Eyes, which, as you may know, has spent nineteen years helping Yidden who struggle with internet addiction and shmiras einayim. Tens of thousands have come through GYE’s forum, mentors, and programs, most of them quietly.

This is the first thing GYE has built for everyone. Not for people in crisis, but for every home with a device. In Rav Elefant’s words, instead of helping only those who struggle, GYE can now upgrade the kedusha of all of Klal Yisroel.

The reviewer has been fighting GYE for 19 years and has been losing ground the whole time. His complaint: every time he evolves, GYE evolves with him. This year he asked for thirty seconds to tell his side, and he ends by asking you, personally, not to help.

GYE Launches Project Kedusha: One Address for Every Question About Technology and Kedusha

Yeshiva World News

Try this. Pick up a phone, any phone, and dial 1-866-KEDUSHA (533-8742). Then ask it something real. “My daughter wants to upgrade her phone for her bas mitzvah.” “Which filter works on a work laptop without blocking the sites I need for parnassa?” “When should I speak to my boys about kedusha?”

What comes back is not a menu. It is an answer, tailored to your family and your community’s standards, drawn from close to 200 vetted resources. Free, anonymous, and available at any hour. No internet needed.

The hotline is one of three parts of Project Kedusha, launched this Elul by Guard Your Eyes (GYE), the organization that for nineteen years has helped tens of thousands of Yidden struggling with shmiras einayim and the challenges of the online world.

The problem was never a lack of solutions

“Not everyone who comes to GYE is fighting an addiction,” the organization explains. “Many just need the right guidance. But between dozens of kosher phones, filters, kosher AI tools and educational resources, few people have the time or knowledge to figure out what’s right for them.”

Klal Yisroel is blessed with remarkable resources for every one of these questions. Filters, kosher phones, curriculums, chizuk programs, therapists, hotlines. Nothing was missing except a way to find them, and to know which one fits a specific home.

So GYE set out to bring every organization working on kedusha under one roof, and make it simple for any family to see what fits them. The result comes in three forms:

  1. The Kedusha Guide, in print. Close to 100 full-color pages, close to 200 vetted resources from dozens of organizations, organized into six sections: Education by age, Chizuk, Struggles, Kedusha Safeguards, Screen Addiction, and the Kosher Aisle of safe entertainment for children.
  2. Kedusha.com. The same library online, kept current, powered by an AI chat trained on GYE’s own database. Type a question and get instant, personalized guidance.
  3. 1-866-KEDUSHA. The same guidance by voice, for anyone, and especially for those without internet.

The largest section, Kedusha Safeguards, compares every filter in America and Israel side by side, along with all popular kosher phones by region and community standard, routers, accountability software, the new kosher AI tools, and the devices people forget: the Xbox, the smart TV, the Tesla. It lists all the worldwide TAG offices, and Project STEP for anyone who needs help setting up a filter or covering its cost.

“Wherever you see the GYE badge, it is ours,” GYE says. “Everything else is the work of others, and that is the point. This is all of Klal Yisroel’s solutions under one roof.”

“A game changer”

Rav Yosef Elefant of the Mir called Project Kedusha “a game changer.” Instead of helping only those who struggle, GYE can now upgrade the kedusha of all of Klal Yisroel.

Harav Simcha Bunim Cohen, Rav of Kahal Ateres Yeshaya in Lakewood: “Now that AI came up, which is another tool of the Yetzer Hara, Project Kedusha is teaching us how to use it.”

Harav Uri Deutsch, Mara D’asra of Forest Park, Lakewood: “I was very moved and extremely impressed by this new effort, both the printed and the electronic resources, to be mechazek what we know is the nisayon of Klal Yisroel in our time.”

The content is overseen by Rabbi Leizerson of Mishor in Lakewood, who keeps every solution current as filters update, kosher phone models change, and new tools appear.

Removing the shame

For years, GYE was identified with crisis, and crisis is hard to speak about. Project Kedusha changes the conversation. The Guide belongs on every Jewish bookshelf, not because something is wrong in the house, but because every family is navigating technology today.

“A man will type at two in the morning what he could never say out loud at two in the afternoon,” says GYE founder Yaakov Nadel. “Our only job is to make sure he gets the best resources possible, in the most empathetic and Torahdik way, and that a real human is standing right behind it.”

This is one project of Guard Your Eyes

Project Kedusha is not an organization of its own. It is one project of Guard Your Eyes, which has spent almost twenty years on a single question: how do you help a Yid hold on to his kedusha in a world that was rebuilt around the internet.

Thousands of people come to GYE every year, quietly, most of them certain they are the only one it has ever happened to. What they find is a website, a forum where nobody uses his real name, a 90-day chart, chizuk emails, coaching, a partner program, trained mentors, a hotline at 1-877-GYE-4-YOU, and staff who answer the phone. Over a hundred thousand Yidden have been empowered to protect and elevate their kedusha. Tens of thousands of marriages have been strengthened.

None of it is automated. GYE builds with the newest technology it can get its hands on, Project Kedusha included, and then puts a real person behind it, because accountability and judgment come from people and nothing else comes close.

Project Kedusha is only the first step. For almost twenty years, GYE focused mainly on struggling adults, roughly 5,000 new ones a year. This year the organization begins the largest expansion in its history: three dedicated platforms for teenagers, parents and spouses, each with real support. The goal is to reach 25,000 Yidden a year, five times as many as today.

The other side tells the story

This year GYE handed the narration to its opponent. Thirty seconds, the yetzer hara’s own account of a fight he has been losing, and his one complaint: every time he evolves, GYE evolves with him. He ends it by asking you not to help. Watch it and decide for yourself.
