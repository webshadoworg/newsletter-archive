GYE Launches Project Kedusha: One Address for Every Question About Technology and Kedusha

Yeshiva World News

Try this. Pick up a phone, any phone, and dial 1-866-KEDUSHA (533-8742). Then ask it something real. “My daughter wants to upgrade her phone for her bas mitzvah.” “Which filter works on a work laptop without blocking the sites I need for parnassa?” “When should I speak to my boys about kedusha?”

What comes back is not a menu. It is an answer, tailored to your family and your community’s standards, drawn from close to 200 vetted resources. Free, anonymous, and available at any hour. No internet needed.

The hotline is one of three parts of Project Kedusha, launched this Elul by Guard Your Eyes (GYE), the organization that for nineteen years has helped tens of thousands of Yidden struggling with shmiras einayim and the challenges of the online world.

The problem was never a lack of solutions

“Not everyone who comes to GYE is fighting an addiction,” the organization explains. “Many just need the right guidance. But between dozens of kosher phones, filters, kosher AI tools and educational resources, few people have the time or knowledge to figure out what’s right for them.”

Klal Yisroel is blessed with remarkable resources for every one of these questions. Filters, kosher phones, curriculums, chizuk programs, therapists, hotlines. Nothing was missing except a way to find them, and to know which one fits a specific home.

So GYE set out to bring every organization working on kedusha under one roof, and make it simple for any family to see what fits them. The result comes in three forms:

  1. The Kedusha Guide, in print. Close to 100 full-color pages, close to 200 vetted resources from dozens of organizations, organized into six sections: Education by age, Chizuk, Struggles, Kedusha Safeguards, Screen Addiction, and the Kosher Aisle of safe entertainment for children.
  2. Kedusha.com. The same library online, kept current, powered by an AI chat trained on GYE’s own database. Type a question and get instant, personalized guidance.
  3. 1-866-KEDUSHA. The same guidance by voice, for anyone, and especially for those without internet.

The largest section, Kedusha Safeguards, compares every filter in America and Israel side by side, along with all popular kosher phones by region and community standard, routers, accountability software, the new kosher AI tools, and the devices people forget: the Xbox, the smart TV, the Tesla. It lists all the worldwide TAG offices, and Project STEP for anyone who needs help setting up a filter or covering its cost.

“Wherever you see the GYE badge, it is ours,” GYE says. “Everything else is the work of others, and that is the point. This is all of Klal Yisroel’s solutions under one roof.”

“A game changer”

Rav Yosef Elefant of the Mir called Project Kedusha “a game changer.” Instead of helping only those who struggle, GYE can now upgrade the kedusha of all of Klal Yisroel.

Harav Simcha Bunim Cohen, Rav of Kahal Ateres Yeshaya in Lakewood: “Now that AI came up, which is another tool of the Yetzer Hara, Project Kedusha is teaching us how to use it.”

Harav Uri Deutsch, Mara D’asra of Forest Park, Lakewood: “I was very moved and extremely impressed by this new effort, both the printed and the electronic resources, to be mechazek what we know is the nisayon of Klal Yisroel in our time.”

The content is overseen by Rabbi Leizerson of Mishor in Lakewood, who keeps every solution current as filters update, kosher phone models change, and new tools appear.

Removing the shame

For years, GYE was identified with crisis, and crisis is hard to speak about. Project Kedusha changes the conversation. The Guide belongs on every Jewish bookshelf, not because something is wrong in the house, but because every family is navigating technology today.

“A man will type at two in the morning what he could never say out loud at two in the afternoon,” says GYE founder Yaakov Nadel. “Our only job is to make sure he gets the best resources possible, in the most empathetic and Torahdik way, and that a real human is standing right behind it.”

The beginning of a bigger expansion

Project Kedusha is only the first step. For almost twenty years, GYE focused mainly on struggling adults, roughly 5,000 new ones a year. This year the organization begins the largest expansion in its history: three dedicated platforms for teenagers, parents and spouses, each with real support. The goal is to reach 25,000 Yidden a year, five times as many as today.

How to get the Guide

Shuls, schools and organizations can request copies in quantity, and individual copies can be ordered at kedusha.com/order. Everything in it lives, kept current, at kedusha.com, and the hotline 1-866-KEDUSHA stands ready for any question.

GYE’s annual Yomim Noraim campaign, which makes Project Kedusha and all of GYE’s work possible, runs through Yom Kippur at charidy.com/gye. Every donor receives a free printed copy of the Kedusha Guide for their home.
