Later

Parked deliberately. Members currently get no notification when staff reply, which is the worst possible state — none of this is worth delaying v1 for. Nothing here requires changing the v1 design.

Next up — the discreet version

A second, quieter template members can switch to

Some members use a dedicated anonymous address, where preview exposure barely matters. Others are on a primary or shared inbox, usually because making a second address was too much friction. Neither GYE nor an algorithm can guess right for an individual, so the member picks.

Roughly: from name GYE, subject “You have a new message,” fixed preheader, and no message text in the body. Same skeleton, same button, same footer geometry as v1, with each footer linking to the other.

Make it a member-level flag, not a per-email toggle. Someone who asks for discretion should not then get a newsletter with a loud subject line.

One open question when we get there: the body says “Chaim sent you a message.” A bare first name reveals almost nothing and keeps a trace of warmth. Fully anonymous would be “A message is waiting for you.”

Worth deciding separately: stripping the envelope and stripping the body are two different things. Discretion is about what is visible without opening. Removing the message text too also throws away the “show the specific thing” effect that makes the email feel like a reply to you. Unless the worry is a shared inbox someone else opens — then it is right.

Letting members choose it themselves

The template is the cheap half. The choice mechanism — link, page, member flag, propagation across every email type — is the expensive half, and in v1 it stands between members and getting notified at all. Until it exists, staff flip the flag on request.

Use a confirmation page, not a one-click link. Corporate mail scanners and inbox prefetchers follow links automatically and would silently switch people to the discreet version. One small page with one confirm button.

Put the offer in the welcome email. No sensitive content there, and the member gets the choice before a real support reply ever arrives. Better than asking a privacy question at signup, where the beta already showed people balking at handing over an email at all.

When replies thread into GYE Connect

Four changes, nothing structural

  • Reply-To becomes reply+{{conversation_id}}@mail.gye.org
  • The line under the button flips from discouraging replies to inviting them: “Or just reply to this email, it goes straight to Chaim.” No more hand-carrying
  • Inbound processing strips quoted text and signatures before the reply enters the thread
  • Revisit the from name — this is the moment “Chaim from GYE” earns its keep, because hitting reply genuinely emails Chaim

Getting v1 right means the member-facing experience never has to migrate.

Why the layout looks the way it does

From real notification emails by Google Ads, Google Account, Facebook, LinkedIn and Google Maps:

  • A logo is customary but small. Corner or a compact centered mark. None use a full-width coloured brand band. The logo is an anti-phishing signal, not a billboard.
  • The person is the hero. LinkedIn’s message notification is the closest analogue — sender photo, name and title centered and dominant, logo a small afterthought.
  • Warm framing first, mechanics demoted. Google Maps leads with “Thanks for sharing your knowledge” and pushes status into small metadata below.
  • Show the specific thing. Google Maps embeds the actual map of the place you edited. Here that is the agent’s real opening line rather than a generic “you have a message.”
  • A short “why you’re getting this” line is standard.

This is a 1:1 transactional email, not a broadcast, which is why the newsletter’s rating widget and editorial chrome are out.
