The lock screen cuts around 35-40 characters. Subject A is 34 with a short name and 37 with a long one, so it clips for some agents and not others. Subject B fits any name. That is question 2.

The preheader is the only anonymity risk

The from name and subject are fixed strings we control. The preheader is the one slot that can carry the agent’s actual words onto a lock screen. Compare:

Generic: A reply is waiting for you on GYE.
Real first line: Hi, thanks for reaching out, and for trusting me with what’s…

The second one implies a confided problem to anyone holding the phone. Question 3.

Send headers

Never the agent’s name — a stable sender survives handoffs and does not look spoofed.
Fromhelp@gye.org — a real monitored inbox. No no-reply@ anywhere visible.
Reply-ToOmitted. help@ is already the reply target.
Return-Pathbounces@mail.gye.org — invisible plumbing, the one slot a subdomain belongs in.
Subject{{agent_first_name}} replied to your conversation Q2
Preheader{{email_preview}} — hidden div, first element in the body, 90-char cap. Q3
Snippet{{message_snippet}} — first ~250 chars of the reply, cut at the last sentence end before 250, greeting included.
LinksAll point at app.gye.org. Mail comes from gye.org, people go to app.gye.org.

From name and subject are send-time headers, not template HTML — a branch in the sending logic.
