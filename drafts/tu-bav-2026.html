One Conversation, From the First Click

Picture GYE as a small office serving fifty people.


We would know every one of them. We'd notice that the man who signed up two weeks ago hasn't come back since. We'd notice the one showing up every day who's probably ready for something deeper. And we'd reach out to each one personally, with something that actually fit where he was holding. In an office of fifty, you can't lose track of someone you see every day.


GYE serves far more than fifty. And since we wrote to you about deepening personal support, we haven't been waiting. Every person who signs up has been offered a real conversation with a real member of our team - welcome messages, follow-ups, reminders going out to the right groups at the right moments. Everybody gets a chance, many write back, and those conversations change lives daily.


But we were doing it with tools that weren't built for this. The outreach went to batches rather than to people. And there were two stages where, despite all our care, things could still go wrong.


The first moment: the first hello.


A man sits at his screen late at night. He's been thinking about this for weeks, maybe years. He finally comes to GYE, signs up, writes a few honest lines. Getting that far is the hardest thing he's done in a long time.


And then someone on our team has to reach out to a person carrying real shame, who has never spoken about this to anybody, and start a conversation from nothing. How do you break the ice with a man like that, when everything depends on the first message and you only get one try? Some of those first conversations landed beautifully. Others never got off the ground - not for lack of care, but because breaking ice is hard, and the man on the other side was already halfway to closing the tab.


So we moved the conversation to the very beginning. Our new intake - around the office we've taken to calling it "the open door" - is now in beta on gye.org/start. It isn't a form. It's a conversation. The first thing a person sees is a question - "Welcome to GYE. What are you hoping for here?" - and from there he just talks, in his own words, at his own pace. If he opens up, it goes deeper. If he hesitates, it makes room. If he's here for a son or a husband rather than himself, it turns that way instead. And we're straight with him: early on, in plain words, he's told that a real person at GYE will read what he shared and follow up personally.


By the time our staff member arrives, nobody is a stranger anymore. The man has already said the hard thing out loud and discovered that nothing bad happened. The ice is broken before the first human message is ever written.


And even those who start the conversation and don't finish it are teaching us something. We're now analyzing the full record of registration chats - including the people who opened the door and walked away. Where did the conversation lose them? What question came up that they weren't ready for? What's on the website that doesn't answer what they're actually asking? Every one of those incomplete chats is a window into what we could be doing better, both to bring more people through and to reach the ones who weren't ready to come all the way in.


The second moment: everything after.


In the past, everything used to depend on our staff - remembering who he was, digging up what he told us months ago, piecing the picture together, then starting the same detective work over for the next person. Hours that belonged to our members were going into figuring out who our members were.


This past year we put serious time into one question: what can the new AI technologies genuinely do for an organization like ours? One answer kept surfacing. AI is strongest exactly where we were weakest - reading through mountains of information and handing a human being the parts that matter.


The result is now live. We call it GYE Connect, because the connection that takes care of itself in a room of fifty is precisely what we had to build for thousands.


Every member is visible to our team, one by one: how he's been engaging, what he's working on, what he's avoiding, where he's holding right now. Outreach isn't a reminder sent to a batch - it's a specific message to a specific person about his actual situation. And when he writes in, everything he has ever shared with GYE is already gathered in one place and read for us before we read it: every past conversation with any staff member, his forum posts, his tracking history, summarized into one clear picture - with the small detail from eight months ago surfaced instead of lost. A staff member absorbs a member's full story in under a minute and spends his time on the part only a human can do.

That first conversation from the intake? It isn't filed away as a registration record. It flows straight into GYE Connect as the opening of his ongoing relationship with us. What he typed at one in the morning is the same thread a staff member picks up the next day. One conversation, from the open door onward, that never closes.


And the system carries it forward on its own. When we match a member with a mentor, we follow up at the moments that matter - an early check-in, three months, a year. It no longer depends on anyone remembering. The follow-up arrives because the system brings it, not because someone happened to think of him on a busy Tuesday. That's the difference between meaning to keep a promise and being built to keep it.


We stay with a member until he's doing well and genuinely no longer needs us - or until he's ready to turn around and help the next person walking through the door.


We're still building, and every week teaches us something new. But the direction is certain: no one falling through the cracks, and a system that guarantees it.
