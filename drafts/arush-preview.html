A Happy Day of Judgment

Rosh Hashana is the yom hadin, and nobody needs to be told to be afraid of it. There is a whole month of selichos in front of it. But it is worth being exact about what kind of day it is.

Rav Shalom Arush puts it this way: the judgment of Hashem is not a trial convened in order to punish a person. The awe of the day is real, but it is the awe of standing in front of a king, not the dread of a man waiting to hear his sentence.

Wanting counts before anything works

And then he says something that is hard to accept the first time you hear it. If a person only wants to change his path, only wants to, from that moment he is already counted as a complete tzaddik. Before he has managed to change anything. Even if the changing is going to take him years.

Read that slowly, because it is easy to turn it into an accusation, and it is not one. He is not saying that a man who wants it badly enough stops falling. He is saying the opposite. He says outright that it takes time, that it can take years, and that the wanting counts from the first moment anyway.

So if you have fallen many times and you still want out, that is not evidence that you do not really want it. That is the thing itself.

The King is in the field, and He did not come for the people who were already close

Why Elul? He brings the Baal HaTanya’s mashal. In Elul the King is not in the palace, He is out in the field, and He shows a smiling face.

And then the question nobody usually asks about that mashal. When the King is in the palace, who can get in? Who can get close? Who is even going to bring you in to see Him? It takes an enormous amount to reach a king. In Elul the King comes out to the nation Himself, and He goes down to low places, so that all of them can come close to Him before the day of judgment. Even the ones standing in the lowest place.

Which is the part worth sitting with. The mashal is not a nice thought for people who were doing well anyway. The whole point of it is where He went.

What to think on the day

So the day is not fear that ends in a man’s anxiety, chas veshalom. This is what he says a person should be thinking on Rosh Hashana:

Now the King is judging us. He wants to do good for us, to know what we are missing, what the conditions are that we need in our lives in order to come close to Him.

That is a different question from the one most of us walk in with. Not what am I about to be charged with, but what do I actually need in order to get close. Walk in with that one.

Adapted from a Rosh Hashana piece by Rav Shalom Arush. The mashal of the King in the field is the Baal HaTanya’s, in Likutei Torah, Re’eh.
