A Freilechen Yom HaDin

Rosh Hashana is the yom hadin. There is fear and dread of this day, and that is why there is a whole month of selichos and preparation before it.

But the judgment of Hashem is not a trial held in order to punish a person. The אימה of the day is not פחד, it is יראת כבוד. Because if a person only wants to change his path, only wants to, even if he has not managed to change anything yet, even if it will take him time, even if it will take him years, from that moment he is already counted as a complete tzaddik.

Everyone wants to be okay. And if you at least want to be okay, that is already okay.

So the אימה belongs only to a person who does not want. Someone who wants can smile. He can be happy. He can come into Rosh Hashana ready. We are happy on Rosh Hashana, very happy. Not light headed, no דברים בטלים, but happy and sure of ourselves.

The Baal HaTanya says that in Elul the King is in the field and shows a smiling face. What is that really about? Moshe Rabbeinu went up to shomayim on Rosh Chodesh Elul, forty days and forty nights, and came down on Yom Kippur. Those forty days he was up there are the days of teshuva, from Rosh Chodesh Elul until Yom Kippur, because what Moshe Rabbeinu brought about in shomayim was that these days should be days of teshuva and mechila and selicha and kapara.

That is why the King, the King of the world, is out in the field in these days. When the King is in his palace, who can get in to him? Who can come close? Who is even going to bring you in to the King? It takes an enormous amount to reach a king. But in Elul the King comes close to the nation. The King Himself goes down to low places, so that every person, even someone standing in the lowest place there is, can come close to Him before the day of judgment.

And what is Rosh Hashana? It is the day the malchus of Hashem is revealed. The day we crown Him. And the King wants everyone to come close to Him, everyone. That is why He comes down to us. And we are happy that the King comes out of his palace for us. That is an enormous simcha.

So Rosh Hashana is not fear that brings a person to anxiety, chas veshalom. Just the opposite. A person should be thinking:

Now the King is judging us. He wants to do good for us, to know what we are missing, what the conditions are that we need in our lives in order to come close to Him.

Let all the thoughts be good thoughts.

Based on an essay by Rav Shalom Arush shlita. The mashal of the King in the field is the Baal HaTanya’s, in Likutei Torah, Re’eh.
