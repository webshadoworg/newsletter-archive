I think there may be an important nekudah to consider here:

Do YOU want to do it?

What happens with us a lot of the time is that we say to ourselves, "I don't want to do it! I want to change! I want to never do this again!" This brings on a constant struggle. We see it as: The Yetzer Hara wants us to do something, we don't want to do it, and we fight against it. It's basically stretching a rubber band. Hence, what I understand to be your question:

How is it possible to just fight that off forever?

I think, that If we are able to step outside of ourselves and look a little more objectively at things, we would see something that is initially very scary to accept.

We do want to do these things. Our actions, the fact that we allow ourselves to wind up back here time after time, shows that we actually DO want it. We are not in a true state of pure wanting to do the Ratzon Hashem. It's not just the Yetzer Hara that we are fighting. When we are triggered, WE want to do it. And, by the way, the more we even try bringing the Yetzer Hara into this equation, the more cover we are giving ourselves, taking away how much we want to do it deep down.

The truth, therefore, is that the only way to actually stop is to not wait for that blast of motivation, (Usually heard, seen, or experienced in pretty close proximity to a fall, BTW) that will change us over into people who want tahara.

Instead, we have to learn how to do the work of changing ourselves from something that we want.

If someone adamantly insists, "I hate unhealthy food! I don't want it anymore! I just LOVE Celery!"

How long can he hold out for? His insistence that he doesn't want junk will conflict with his body's reality that he does, until he runs out of willpower.

He can only hope for success by admitting the truth to himself instead of (literally) sugarcoating it.

Even if he can recognize and admit that, if our friend the dieter spends his whole day fighting off thoughts of Oreos, Bloomeos, or whatever more Yeshivish equivalent there is to Oreoes without being the Paragon of Taanugei Olam Hazeh that those things are, he will not succeed either. The same problem mentioned before: There is a limited amount of willpower a person has to put in.

The solution, therefore, must be to not be dealing with these thoughts and problems for months until I snap.

That takes ideas and practice of how to healthily deal with urges.

For

  • mechazaik ends on “its almost as if i dont want to completely leave go” and buries it. JacktheJew digs it back up and builds the whole answer on it. That is answering the man, not the topic.
  • It explains why the mindset he is asking for does not work: “His insistence that he doesn’t want junk will conflict with his body’s reality that he does, until he runs out of willpower.” Willpower is finite. That is the real reply to “how do you fight it off forever”.
  • The dieter is a good mashal because it is about food and not about a lesser struggle. It does not shrink the problem.

Against

  • It tells the reader he wants it. “When we are triggered, WE want to do it.” True, and said in the first person plural by someone including himself. But a reader who fell last week reads that as an accusation unless the framing is protected. Do not let a heading or an intro turn his “we” into “you”.
  • It names a solution it does not give: “That takes ideas and practice of how to healthily deal with urges.” Ending a section there is a tease. It needs a practical answer printed under it, which is exactly what the two short ones are.
  • Longest of the five. It can lose the Oreos paragraph without losing the argument.
