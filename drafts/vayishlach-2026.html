Yaakov’s Three-Step Plan for the Battle

Based on an essay by Rabbi YY Jacobson


After thirty-four years away, Yaakov heads home to Eretz Yisrael, and learns that Eisav is marching toward him with an army of four hundred men. The Torah tells us how Yaakov prepared, and Rashi lays out his strategy in three parts: doron, tefillah, milchama, tribute, prayer, and war. First he sent Eisav lavish gifts to soften him. Then he poured out his heart in prayer. And finally he readied himself and his family to fight.


The stories of the Torah are never only history. Yaakov and Eisav are also two forces inside every one of us. Eisav is the animal self, the ego, the cravings, the impulsive pull. Yaakov is the neshama, the higher, idealistic soul. And every single day, the Eisav within us comes marching, four hundred strong, to overwhelm the Yaakov within us. So how do we survive that confrontation? With Yaakov’s exact three-step plan.


Step one: tribute. Honor the animal, don’t starve it.


The first move is surprising. Yaakov doesn’t go straight to war. He sends Eisav gifts. The lesson is that we cannot win this battle by pretending the body doesn’t exist. We are not meant to be ascetics who deny every physical need. The animal soul has legitimate needs, to eat, to sleep, to exercise, to earn a living, to be a healthy human being in a physical world. We owe it an honest daily “tribute” of our time and energy. Starving it completely, treating every physical need as the enemy, is not the Torah’s way, and it usually backfires.


Step two: prayer. Keep it from taking over.


But once you’ve let Eisav become your “brother,” once you’re engaging the physical world, how do you keep it from taking the throne? Through tefillah. Yaakov prays, “Save me from the hand of my brother, from the hand of Eisav.” Prayer is the daily moment when you put the ego to sleep and reconnect to your soul, when you step out of the tent and look up at the stars. All day we think about the tent. In davening, we remember the sky. And this is intensely practical: when you start your morning grounded and connected, you walk into the day with the clarity and strength to keep the animal in its place. When you skip it, you’re far more exposed to whatever Eisav throws at you.


Step three: war. For the furnace moments.


And still, tribute and prayer are not always enough. Sometimes, by day or by night, an urge rises up burning in the heart like a furnace, and it cannot be reasoned with or gently redirected. For that moment, there is only one answer: war. You don’t sit down and negotiate with it. You hit it hard, refuse it outright, and get on with your life. War is not pleasant. But when something is coming to destroy you, standing and fighting is sometimes the only way to survive the attack.


There’s a story that captures the alertness this takes. Reb Simcha Bunim of Peshischa once said that a truly spiritual person is one who always feels as if his head is lying in a guillotine, with his yetzer hara hovering above it, ready to strike at any moment. “But Rebbe,” a chossid asked, “what if a person doesn’t feel that way?” Reb Simcha Bunim answered, “Then his head has already been cut off.”


He didn’t mean we should live in terror. He meant we should never fall asleep at our post. The person who thinks the battle is over, who assumes Eisav has gone home, is the one most likely to be caught. The one who stays awake, alert, and ready is the one who wins.


So when Eisav comes marching this week, and he will, remember you already have the plan, straight from Yaakov Avinu. Give the body its honest due. Stay connected through tefillah. And when the furnace moment comes, don’t negotiate. Fight. Tribute, prayer, and war. It carried Yaakov safely past an army of four hundred, and it will carry you too.


FROM A MEMBER

“It did not make any sense to work this time”

Based on a forum post by @thistimeillwin


One year ago, on 12 Kislev, I started another of the hundreds of attempts in the past 30+ years. An attempt to rid myself once and for all of the P & M which was dragging me down and destroying my life (ruchniyus and gashmiyus). Somehow, as the song goes ‘this time it will be was different.’ and I soared to heights beyond belief, with now one full year free of the garbage I have been dealing with for most of my life.


As I’ve mentioned before, it did not make any sense to work this time. One of my biggest triggers, stress, was at an all time high. Financial stress was at a peak, and was dealing also with a personal crisis. Family life was also facing huge challenges, as I’ve detailed in the BB Forum. Yet, with all that, I was able to reach goals I never have before, and not for lack of trying. It is truly a gift from Hashem, who has seen the number and level of attempts and decided that it was time. He also eased one trigger for me, which was my Shalom Bayis, which suddenly took an about-face and got much better (now when the bickering starts, I try to control myself saying my year of sobriety depends on this). I also met and spoke with a few mentors, though not as much as I should, but it made the fight not as lonely.


Since my initial 90-day journey, I have been mostly in cruise-control, with white-knuckle days lessening, and getting used to a life without P & M. I know I have much more to work on, in my battle against lust and in my general ruchniyus wellbeing, but the challenges of my day-to-day life have left those mostly unattended. Today will hopefully be a wakeup call for me that I can’t sit on my laurels and declare victory; the clock is ticking and I need to get to the next phase of the battle.


I have been largely absent from the Forum, for a few reasons. The Forum had become an obsession (maybe taking the place of my P & M obsession - better, but not healthy). I was checking on it multiple times a day and reading every post, and it was affecting my work. And work got very busy, so I dropped off for a while, just logging in to update my count.


In my personal life, things have eased a bit. My financial issues peaked sometime over the last year, but have pulled back from the brink for the time being. I have come to terms with some personal challenges. And family life has also regulated a bit, though not fully to where I would like it. In summary, it’s normal living.


Hashem should help me continue on the path to וטהר לבנו לעבדך באמת, and should help all of the chevra here who are trying. Remember, I was at it for close to 35 years, was falling multiple times a day, tried stopping hundreds of times. If I can do it, you can too! Hashem will see all your failed attempts and at a certain point say אפתח לו פתח כפתחו של אולם.
