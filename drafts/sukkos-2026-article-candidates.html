Three article candidates, ranked

The three maamarim from Rafael’s Hebrew Sukkos issue. Nothing on this page is a draft. The summaries are mine so you can rank them without reading the Hebrew.

Verdict

All three are printable. Take the Arba Minim piece. It is the only one of the three that opens on the reader’s own thought instead of on a source, it makes one move rather than four, and it lands on a line that is also a subject line. It is the least work to bring into English and the most likely to be read to the end.

The Rashbam piece is the close second and it is the one whose application is best aimed at our list: it is written for the man who is doing well, which is who a Yom Tov issue reaches. It costs more, because four moves have to become two.

The ananei kavod piece has the best Torah and the weakest ending. It hands the reader a category instead of a step, and it asks him to decide whether he is an addict. I would hold it.

This is a recommendation. What runs is yours.

Candidate 1 · Arba Minim · 503 Hebrew words

I am an esrog. And so are you.

Read it in the Hebrew issue

What it says

The Midrash (Vayikra Rabbah 30:12-14) reads the four species as four kinds of Jew. The writer opens by putting himself in the table and coming out an arava: years of falling, so no taste and no smell, dry and beaten.

Then the turn. The same Midrash also reads the four species as four limbs. If the esrog is the heart, look inside: what do you actually want? If there were a button here that would free you from this fight forever, and you were at your best, clear-headed, not tired, not under pressure, in the middle of Neilah, do you press it? Obviously you press it.

So that is the heart. The arava is the mouth, the outside, what is actually happening, and yes that still needs fixing. But the struggle is not the identity. He closes on the reader directly.

It carries a four-row table: species, which kind of person, which limb, what it means for us.

The line it turns on

אבל אני? אני אתרוג! וגם אתה - אתה אתרוג!

working rendering, not copy But me? I am an esrog. And you too, you are an esrog.

Why it works here

  • It opens on the reader’s own thought, not on a description of the piece. The writer sorts himself into the arava before he argues anyone out of it.
  • One problem, one move, one landing. It is the tightest of the three.
  • The close is quotable and it is a subject line.
  • It does not blame. It never tells the reader the fall was his fault or that he was given the test so he could pass it.

What it costs

  • The button thought experiment is a device. It is the author’s own, so it prints as written, but it is the one place an English reader could feel handled.
  • The table needs rebuilding for email. It is the only candidate with one.

Candidate 2 · Rashbam · 469 Hebrew words

Why the sukkah is in Tishrei and not Nisan

Read it in the Hebrew issue

What it says

The question: yetzias Mitzrayim was Nisan, so why is the mitzvah of sukkah in Tishrei? The Rashbam (Vayikra 23:43). Sukkos is chag ha’asif, the ingathering. Most of us are not farmers, so he explains it: a farmer is not on a monthly salary, he works a whole year and then the entire year’s income lands at once.

That is the dangerous moment, and the Torah names it in Eikev: you will eat and be satisfied and build good houses, and your heart will grow haughty, and you will forget Hashem, and you will say my own strength made me all this. So exactly at the ingathering, exactly at the moment of success, we go out and sit in a hut.

The application, and this is the part that is about our readers: after a long clean stretch the thought arrives, this is completely behind me now, I do not need help any more. That thought is the writing on the wall. He compares it to a skin fungus: the doctor says thirty days of cream, it clears in two weeks, you stop, it comes back worse. It was always there, under the surface.

Then the paradox: we think forgetting where we were will make us feel better, and Sukkos, whose whole point is to make us remember it, is the one the Torah commands us to be happy on. Remembering where you came from is what feeds the gratitude and the motivation.

It ends on leaving the permanent dwelling, Sukkah 2a.

The line it turns on

בתוכנית 12 הצעדים מקובל לומר, שכשהתאווה הולכת - היא הולכת לחדר כושר, כדי להתכונן למאבק הבא…

working rendering, not copy In the 12 step rooms they say that when the lust goes away, it goes to the gym, to get ready for the next fight.

Why it works here

  • This is the one whose application is aimed squarely at our list. The reader it is written for is the one doing well, which is the reader a Yom Tov issue actually reaches.
  • The gym line is the best single sentence on the page and it survives translation intact.
  • The fungus mashal is concrete and nobody has used it in this newsletter.

What it costs

  • It is the longest and it makes four moves: asif, pen tochal, the fungus, v’samachta. In English at newsletter length something comes out, and that is an editorial cut, not a build decision.
  • It coins v’samachta b’nikyonecha off the pasuk. That reads well in Hebrew and needs a decision in English.
  • Warning a man on a good streak that he is about to fall is a hard note to land the week of Yom Tov. The Hebrew earns it. An English version has to earn it again.

Candidate 3 · Ananei Kavod · 382 Hebrew words

Which sukkah are you building

Read it in the Hebrew issue

What it says

Tannaic machlokes in Sukkah 11b: R’ Eliezer says the sukkos of the midbar were ananei kavod, clouds Hashem sent; R’ Akiva says sukkos mamash, huts they built themselves. The machlokes is really about how much of the protection was theirs.

He then notices that the pesukim use two different verbs for a person in a sukkah. Sitting, yeshiva, which is what the brachah says, and being hidden, tzefinah, titzpenem b’sukkah. Sitting is active: you eat, drink, sleep, walk around in it. Being hidden is passive: Hashem puts you there.

The application: a regular struggler is better suited to a sukkah he builds himself, with motivation, planning and connection. An addict needs the ananei kavod sukkah, given from above, which is the 12 steps. Then he immediately softens it: both are true for everyone, it is a question of emphasis. A regular struggler still needs siyata dishmaya. An addict is still not exempt from the work, taaseh v’lo min he’asuy, the miracle does not happen on its own.

It carries a small two-column table contrasting the two.

The line it turns on

הנס לא קורה לבד, וכדי "לא להיפלט מהענן החוצה" - נדרשת עבודה רבה ותחזוקה מתמדת של המצב הרוחני.

working rendering, not copy The miracle does not happen by itself. Not being spat out of the cloud takes real work and constant maintenance of where you are holding.

Why it works here

  • The best Torah of the three. The yeshiva and tzefinah observation is genuinely sharp and I have not seen it in our archive.
  • It is the shortest.

What it costs

  • The payoff is a framework, not chizuk. The reader finishes it holding a category, not a step.
  • It asks the reader to decide whether he is an addict or a regular struggler. That is the touchiest thing on this page for a mass English send, and the Hebrew hedges it in the very next paragraph, which tells you the writer felt it too.
  • It is the one that most needs a name on it. Naming who is an addict is a claim, and a claim needs an author.

What I need from you

Whose name does it run under?

These are Rafael’s Hebrew issue. An English version is a translation of somebody’s article, and I am not printing a translated piece with no byline or with a byline nobody cleared. Tell me the author and how he should be credited.

Translation or adaptation?

A straight translation keeps his structure and his sources and reads a little formal. An adaptation reads like our newsletter and stops being his article. Either is fine, they are different jobs, and the second one needs his yes.

One or two?

Candidate 1 and candidate 2 do not collide. One is about identity after years of falling, the other is about the danger of a good streak. They could run as the dvar Torah and the Yom Tov piece. Candidate 3 overlaps candidate 2 on the sukkah, so I would not run those two together.
