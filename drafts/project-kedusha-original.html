“Project Kedusha” - A New Year of REAL Change

“I’m struggling with you-know-what”
“I think my husband is having a hard time”
“I’ve been clean for two years and slipped last night, is it over?”
“My chavrusah confided in me and I don’t know what to say to him”

Every day, questions like these are asked by Yidden around the world.

A father in Lakewood: “My son saw something on his phone, what do I do?”
A rebbi in Flatbush: “I need help with a Talmid”
A mother in Yerushalayim: “We can’t afford a filter”
A bochur, alone, at two in the morning: “I gave my word to my Rav and broke it again”...

Kedusha is one of the greatest challenges facing Klal Yisroel today, and one of the hardest to even speak about.

For close to 20 years, Guard Your Eyes has been on the front lines of this struggle. The battle began with the internet, then smartphones, then social media, and now AI. As the technology changes, so must our response.

Through it all, GYE’s unique strength has been helping those already struggling, from typical teenage nisyonos through full-blown addiction, with tools built on Torah sources and the latest behavioral science. Our program stands on three pillars. One, motivation — why change matters to you personally. Two, planning — practical tools and a personalized roadmap. And three, connection — the glue that ties it all together for lasting change. Mentors, forums, a community who understand exactly what you’re going through.

And we never stop innovating.

As the world grows comfortable talking with AI, a door opened. Today, when a new member reaches out, GYE welcomes him. No forms, no passwords, just a conversation. People open up in ways they never could to another person. With our proprietary AI, trained on Torah sources, mental-health professionals, and our most experienced mentors, he feels understood from the first minute. And behind the scenes stand real people, ready to step in. Thank God, tens of thousands have broken free. And behind every number, marriages rebuilt. Self-respect restored. Fathers present again for their children. But helping people in crisis was never the whole story. Strengthening people before a serious struggle begins matters just as much. And GYE has been there from the earliest days.

Back in 2013, we helped build the VCF filter, which ultimately evolved into GenTech. We developed Webshadow for parent-child accountability, and we published filter guides, prevention tips for parents, and ran community Filterthons. That work continues today with our recently launched Project STEP. Many people want filters, but never get them. Either they’re not tech-savvy, or worried it’ll block too much, or they’re unable to afford one. Project STEP walks them through the whole process, with sponsors covering the cost for families in need.

We’ve also taken these solutions directly to the people.

For adults, the Kedusha Challenge, a 30-day program run by Rabbanim in their own kehillos. The Rav creates a WhatsApp group for his community, and every single day he’ll send on this group a short dvar chizuk and a challenge that will help people wean off of their phone addiction. We ran it in over 40 shuls during Shovavim last year and it was a smashing success!

For teens, Esa Einai — a 50-day Shovavim program now used by thousands. So many boys told us that they had their strongest winter z’man ever.

A Shulchan Aruch for Kedusha

Centuries ago, a Yid seeking practical halacha had to search across countless seforim — Bavli, Yerushalmi, and Rishonim — and many didn’t know where to turn until Rav Yosef Karo came and wrote the Shulchan Aruch. Lehavdil, we face that challenge today.

Klal Yisroel is blessed to have access to so many incredible resources built by many different organizations and companies. Filters. Kosher devices. Education. Recovery. But it can be overwhelming. Where do you start? What’s right for my child? For my community?

Our vision was a living, constantly updated Shulchan Aruch for Kedusha, so that any Yid with any question could find the answer. That idea became Project Kedusha. Project Kedusha has three parts. The Kedusha Guide, in print. Kedusha.com, online and powered by AI. And a hotline for those without internet.

This is the Kedusha Guide. Not a pamphlet. A real book. Close to 100 full-color pages. The first of its kind. The best resources in the Jewish community, gathered into one volume. Six sections: Kedusha Education, Chizuk, Struggles, Safeguards, Screen Addiction, and Kosher Entertainment.

For parents — how to have the conversation, for every age. For Chizuk — daily videos, shiurim, and seforim. For those who struggle — the complete GYE framework, and therapy referrals. For protection — every filter, every kosher phone, all 70-plus TAG offices, even the devices people forget: the Xbox, the smart TV, the Tesla. And for the family — kosher entertainment for every age, so parents finally have an answer to “I’m bored.”

Wherever you see the GYE badge, it’s ours. Everything else is the work of others. Because this isn’t about one organization. It’s all of Klal Yisroel’s solutions, in one place.

The Guide has drawn an extraordinary response. Rav Yosef Elefent of the Mir called it a game changer. Our goal: thousands of copies, through shuls, schools, and events across America and Eretz Yisroel. Because when a book sits on the shelf at home, or a table in shul, it does something profound. It normalizes these conversations. No searching. No shame.

But a printed book is fixed the day it leaves the press. And technology changes daily. Which is why we built Kedusha.com. Everything you’ve just seen, now alive online. Every resource updated continuously with our partners at Mishor. When a new filter launches, a new device appears, a new challenge emerges, Kedusha.com already knows.

Or you can do something no book has ever done. You can just ask. A father types, “I found my son watching things he shouldn’t.” The AI responds with warmth. Then does what a wise mentor would. It asks, “How old is your son? What device?” And then, it guides him to exactly the right resources, and how to speak with his son. A rebbi with a talmid who’s struggling. A man who can only type, “I need help with… you-know-what.” A mother looking for kosher videos. Each guided to exactly what they need. Anonymously. Instantly. Without judgment.

Kept up to date by the experts on the Mishor team in Lakewood, and with every conversation reviewed anonymously by our team, it learns from every interaction, shaping the next edition of the Kedusha Guide.

And for those without internet, the same intelligence is just a phone call away. Call 866-KEDUSHA. Ask your question and get your answer. This is the next chapter.

And remember those questions? The father, asking about his son’s first phone, now has an address. The rebbi, searching for resources, now has a library. The mother who couldn’t afford a filter, now has Project STEP. And the bochur, alone at two in the morning, is not alone anymore.

And here is why this matters so deeply. There’s a very large proportion of the veterans who fall prey to this addiction. Guard Your Eyes has always been identified with crisis. It’s hard to speak about, and many hesitate to reach out because of stigma and shame. Project Kedusha is a game changer for those people. GYE becomes just a part of a much broader conversation — raising healthy families, education, wise technology choices, and yes, helping those who struggle. Suddenly, the Kedusha Guide belongs on every Jewish bookshelf. Not because of a crisis, but simply because everyone is navigating technology.

Which is why the Guide was not written only for the person in the middle of a struggle. Every family in Klal Yisroel is already making these decisions. The first phone for a bar mitzvah bochur. The laptop a school now requires. The tablet the children share. The smart TV a neighbor is giving away. Most of us decide alone, quickly, and hope for the best. The Guide and Kedusha.com are simply the address to check first: what to buy, what to install, what to say, and who to call, long before any of it becomes a question about your own child.

Every family deserves a trusted place to turn. Project Kedusha. One small step for countless individuals. One giant upgrade to Klal Yisroel’s Kedusha.
