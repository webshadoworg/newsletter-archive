Elul is here, and for a lot of us it arrives as bad news - a month whose entire subject is everything we have not managed to fix. This new series is going to work through that, one idea at a time.


NEW SERIES · JOYFUL TESHUVA

What is the best way to use the month of Elul to reach freedom?

Elul, the way most of us hear it, is the month for going after what is wrong with us. The Lakewood Mashgiach, Rav Nosson Meir Wachtfogel zt’l, says, it’s the opposite: “עיקר העבודה באלול צריך להיות בעשה טוב דוקא, ולא בסור מרע!” - the main avoda of Elul has to be in aseh tov specifically, and not in sur me’ra. In these days of רצון ורחמים, the Mashgiach explained, נפתח המוח ומתרחב הלב - the mind opens and the heart widens. Elul is the month a person can carry more than he usually can. So the way you use it is by adding.


He put one condition on the adding, and it is the reason the whole approach either works or does not: “מתיקות בלימוד, אחרת האדם נהפך למתאוננים! ומחפש הנאות במקום אחר חלילה” - there has to be sweetness in the learning. Otherwise a person turns into a complainer, and goes looking for pleasure somewhere else, chas v’shalom.


And he said what we are actually up against. Not appetite - humiliation. “עיקר כח היצר להשפיל את האדם, ולשכח ממנו את מעלתו שהוא בן מלך” - the yetzer’s main power is to bring a person low and make him forget his own madreiga, that he is a ben melech. That is how a person comes to do things that do not belong to a ben melech at all.


He brings Yosef HaTzaddik at the hardest nisayon of his life, whose answer was “לי יש שייכות לאבות” - I am connected to the Avos; what would I have to do with what you are describing. And then the conclusion: “וכשנזכר ומרגיש שהוא בן מלך, ממילא מתנהג אחרת!” - when a person remembers and feels that he is a ben melech, he behaves differently on his own. That, said the Mashgiach, is the avoda of אני לדודי ודודי לי.


One last line from him, and it governs everything above: “אין דרך מסוימת ברורה לכולם, אלא כל אחד צריך לעמול” - there is no one clear derech for everybody; each person has to work his own out.


Now bring those three home. For a man who is still in the middle of this fight, each one of them turns into something specific.


Adding good, first. On GYE we call it the green circle: deliberately filling your day with productive, positive things. That is not a pleasant extra alongside the real work - it is one of the strongest defenses there is. A day with structure in it, and something good in the gaps, is a day that is much harder to fall in. And since there is no one derech for everybody, the circle is yours to build. For one man it is the morning: up on time, a seder that starts when it says it starts. For another it is the night, where the trouble usually is, and what he needs is a fixed routine and an hour when the phone is somewhere else. In yeshiva it is mostly the sedarim. Outside yeshiva it can be a set learning time, work you are moving forward in, exercise, a hobby you genuinely enjoy. That last one is not a compromise. A man with good things in his life is a much harder target.


Then sweetness, which for us is not a refinement, it is the diagnosis. The Mashgiach’s line about a person going to look for pleasure somewhere else is our own story told in advance. A life with nothing enjoyable in it will go looking for enjoyment, and it will find the worst version available.


Rav Michel Yehudah Lefkowitz zt’l heard the mechanism in a single word of the Gemara. Hashem created the yetzer hara and created the Torah as a תבלין for it - a spice. Not a רפואה, not a cure. A cure works whether or not you enjoy swallowing it; a spice only does its job if there is taste in it. Learning without sweetness, he said, does not protect a person from the yetzer hara at all. Which turns sweetness into an instruction rather than a mood. Chazal’s advice here is to learn what your heart desires, in the place your heart desires - so pick the sefer you actually want to open. And ask for it: והערב נא, every morning, is a request that the Torah be sweet in our mouths.


And remembering who you are, which is the hardest of the three to do on demand. It helps to know where the King is standing this month. All year He is in the palace, and getting in takes permission, protocol, and a reason to be there. In Elul, in the Alter Rebbe’s mashal, the King is out in the field - among ordinary people, on their ground, where anyone who wants to come over can come over, in his work clothes, with no appointment, and He receives every one of them with a smile.


You do not have to be cleaned up first in order to come over. That is the whole mashal, and it is why אני לדודי is not a slogan for the season. It is a reminder of who you are, in the month the King came out to the field to be with you.


ONE THING TO ADD THIS WEEK

Pick one thing to increase, and only one. Size it so a bad day cannot break it: five minutes of learning you actually enjoy, one seder you do not miss, one hour at night that has a plan in it. Not something to stop. Something to add.


The Mashgiach’s words are from Likut Reshimos b’Inyanei Elul v’Yamim Noraim (5766), pp. ה-ז. Rav Michel Yehudah Lefkowitz on tavlin is cited in Yechezkel Leiman, The Heart of a Nation (Feldheim, 2018), p. 77, on Bava Basra 16a. The King in the field is from the Alter Rebbe, Likutei Torah, Parshas Re’eh.
