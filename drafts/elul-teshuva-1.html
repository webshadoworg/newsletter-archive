Elul is here, and for a lot of us it arrives as bad news - a month whose entire subject is everything we have not managed to fix. This new series is going to work through that, one idea at a time.


NEW SERIES · JOYFUL TESHUVA

What is the best way to use the month of Elul to reach freedom?

In honor of the month, we want to bring a few very inspiring ideas about Elul from the Lakewood Mashgiach, Rav Nosson Meir Wachtfogel zt’l. They are four separate ideas, not one argument. Each one is worth sitting with on its own.


  1. Elul, the way most of us hear it, is the month for going after what is wrong with us. The Mashgiach says, it’s the opposite: “עיקר העבודה באלול צריך להיות בעשה טוב דוקא, ולא בסור מרע!” - the main avoda of Elul has to be in aseh tov specifically, and not in sur me’ra. In these days of רצון ורחמים, he explained, נפתח המוח ומתרחב הלב - the mind opens and the heart widens. Elul is the month a person can carry more than he usually can. So the way you use it is by adding.
  2. He put one condition on the adding, and it is the reason the whole approach either works or does not: “מתיקות בלימוד, אחרת האדם נהפך למתאוננים! ומחפש הנאות במקום אחר חלילה” - there has to be sweetness in the learning. Otherwise a person turns into a complainer, and goes looking for pleasure somewhere else, chas v’shalom.

    [Rav Michel Yehudah Lefkowitz zt’l heard the same thing in a single word of the Gemara. Hashem created the yetzer hara and created the Torah as a תבלין for it - a spice. Not a רפואה, not a cure. A cure works whether or not you enjoy swallowing it; a spice only does its job if there is taste in it. Learning without sweetness, he said, does not protect a person from the yetzer hara at all. And Chazal’s advice for enjoying your learning is to learn what your heart desires, in the place your heart desires.]
  3. What we are up against is not appetite. It is humiliation. “עיקר כח היצר להשפיל את האדם, ולשכח ממנו את מעלתו שהוא בן מלך” - the yetzer’s main power is to bring a person low and make him forget his own madreiga, that he is a ben melech. That is how a person comes to do things that do not belong to a ben melech at all. He brings Yosef HaTzaddik at the hardest nisayon of his life, whose answer was “לי יש שייכות לאבות” - I am connected to the Avos; what would I have to do with what you are describing. And then the conclusion: “וכשנזכר ומרגיש שהוא בן מלך, ממילא מתנהג אחרת!” - when a person remembers and feels that he is a ben melech, he behaves differently on his own. That, said the Mashgiach, is the avoda of אני לדודי ודודי לי.
  4. When people asked him outright what the derech of avoda for Elul should be, he said he had no derech to hand them: “אין דרך מסוימת ברורה לכולם, אלא כל אחד צריך לעמול” - there is no one clear derech for everybody; each person has to work his own out. Only a kabbala from his rebbeim: whoever works and exerts himself and sweats, and does not stop, will get somewhere in the end. Nobody arrives on yichus. A person’s whole madreiga is set by how much he toils. He would say it in Yiddish - “א טראפען שוייס ווערט ניט פארלאָרן”, a drop of sweat is never lost.

We think all four are especially relevant to GYE members - more than they may look at first. Here is how each one lands for a man who is still in the middle of this fight.


The first is close to our whole approach. Adding good, rather than going after what is wrong, is what we call the green circle: deliberately filling your day with productive, positive things. That is not a pleasant extra alongside the real work, it is one of the strongest defenses there is. A day with structure in it, and something good in the gaps, is a day that is much harder to fall in. And since there is no one derech for everybody, the circle is yours to build. For one man it is the morning: up on time, a seder that starts when it says it starts. For another it is the night, where the trouble usually is, and what he needs is a fixed routine and an hour when the phone is somewhere else. In yeshiva it is mostly the sedarim. Outside yeshiva it can be a set learning time, work you are moving forward in, exercise, a hobby you genuinely enjoy. That last one is not a compromise. A man with good things in his life is a much harder target.


The second is not a refinement for us, it is the diagnosis. A person going to look for pleasure somewhere else is our own story, told in advance. A life with nothing enjoyable in it will go looking for enjoyment, and it will find the worst version available. Which makes sweetness an instruction rather than a mood: pick the sefer you actually want to open, and ask for it - והערב נא, every morning, is a request that the Torah be sweet in our mouths.


The third is the one we would underline hardest. Anyone who has been in this fight a while knows how low it brings him, and knows that most of the falling happens after he has already written himself off. The Mashgiach is saying that the lowering is not the damage afterwards - it is the weapon itself. Which turns remembering who you are into avoda rather than comfort.


And it helps to know where the King is standing this month. All year He is in the palace, and getting in takes permission, protocol, and a reason to be there. In Elul, in the Baal HaTanya’s mashal, the King is out in the field - among ordinary people, on their ground, where anyone who wants to come over can come over, in his work clothes, with no appointment, and He receives every one of them with a smile. You do not have to be cleaned up first in order to come over. That is why אני לדודי is not a slogan for the season. It is a reminder of who you are, in the month the King came out to the field to be with you.


The fourth is for a bad week. A man who has fallen many times can be very sure that all the trying added up to nothing, and there is usually no result on the day itself to argue with him. A drop of sweat is never lost. The toil is the madreiga.


ONE THING TO ADD THIS WEEK

Pick one thing to increase, and only one. Size it so a bad day cannot break it: five minutes of learning you actually enjoy, one seder you do not miss, one hour at night that has a plan in it. Not something to stop. Something to add.


The Mashgiach’s words are from Likut Reshimos b’Inyanei Elul v’Yamim Noraim (5766). Rav Michel Yehudah Lefkowitz on tavlin is cited in Yechezkel Leiman, The Heart of a Nation (Feldheim, 2018), p. 77, on Bava Basra 16a. The King in the field is from the Baal HaTanya, Likutei Torah, Parshas Re’eh.
