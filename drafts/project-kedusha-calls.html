Even We Couldn’t Keep Up

For nineteen years Guard Your Eyes has been taking two kinds of calls. The hard part was never wanting to answer. It was that the right answer keeps changing.

A father calls to ask whether there is an app that can limit how long his ten-year-old sits with a tablet. Not a filter. Not therapy. A time limit. He apologizes before he asks, because somebody in shul gave him the Guard Your Eyes number and he suspects he has the wrong place.

He does not have the wrong place. Calls like his have been coming for years.

That is the part most people get wrong about this work. GYE is known for the man who is already stuck, and that is real. But the phone has always carried a second conversation alongside it, and it is the larger one. Parents who want to protect a child before anything happens. Families who have no problem at all and would like to keep it that way.

A rebbi in Flatbush with one talmid he cannot reach. A mother in Yerushalayim who has priced filtering four devices and cannot do it. A father whose fifteen-year-old left a search open, who does not want a program, he wants to know what to say at breakfast. A chassidishe family with no internet in the house at all, who still has to pay a water bill and order a ride to a wedding. A man with a perfectly kosher phone who reaches for it every free minute and has begun to be frightened of himself.

None of them is in crisis. All of them are decisions, and every one has a right answer somebody has already worked out.

Everything They Needed Already Existed

Klal Yisroel is not short of answers here. It is extraordinary how much has been built. Organizations filtering devices in dozens of cities. Seforim for a nine-year-old and for a chosson. Curriculums in Bais Yaakovs. Kosher phone standards worked out community by community. Hotlines, mentoring, sponsorship funds for families who cannot pay.

The father with the tablet did not need anything invented for him. He needed somebody who knew where to look.

The Answer Keeps Moving

Here is the thing GYE is willing to say about itself.

We could not keep up either.

A filter that is the right recommendation in Adar quietly stops being the right one after an update nobody announced. A phone that a kehilla accepted last year is not the one it accepts now. AI arrived and changed the question in a single season. Something new appears every few weeks, and working out which of them is actually good, on which device, at what price, in which community, in which language, is full-time work.

GYE’s expertise is treatment. We know how to sit with a Yid who is stuck. We were never on top of the technology, and we were spending hours we did not have trying to find out, on behalf of people who had been told we would know.

Which is the honest reason Project Kedusha exists, and the reason it is a partnership rather than a GYE product. The goal is not a bigger organization. The goal is that every family in Klal Yisroel that wants to be protected can find out how, quickly, without it costing them an evening.

One Address, Three Ways In

What came out of it is Project Kedusha, and this Elul it reaches Klal Yisroel in three forms.

The Kedusha Guide, in print, close to a hundred full-color pages: 230 vetted resources from dozens of organizations, across six sections. Safeguards, the largest of them, comparing every filter in America and in Eretz Yisroel, kosher phones by region, routers, accountability software for a man who must keep open internet, all seventy-plus TAG offices, and the devices people forget, the Xbox, the smart TV, the Tesla. Then Education, by age, from a book for a four-year-old to pre-marriage material. Chizuk. Struggles. Kosher Isle, which is what you put on instead. And Screen Addiction, which is not about content at all. It is a yearly edition, revised each year, and it can also be printed at home.

Kedusha.com, the same library alive online. A father types one sentence in his own words. What answers him asks his son’s age, the device, what the family can spend, which community, which language, and only then narrows 230 resources to the few that actually fit him, and tells him how the conversation in the morning might sound. So the mother in Yerushalayim gets Israeli filters, not American ones, and the father with the work laptop is not handed the answer meant for a family iPad.

A phone number, 866-KEDUSHA, answered the same way, for a family with no internet. It is answering now.

Wherever the GYE badge appears, that resource is ours. Everything else is somebody else’s work, printed with their name on it. Rav Yosef Elefent of the Mir called it a game changer. Rav Elya Brudny shlita his own words and exact title: need from you.

The Barrier Was Never the Answers

The father who apologized before he asked is the whole point.

He was not weak or negligent or in crisis. He was a normal father making a normal decision about a machine in his house, who could not find out what the right decision was, so he guessed. Multiply him by every family you know.

Information should not be the thing standing between a Yid and the solution he needs. Not in this, of all subjects.

The Guide belongs on a shelf in the house, next to the benchers, where a child can see it and a guest can pick it up. Not because something went wrong. Because every family is already making these decisions, and until this year most of us made them alone.
