PARSHAS NITZAVIM-VAYEILECH

You Don’t Stand There Alone

“Atem nitzavim hayom kulchem lifnei Hashem Elokeichem”, “You are standing today, all of you, before Hashem your G-d.” (Devarim 29:9)


Nitzavim is read right before Rosh Hashana, and the Nesivos Shalom notices that the pasuk stresses kulchem, all of you together. Why does it matter that we stand before Hashem as one?


He explains that there are two ways to approach Hashem: through din, strict judgment, or through rachamim, mercy. Rosh Hashana is the day of judgment, so how do we awaken mercy? Through the koach harabbim, the power of the group. A father’s mercy is aroused most when his children come to him together.


An individual Jew is called Hashem’s child as part of klal Yisrael. When a person binds himself to other Jews, the words “banim atem laHashem,” you are children to Hashem, rest fully on him, and a father’s mercy follows. This, the Nesivos Shalom says, is why Rabbi Akiva called “v’ahavta l’re’acha kamocha” the great principle of the Torah: loving your fellow Jew is how each person joins the group and draws on its strength.


Then he brings the line that reaches the hardest case of all. Quoting the Rebbe of Karlin, he teaches that even a Jew who has fallen in the worst way, who afterward feels he cannot stand and daven, feels he has no face to turn to Hashem, can still reach Him through the koach harabbim. By joining himself to the tzibbur, he stands before Hashem carried by the whole. A Jew is never completely pushed away, and that truth becomes available through the group, because when Jews join together, they are as one.


Before we apply that, it is worth being clear about who we are talking to. The men reading this learn, daven, raise families, give tzedaka, and are careful with mitzvos that plenty of people never think twice about. They are struggling in one specific area, and it happens to be one of the hardest there is. A person is not defined by his record in his hardest battle. And even inside this battle there are real wins: the day you got up after falling, the hour you held out, the call you made instead of giving in. Those count before Hashem, and they may be the most valuable thing any of us brings to Rosh Hashana.


So the koach harabbim is not a fallback for people with nothing of their own. It is there for the days when shame insists you have nothing of your own. On those days, there is something underneath you that doesn’t depend on how the week went. Atem nitzavim hayom kulchem: you stand as part of all of us, and as part of the whole you are a child, and a father’s mercy reaches you regardless.


This is why the connection at the heart of GYE matters so much. Someone once pointed out that there may be no other place where Jews from so many different communities engage with each other this closely, and about something this private. Men who would otherwise never meet are holding each other up and asking nothing in return. That is ahavas Yisrael in its plainest form, kulchem.


So as Rosh Hashana comes, bring what you have, and bring it without apology, your Torah, your mitzvos, your wins in the hardest place. And bring the strength of the whole along with it: Atem. You, standing with klal Yisrael as a child before a Father whose mercy was never in question. No Jew is ever pushed away entirely.


May the caring between members, the volunteering, and the chizuk given by people with nothing to gain awaken that mercy on this whole community and on klal Yisrael, and may we all be written and sealed for a good and sweet year. Kesiva vechasima tova.

JOYFUL TESHUVA · PART 4

Who Told You There Is No Tikkun?

This week the series continues with comforting words from a number of sources: any spiritual damage caused by the addictive behaviors that so many of us struggle with can be fully corrected.


One note before we start. As we have said, the subject of tikkunim, and actually doing them, is generally only recommended once you feel the behavior is no longer out of control. Until then the focus is on stopping, and on planning how to stop. So we are not getting into specific tikkunim here, only into the principle that a tikkun is possible and that it is doable, which is worth knowing for whenever the time comes.


1. The Shelah: there is nothing that teshuva does not repair


The earliest source on the question is the Shelah HaKadosh, close to four hundred years ago, and he opens by saying that he is writing to save the man who reads a passage in the Zohar and takes from it that there is no teshuva:


מתחילה אבוא להציל ממיתה נצחיית את הקורא המאמר הזה, שאין תשובה למי שחטא חטא זה, ואז החוטא יתייאש ויאמר כאשר אבדתי אבדתי, ויעשה משאלות לבו ויאמר נואש. אלא באתי לעורר החוטא שיקום בזריזות לעורר תשובה, ואל יתמהמה רגע אחד, כי אין דברי המאמר הזה כפשוטו שלא יועיל תשובה, כי אין דבר בעולם שלא יתוקן בתשובה, אפילו מי שכפר בעיקר.


First I come to save from eternal death the man who reads this passage and takes from it that there is no teshuva for one who sinned this sin, and then the sinner despairs and says, I am lost anyway, and does whatever his heart wants and gives up hope. Rather, I have come to rouse the sinner to get up quickly and awaken teshuva, and not to delay a single moment. Because the words of that passage cannot be meant the way they sound, that teshuva will not help. For there is nothing in the world that is not repaired by teshuva, even one who denied the ikar.


The Midrash Talpiyos, R’ Eliyahu HaKohen, the baal Shevet Mussar, says it harder. On the pious men who are machmir to go around saying there is no tikkun for this, he rules that they are not chassidim, pious ones, but chaseirim, ones who are lacking, and that their punishment is heavier than the punishment of the people they are talking about.


אלו החסידים המחמירים לומר אין תיקון לזרע לבטלה, כאשר שמעתי מפיהם - הם המרבים פושעים בישראל, ומרחקים לבני אדם מתחת כנפי השכינה. שכל מי שיש בו עון זה ושומע מפיהם שאין לו תקנה, הולך ועושה מה שלבו חפץ, באומרו וכאשר אבדתי אבדתי. ואלו הגורמים כך - אינן חסידים כי אם חסרים, וענשם כבד משלהם.


These pious men who are strict to say there is no tikkun for zera levatala, as I have heard from their own mouths - they are the ones who multiply sinners in Israel, and they push people away from under the wings of the Shechina. Because anyone who has this sin and hears from them that he has no remedy goes and does whatever his heart desires, saying, I am lost anyway. And those who cause this are not pious ones, they are ones who are lacking, and their punishment is heavier than theirs.


Shelah HaKadosh, Shaar HaOsiyos, Kedushas HaZivug (Hilchos Biah, os 24). Midrash Talpiyos, anaf zera levatala, os 7. Both are quoted in the commentary printed in most editions of the Kesser Rosh, where a typo runs them together as one source.



2. The Steipler: the seforim told you half of it


The Steipler is answering a man who had read in the seforim that this aveira seizes his zechuyos. He tells him first that nothing of the mitzvos themselves is taken away from him, not at all and not in the slightest. What gets seized is the flow of help those mitzvos generate, and only for a time. Then he says what the seforim left out:


הספרים הקדושים מתוך כוונה לעצור בעד החוטא כתבו בביאור עצום איך שע”י זה נחטפים זכויותיו, וקיצרו לבאר צד השני. דהיינו, אע”פ שכמה פעמים נכשל, מ”מ אם לעומת זה הרבה פעמים מנצח ומתגבר על התאווה, אז באותו פעם שמתגבר על התאווה הבוערת בו באופן נורא, הרי הוא ממשיך אור הקדושה על עצמו ועל העולמות באופן קדוש מאוד מאוד, וחלק גדול מאוד מהניצוצות הקדושות הרי הוא מוציאם ומחזירם לקדושה, ואין לשער גודל רוממות קדושת זה האדם הכובש את תאוותו בזמן תוקפו, והוא אז בבחינת יוסף הצדיק. והכלל, אדם שיש לו מלחמת היצר ופעמים מנצח פעמים נכשל, הרי כמו שהחטא נורא ואיום, לעומתו זכותו הוא נורא ואיום מאד, ויזכה עי”ז לתקן בחייו את מה שפגם ולהחזיר לצד הקדושה כל הניצוצות הקדושות שנעשקו ע”י עוונותיו.


The holy seforim, meaning to stop a man from sinning, wrote in enormous detail about how his zechuyos are seized through this, and they were brief in explaining the other side. Namely: even though he has stumbled many times, nevertheless, if against that he wins many times and overcomes the taava, then at that moment when he overcomes a taava burning in him in a terrible way, he draws a light of kedusha onto himself and onto the worlds in a very, very holy way, and a very large part of the holy sparks he takes back out and returns to kedusha. There is no measuring how high the kedusha is of this man who conquers his taava at the hour of its full force; he is then in the aspect of Yosef HaTzaddik. And the rule is this: a man who has the war of the yetzer, and sometimes wins and sometimes falls, just as the sin is awesome and terrible, correspondingly his zechus is awesome and exceedingly terrible, and through this he will merit to repair in his lifetime what he damaged, and to return to the side of kedusha all the holy sparks that were taken away by his aveiros.


Kereina D’igrasa vol. 1, 105-106.



3. Two examples of a tikkun


To conclude, two examples of a specific tikkun, from two different directions. The first, in the words of the Baal HaTanya:


אך החיות שבטפות זרע שיצאו ממנו לבטלה, אף שירדה ונכללה בשלש קליפות הטמאות - הרי זו עולה משם בתשובה נכונה, ובכוונה עצומה בקריאת שמע שעל המטה, כנודע מהאר”י ז”ל, ומרומז בגמרא: כל הקורא קריאת שמע על מטתו כאלו אוחז חרב של שתי פיות, להרוג גופות החיצונים שנעשו לבוש לחיות שבטפות, ועולה החיות מהם.


But the energy in what went out from him levatala, even though it went down and was absorbed into the three impure klipos, that energy comes back up out of there - through proper teshuva, and through כוונה עצומה, intense kavana, in the Krias Shema said at bedtime. This is known from the Arizal, and it is hinted at in the Gemara: whoever says Krias Shema on his bed is like a man holding a double-edged sword, to kill the outer bodies that became a garment for that energy, and the energy rises up out of them.


The second runs in a different direction: Torah. The Steipler comes back to it again and again in his letters, and this is from the same letter quoted above:


עיקר התיקון לחטא זה הוא התורה ובפרט יגיעת התורה היינו כשלומד גם בשעה שאין לו תענוג מהלימוד ולומד רק משום שכן צוה השי”ת כגון שחוזר הסוגייא הרבה פעמים או שמייגע מוחו להבין היטב ד”ת. הכלל מי שמייגע בתורה לש”ש ודאי שסוף סוף יזכה לתקן את אשר קלקל.


The main tikkun for this sin is Torah, and in particular יגיעת התורה, toiling in it. That means learning even at a time when he takes no pleasure in the learning, and learning only because that is what Hashem commanded - reviewing the sugya many times over, or wearing out his mind to understand a matter of Torah properly. The rule is: whoever toils in Torah lishma will certainly, in the end, merit to repair what he damaged.


R’ Aharon Leib Shteinman zt”l put it simply:


מה שנוגע על העבר ודאי אתה מתחרט, ועיין בכתר ראש (אות קלג) דמבואר שלימוד התורה מכפרת על העבר אם עושין תשובה בחרטה.


As for the past, of course you regret it - and see the Kesser Rosh (os 133), where it is explained that learning Torah atones for the past, if a person does teshuva with regret.


(This is based on the Zohar as explained by the Vilna Gaon and cited by R’ Chaim of Volozhin in Kesser Rosh)


Tanya, Likutei Amarim ch. 7; the Gemara is Berachos 5a. Kereina D’igrasa vol. 1, 105-106. R’ Aharon Leib Shteinman’s letter is printed at the front of Zos Brisi; R’ Shlomo Wolbe zt”l notes that the Chazon Ish showed him this section of the Kesser Rosh as well.

7 KEYS TO A STRONGER MARRIAGE · KEY #4

Let Your Spouse Influence You

Adapted from the research of Dr. John Gottman


The last three keys were about knowing your spouse, admiring them, and turning toward them in the small moments. This week’s key is about something harder for many of us, especially for husbands: actually letting your spouse’s opinion move you.


Gottman found one of the most striking predictors of a marriage’s future in a single question: does the husband accept his wife’s influence? In his research, marriages where the husband was unwilling to share power, to let his wife’s views genuinely affect his decisions, were vastly more likely to fail. And the ones where he did let her influence him were far more likely to thrive. Note the direction: Gottman found that wives generally already accept their husbands’ influence; the piece that’s often missing, and that makes the biggest difference, is the husband learning to yield.


Now, “accepting influence” doesn’t mean losing yourself or agreeing with everything. It means treating your spouse as a genuine partner whose perspective has real weight, so that when they raise a concern or want something, your instinct is to consider it and look for the yes, rather than to defend, dismiss, or dig in. It’s the difference between “here’s why you’re wrong” and “help me understand, you might be seeing something I’m not.”


The Torah’s picture of the husband who listens


This is old wisdom. The Gemara teaches, “if your wife is short, bend down and take counsel with her”, lower yourself to hear her perspective, because her insight is worth having. Chazal held up the man who honors his wife’s opinion, not the one who overrides it. In fact, the Gemara famously says a man should be careful with his wife’s honor, because blessing, in wealth and in the home, comes to a household on her account. The Torah’s vision of a strong husband is not the one who wins every argument. It’s the one secure enough to be moved.


And notice the Gemara’s balance, because Gottman found the same thing. The same passage that says “bend down and seek her counsel” also cautions against following blindly and thoughtlessly. Accepting influence isn’t surrendering your judgment; it’s genuinely weighing your spouse’s, so the decision becomes ours rather than a contest of wills. Two partners looking for common ground, not a winner and a loser.


Yielding is strength, not weakness


There’s a reason Chazal praised the one who is ma’avir al midosav, who can let go, bend, and not need to be right. The person who insists on winning every disagreement doesn’t actually have a strong marriage; he has a scoreboard. The one who can say “you make a good point, let’s do it your way” has something far stronger: a partner who feels genuinely heard, and who becomes, in turn, far more eager to hear him. Influence flows both ways once someone is brave enough to let it flow at all.


Your Action Steps This Week


  1. Find the yes. The next time your spouse raises something you’d instinctively push back on, pause and look for the part you can agree with. Start your response with that: “You’re right that…” It changes the whole conversation.
  2. Ask instead of defend. When you feel yourself getting defensive, replace the defense with a question: “Help me understand how you’re seeing this.” Then actually listen for the thing they might be right about.
  3. Yield on one real thing. Pick one genuine disagreement this week and let your spouse’s view carry the day, not as a concession you resent, but as a partner choosing “us.” Notice what it does to the temperature between you.
