“Project Kedusha” - A New Year of REAL Change

It is eleven at night in Lakewood, and a father is standing in his kitchen holding a phone that isn’t his.

He has just seen what his son saw. He doesn’t know how long it has been going on. He doesn’t know whether to take the phone away tonight or say nothing until the morning, and he doesn’t know which of those his son will remember for the next twenty years. What he does know is that there is nobody to ask. Not the neighbor, whose son sits next to his in class. Not his Rav, at this hour, about this. Not his own father.

So he does what everybody does. He opens a browser and types the sentence he cannot say out loud. A thousand answers come back: filters, articles, a therapist, a product, a lecture. Not one of them written for a frum father standing in his kitchen at eleven at night with one question about one child.

Until this year, that is where the story ended.

He is not the only one asking. Every day, questions like these reach Guard Your Eyes from Yidden around the world.

“I’m struggling with you-know-what”
“I think my husband is having a hard time”
“I’ve been clean for two years and slipped last night, is it over?”
“My chavrusah confided in me and I don’t know what to say to him”

A father in Lakewood: “My son saw something on his phone, what do I do?”
A rebbi in Flatbush: “I need help with a Talmid”
A mother in Yerushalayim: “We can’t afford a filter”
A bochur, alone, at two in the morning: “I gave my word to my Rav and broke it again”...

Kedusha is one of the greatest challenges facing Klal Yisroel today, and one of the hardest to even speak about.

Every Answer Exists. Nobody Can Find It.

Here is the strange part. Almost every one of those questions already has a good answer, and has had one for years.

Klal Yisroel is blessed with remarkable resources, built by dozens of different organizations. Filters. Kosher devices. Education. Chizuk. Recovery. Kosher entertainment. The problem was never that nothing exists. The problem is that it is scattered, and a father at eleven at night has no way to find it. Where do you start? What is right for my child? For my community?

Centuries ago a Yid with a practical question in halacha faced something of the same kind. The answer existed, spread across Bavli, Yerushalmi and the Rishonim, and most people had no way to reach it, until Rav Yosef Karo sat down and organized it. Lehavdil, that is our challenge today.

“We stopped asking what else GYE should build. The question became: why is there no single place a Yid can go with any question in this area and simply get an answer?”

That question became Project Kedusha, and this Elul it goes out to Klal Yisroel in three parts. The Kedusha Guide, in print. Kedusha.com, the same library alive online and powered by AI. And 866-KEDUSHA, a phone number, for anyone without internet. One address, free, anonymous, at any hour of the night.

Not a Pamphlet. A Book.

The Kedusha Guide is close to 100 full-color pages, the first of its kind: the best resources in the Jewish community gathered into one volume, in six sections. Kedusha Education. Chizuk. Struggles. Safeguards. Screen Addiction. Kosher Entertainment.

For parents: how to have the conversation, at every age. For chizuk: daily videos, shiurim and seforim. For those who struggle: the complete GYE framework, and therapy referrals. For protection, every filter, every kosher phone, all 70-plus TAG offices, and the devices people forget, the Xbox, the smart TV, the Tesla. And for the family, kosher entertainment for every age, so parents finally have an answer to “I’m bored.”

Wherever you see the GYE badge, it’s ours. Everything else is the work of others. Because this isn’t about one organization. It is all of Klal Yisroel’s solutions, in one place.

The response has been remarkable. Rav Yosef Elefent of the Mir called it a game changer. The goal is thousands of copies, through shuls, schools and events across America and Eretz Yisroel, because when a book sits on a shelf at home, or on a table in shul, it does something profound. It normalizes the conversation. No searching. No shame.

You Can Just Ask

A printed book is fixed the day it leaves the press, and technology changes weekly. Which is why the same library is alive at Kedusha.com, updated continuously with our partners at Mishor in Lakewood. When a new filter launches, a new device appears, a new challenge emerges, Kedusha.com already knows.

And then it does something no book has ever done.

The father in the kitchen types one sentence: “I found my son watching things he shouldn’t.” What answers him is not a page of search results. It answers with warmth, and then it does what a wise mentor would do. It asks how old his son is, and what device. Only then does it point him to the right resources, and to how the conversation in the morning should sound.

A rebbi with a talmid who is struggling. A man who can only bring himself to type, “I need help with… you-know-what.” A mother looking for kosher videos. Each guided to exactly what they need. Anonymously. Instantly. Without judgment. Every conversation is reviewed anonymously by our team, so the site learns, and shapes the next edition of the Guide.

For those without internet, the same intelligence is a phone call away at 866-KEDUSHA. Ask your question, get your answer.

“A man will type at two in the morning what he could never say out loud at two in the afternoon. Our only job is to make sure that what answers him is Torah, and that a human being is standing right behind it.”

Nineteen Years to Get Here

Guard Your Eyes is nineteen years old. It was founded by Yaakov Nadel and Yechezkel Stelzer, with the guidance of Rabbi Dr. Abraham Twerski zt”l and, yibadel lechaim, Rav Aharon Feldman shlita, and for most of those years the work looked nothing like a library. It looked like mentors. The battle began with the internet, then smartphones, then social media, and now AI, and as the technology changed, so did the response. Tens of thousands of Yidden have broken free using GYE’s framework of three pillars, motivation, planning and connection. Behind every number: marriages rebuilt, self-respect restored, fathers present again for their children.

Prevention was never an afterthought either. Back in 2013 GYE helped build the VCF filter, which became GenTech. It built Webshadow for parent-child accountability, published filter guides for parents, ran community Filterthons, and recently launched Project STEP, which walks a family through getting a filter installed, with sponsors covering the cost where needed. For adults there is the Kedusha Challenge, thirty days run by a Rav in his own kehilla over WhatsApp, in more than forty shuls last Shovavim. For teens, Esa Einai, fifty days, now used by thousands. Boys told us it was the strongest winter z’man they had ever had.

Every Jewish Bookshelf

Remember those questions? The father asking about his son’s first phone now has an address. The rebbi searching for resources has a library. The mother who couldn’t afford a filter has Project STEP. And the bochur, alone at two in the morning, is not alone anymore.

For nineteen years GYE has been identified with crisis, and crisis is hard to speak about; many never reach out at all, because of stigma and shame. Project Kedusha changes the subject. It puts GYE inside a far broader conversation: raising healthy families, education, wise technology choices, and yes, helping those who struggle.

Which is why the Guide was not written only for the person in the middle of a struggle. Every family in Klal Yisroel is already making these decisions: the first phone for a bar mitzvah bochur, the laptop a school now requires, the smart TV a neighbor is giving away. Most of us decide alone, quickly, and hope for the best. Suddenly the Kedusha Guide belongs on every Jewish bookshelf — not because of a crisis, but because everyone is navigating technology.

Every family deserves a trusted place to turn. Project Kedusha. One small step for countless individuals. One giant upgrade to Klal Yisroel’s Kedusha.

How to get the Kedusha Guide

The Guide is available now. Shuls, schools and organizations can request copies in quantity; an individual copy can be ordered link or number: need from you. Everything in it, kept current, lives at kedusha.com.

None of it runs on its own. GYE’s annual campaign, dates and goal: need from you, is what puts a Guide on the shelf of a home that would never have thought to ask for one, and keeps a mentor on the other end of the line at two in the morning.

What this version does differently

It opens on one person, not on the organization. The father in the kitchen is the whole lead, in the present tense, and the reader stays with him for three paragraphs before GYE is named. He then returns twice: at Kedusha.com, and in the closing.
The news arrives on the first screen. *"Until this year, that is where the story ended"* is the hook; the reveal is the three parts of Project Kedusha, about 500 words in, instead of halfway down.
The strongest idea moved up. The Shulchan Aruch parallel is the answer to *"why do they need us"*, so it now sits directly under the questions instead of in the middle of the piece.
Nineteen years of history is now one section near the end, not the second thing you read. Same facts, roughly 250 words: founders, three pillars, the AI intake, VCF/GenTech, Webshadow, Filterthons, Project STEP, Kedusha Challenge, Esa Einai. In a magazine, credibility earns its place after the reader cares.
