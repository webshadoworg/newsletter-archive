“Project Kedusha” - A New Year of REAL Change

It is eleven at night in Lakewood, and a father is standing in his kitchen holding a phone that isn’t his.

He has just seen what his son saw. He doesn’t know how long it has been going on, or whether to take the phone away tonight or say nothing until morning. What he does know is that there is nobody to ask. Not the neighbor, whose son sits next to his in class. Not his Rav, at this hour, about this.

So he does what everybody does. He opens a browser and types the sentence he cannot say out loud. A thousand answers come back: filters, articles, a therapist, a product, a lecture. Not one of them written for a frum father standing in his kitchen at eleven at night with one question about one child.

Until this year, that is where the story ended.

He is not the only one asking. Every day, questions like these reach Guard Your Eyes from Yidden around the world.

A father in Lakewood: “My son saw something on his phone, what do I do?”
A rebbi in Flatbush: “I need help with a Talmid”
A mother in Yerushalayim: “We can’t afford a filter”
A bochur, alone, at two in the morning: “I gave my word to my Rav and broke it again”...

Kedusha is one of the greatest challenges facing Klal Yisroel today, and one of the hardest to even speak about.

Most of It Isn’t an Emergency

That father’s night is the extreme case. Most of what a family needs is quieter, and far more common.

A work laptop the children sometimes borrow, which cannot be locked down without breaking the software you earn a living with. What goes on it?

A house where filtering every device properly would cost more than the family can spend. Which of the cheaper options are good enough, and where is it worth paying?

Parental controls that are switched on, and a twelve-year-old who worked around them in an afternoon. What do you look for instead?

A bar mitzvah bochur getting his first phone next month. What do you say, at what age, in which words?

A Motzaei Shabbos with children who are bored and nothing kosher to put on.

Not one of those is a crisis. All of them are decisions. Most of us make them alone, quickly, and hope for the best, and every one has a right answer somebody has already worked out. That is what was missing all along. Not answers. An address.

Every Answer Exists. Nobody Could Find It.

Klal Yisroel is blessed with remarkable resources, built by dozens of different organizations. Filters. Kosher devices. Education. Chizuk. Recovery. Kosher entertainment. Nothing was ever missing except a way to find it.

Centuries ago a Yid with a practical question in halacha faced something of the same kind. The answer existed, spread across Bavli, Yerushalmi and the Rishonim, and most people had no way to find it, until Rav Yosef Karo sat down and organized it. Lehavdil, that is our challenge today.

“We stopped asking what else GYE should build. The question became: why is there no single place a Yid can go with any question in this area and simply get an answer?”

That question became Project Kedusha, and this Elul it reaches Klal Yisroel in three parts. The Kedusha Guide, in print. Kedusha.com, the same library alive online and powered by AI. And 866-KEDUSHA, a phone number, for anyone without internet. One address, free, anonymous, at any hour of the night.

What Is Actually in It

The Kedusha Guide runs close to 100 full-color pages, the first of its kind: the best resources in the Jewish community gathered into one volume, across six sections. Kedusha Education. Chizuk. Struggles. Safeguards. Screen Addiction. Kosher Entertainment.

For parents: how to have the conversation, at every age. For chizuk: daily videos, shiurim and seforim. For those who struggle: the complete GYE framework, and therapy referrals. For protection, every filter, every kosher phone, all 70-plus TAG offices, and the devices people forget, the Xbox, the smart TV, the Tesla. And for the family, kosher entertainment for every age, so parents finally have an answer to “I’m bored.”

Wherever you see the GYE badge, it’s ours. Everything else is the work of others. This isn’t about one organization. It is all of Klal Yisroel’s solutions, in one place.

Rav Yosef Elefent of the Mir called it a game changer. Rav Elya Brudny shlita his own words and exact title: need from you. The goal is thousands of copies, in shuls, schools and events across America and Eretz Yisroel, because when a book sits on a shelf at home, or a table in shul, it does something profound. It normalizes the conversation. No searching. No shame.

You Can Just Ask

A printed book is fixed the day it leaves the press, and technology changes weekly. Which is why the same library is alive at Kedusha.com, updated continuously with our partners at Mishor in Lakewood. When a new filter launches, a new device appears, a new challenge emerges, Kedusha.com already knows.

Then it does something no book has ever done.

The father in the kitchen types one sentence: “I found my son watching things he shouldn’t.” What answers him is not a page of search results. It answers with warmth, and then does what a wise mentor would. It asks how old his son is, and what device. Only then does it point him to the right resources, and to how the conversation in the morning should sound.

The father with the work laptop gets a different answer than the father with a family iPad. Each guided to exactly what he needs. Anonymously. Instantly. Without judgment. Every conversation is reviewed anonymously by our team, so the site learns, and shapes the next edition of the Guide.

For those without internet, the same answers are a phone call away at 866-KEDUSHA.

“A man will type at two in the morning what he could never say out loud at two in the afternoon. Our only job is to make sure that what answers him is Torah, and that a human being is standing right behind it.”

Nineteen Years to Get Here

Guard Your Eyes is nineteen years old, founded by Yaakov Nadel and Yechezkel Stelzer with the guidance of Rabbi Dr. Abraham Twerski zt”l and, yibadel lechaim, Rav Aharon Feldman shlita. For most of those years the work looked like mentors, not a library. Tens of thousands have broken free using GYE’s three pillars: motivation, planning, connection. Behind every number: marriages rebuilt, self-respect restored, fathers present again.

Prevention was never an afterthought. GYE helped build the VCF filter in 2013, which became GenTech, built Webshadow for parent-child accountability, ran community Filterthons, and recently launched Project STEP, which walks a family through getting a filter installed, and finds a sponsor when cost is the obstacle. For adults there is the Kedusha Challenge, thirty days run by a Rav in his own kehilla over WhatsApp, in more than forty shuls last Shovavim. For teens, Esa Einai, fifty days, now used by thousands.

Every Jewish Bookshelf

Remember those questions? The father asking about his son’s first phone now has an address. The rebbi searching for resources has a library. The mother who couldn’t afford a filter has Project STEP. And the bochur, alone at two in the morning, is not alone anymore.

For nineteen years GYE has been identified with crisis, and crisis is hard to speak about; many never reach out at all, because of stigma and shame. Project Kedusha changes the subject. It puts GYE inside a far broader conversation: raising healthy families, education, wise technology choices, and yes, helping those who struggle. Which is why the Guide belongs on every Jewish bookshelf — not because of a crisis, but because every family is navigating technology, and most of us are deciding alone.

Elul is the season of kabbalos that rarely survive Cheshvan. Here is one that will. Pick one thing in the house: a conversation you have been putting off, a device with no real filter on it, one evening with something kosher on it. The Guide will tell you how, and Kedusha.com will answer when you get stuck.

Every family deserves a trusted place to turn. Project Kedusha. One small step for countless individuals. One giant upgrade to Klal Yisroel’s Kedusha.

How to get the Kedusha Guide

The Guide is available now. Shuls, schools and organizations can request copies in quantity; an individual copy can be ordered link or number: need from you. Everything in it, kept current, lives at kedusha.com.

None of it runs on its own. GYE’s annual campaign, dates and goal: need from you, is what puts a Guide on the shelf of a home that would never have thought to ask for one, and keeps a mentor on the other end of the line at two in the morning.
