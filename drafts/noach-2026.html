The Ark You Seal Yourself Into

You fell again, and nobody knows. You decide to keep it that way. You will handle it yourself, quietly, and maybe once it is under control you will tell someone. Closing the door feels like protection.


Then the door stays closed. The guilt and the shame have nowhere to go, and alone with them the pull gets stronger. Feeling bad is the opening the yetzer hara needs.


Shutting yourself in is not always wrong, though. Sometimes it is exactly what saves you.


Hashem told Noach, “Make yourself a teiva” (Bereishis 6:14), and the teiva carried him and his family through the Mabul. A year later He told him, צֵא מִן הַתֵּבָה, “Come out of the teiva” (Bereishis 8:16). The same ark that had saved his life was now the wrong place to be. Staying inside would have gone against what Hashem wanted.


We have a flood of our own, and we need a teiva too. Gedolim have called the beis medrash a teivas Noach, a shelter from the flood running through the streets, and any good thing we are absorbed in can work the same way.


The filter is a teiva as well. It is not watertight, and things can leak through, but it is a long way from giving the flood permission to wash over us every day. We built it in a clear moment, when we were at our best.


A good teiva keeps the flood out. Sealing yourself in alone keeps the help out, and leaves you inside with the flood. That one only looks like Noach’s ark.


Keep every good teiva you have, and leave this one. GYE’s experience, in every kind of struggle, is that staying clean for the long run takes partners. This week, tell one person what is really going on, a mentor or a friend. צֵא מִן הַתֵּבָה.
