Preference page changes

Three changes on the existing email preference page, from Mendy Jul 30. The “Notification settings” link in the new email points here, so this page has to make sense before the email ships.

1 · Unsubscribe-from-all copy — one insertion

“By choosing to unsubscribe from all, you will be removed from all our mailing lists, including our weekly newsletter. However, as a GYE member, you will still receive essential account-related emails, like password reset instructions and replies from the GYE team to conversations you started. To completely halt all communications, please log into your profile and either update your email address or delete your account.”

Why this matters more than it looks. It is what makes support replies survive unsubscribe-from-all, and it puts that in writing before the member clicks. Someone who unsubscribes at 11pm and then gets a reply from Chaim two days later has been told to expect it, which is the difference between a trusted email and a complaint.

It also matches the always-on toggle below, so the two places say the same thing.

What this settles

“Email privacy” is the answer to question 3

My one push-back on the spec was that v1 puts the agent’s real first line into preview text with nobody having chosen it. This radio is the choice. Once it exists, “Show message details” is a real decision the member made and the detailed preheader is legitimate.

So question 3 becomes a sequencing question, not a design one: generic preheader for everyone until this ships, real first line after. If this page ships alongside the email, the objection is gone on day one.

“Keep emails short” is the second template

It is the discreet template from the decision doc, and it needs building before this radio can do anything: from name GYE, subject “You have a new message,” fixed preheader, no message text in the body. Sketched on page 5.

Make it a member-level flag, not per-email-type. Someone who picks “keep emails short” should not then get a newsletter with a loud subject line.

Two small things worth deciding

  • The always-on row is a checked, greyed-out checkbox. That reads as broken to some people — a control that looks interactive and is not. The “Always on” label carries it, but a lock icon or a plain “Always on” badge with no checkbox would read cleaner.
  • Splitting team replies from member messages is the right split and it is worth saying why out loud: a member who mutes peer messages still needs to hear from staff. Those are two different relationships and they were sharing one toggle.
