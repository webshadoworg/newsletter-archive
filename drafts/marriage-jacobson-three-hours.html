MARRIAGE

Why do good marriages drift?

Based on a shiur by Rabbi YY Jacobson

Most of us assume that a marriage, once it is built, holds its position. You do the work before the wedding, you do the work through the first year, and after that it sits roughly where you left it.

It does not work that way. In most cases, Rabbi Jacobson says, a couple who are not actively working on their relationship will drift apart. Not because anything went wrong. Because that is the direction the current runs. Couples do not naturally drift back together.

Fire and water

The Baal HaTanya writes in Likutei Torah that a man and a woman are fire and water, and it is worth sitting for a moment with how unflattering that is. Fire and water do not seek each other out. Leave them alone in a room and they do not grow close; one of them wins. The only way they ever share a space is inside a vessel built to hold them both, and a vessel does not appear on its own. Somebody has to put it there, and keep it there.

So the drifting is not evidence that you married the wrong person. It is the baseline. The marriage you have after ten years is not the marriage you built. It is the marriage you maintained.

Why we daven three times a day

There is research, he says, that a husband and a wife each need to hear roughly every three hours that the other one is still in. Three hours sounds absurd, until you notice that we already run our most important relationship on exactly that clock.

Shacharis, Mincha, Maariv. Krias Shema in the morning, Krias Shema at night. We do not daven Mincha because Shacharis failed. We daven Mincha because a person who stood that morning and said I am here is not, by the middle of the afternoon, still standing in that place. Attention leaks. The Anshei Knesses HaGedolah knew that about human beings and built the shape of the whole day around it, and nobody has ever thought it was a character flaw to need Mincha.

A marriage runs on the same leak, and nobody built us a schedule for it.

The nine hour window

Here is the part that should come as a relief. Missing three hours costs almost nothing. Catch it by six, catch it by nine, and you are back where you were. The damage is never in the gap. It is in the gap that nobody closes. Miss a day, then two, then three, and a week later two people who genuinely love each other are living on separate planets, and neither of them can point to the thing that did it.

That is exactly what makes it so easy to miss. Nothing visible goes wrong. A plant that was watered last week still looks fine for a while. A lamp with no oil in it keeps burning for a while on yesterday’s oil. But we are not promised the Chanukah miracle every week. If you want the flame to still be there tonight, you put the oil in today.

Your action steps this week

Use the three times you already stop. You are already pausing three times a day. Attach one message to your wife to each of them. You do not need a new system, you need to hang this on the one you have.

Send one sentence with nothing in it. Not what time you are coming home, not what is needed from the store. One sentence whose only job is to say that you are here and you are thinking about her. A text counts.

Nine hours late is still on time. If you notice it has been since yesterday, that is not a failed week, and it is not a reason to decide you are bad at this. Say something now. Closing the gap is the whole skill.
