MARRIAGE

Nobody Drifts Closer

Based on a shiur by Rabbi YY Jacobson

Most of us assume a marriage holds its position. You put the work in before the wedding, you put the work in through the first year, and after that it sits roughly where you left it.

Rabbi YY Jacobson says it does not work that way, and that the reason is not laziness. It is closer to physics.

“If you don’t actively work on your relationship, naturally you drift away. I’m not gonna say in all cases, but in most cases… We don’t naturally drift back together.”

He brings the Baal HaTanya in Likutei Torah: a man and a woman are fire and water. Fire and water do not drift toward each other. Leave them alone in a room and they do not become close. They need a vessel that can hold them both, and somebody has to put the vessel there.

Then he gives a number.

“Ideally every three hours a woman needs to hear from her husband, I love you. And a husband needs to hear from his wife, yes, I’m with you.”

Three hours sounds absurd, until he points at where we already live by it.

“I looked at the Anshei Knesset HaGedolah. What did they do? They did every three hours another WhatsApp, another connection with God. Shacharit, Mincha, Maariv, Kriyat Shema, She’al Hamita. What’s this? Every few hours, I’m in, I’m in.”

We do not daven Mincha because Shacharis failed. We daven Mincha because a man who stood and said “I am here” at seven in the morning is not still standing in that place at three in the afternoon. Attention leaks. That is not a character flaw, it is how a person is built, and the Anshei Knesses HaGedolah built the shape of the day around it.

The merciful part is the recovery window.

“If we catch it in six hours or at least nine hours, you come back… But what if you don’t do it a day, and then two days, and then three days? Then after a week or two weeks, couples are in different planets.”

So a missed morning is not a failure. It is a morning. The damage is never in the gap, it is in the gap that nobody closes.

And it is easy to miss precisely because nothing visible goes wrong.

“It’s like a plant. If you don’t water it, it doesn’t continue to grow ’cause you water it last week… But I put in oil yesterday. Not every week are you gonna have the Hanukkah miracle.”

This week: three times in a day, say one sentence to your wife that carries no information. Not what time you are coming home, not what is needed from the store. One sentence whose only job is to say that you are here. A text counts.
