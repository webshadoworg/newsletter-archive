It’s eleven thirty at night in Lakewood, and a father is standing in his kitchen holding a phone that isn’t his.

He has just seen what his son saw. He doesn’t know how long it has been going on, or whether to take the phone away tonight or say nothing until morning. What he does know is that there is nobody he can ask.

Questions like these reach Guard Your Eyes every day, from Yidden around the world.

A rebbi in Flatbush: “I need help with a Talmid”
A mother in Yerushalayim: “We can’t afford a filter”
A father in Monsey: “When and how should I speak to my boys about Kedusha?”
A bochur, alone, at two in the morning: “I gave my word to my Rav and broke it again”...

Kedusha is one of the greatest challenges facing Klal Yisroel today, and one of the hardest to even speak about.

Most of It Isn’t an Emergency

That father’s night is the extreme case. Most of the help that families need is quieter and far more common.

A work laptop the children sometimes borrow, which cannot be locked down without blocking sites you need for parnassa.

A bar mitzvah bochur getting his first phone next month. What do you say, at what age, in which words?

A house where filtering every device properly costs more than the family can spend, and no way to know which cheaper options are good enough.

Parental controls that are switched on, and a twelve-year-old who worked around them in an afternoon.

A Motzaei Shabbos with bored children and nothing kosher to put on.

An adult with a kosher smartphone who reaches for it every free minute, realizing he is not present for his children yet can’t stop.

A family with no internet at all, who still has to pay a bill and order a ride.

Most of these aren’t a crisis, but they are important decisions. Most of us make them alone, quickly, and hope for the best. But we often choose the wrong answers, or at best, the easiest ones.

Every Answer Exists. Nobody Could Find It.

Klal Yisroel is blessed with remarkable resources. Nothing is missing except a way to find them.

Centuries ago a Yid with a practical question in halacha faced something of the same kind. The answer existed, scattered across Bavli, Yerushalmi and the Rishonim, and most people had no way to find it, until Rav Yosef Karo organized the practical halacha in the Shulchan Aruch. Lehavdil, that is our challenge today.

“We stopped asking what else GYE should build. The question became: why is there no single place a Yid can go with any question in this area and simply get an answer?”

That question became Project Kedusha, and this Elul it is being launched in three parts.

The Kedusha Guide, in print.
Kedusha.com, the same library alive online, and powered by AI.
And 866-KEDUSHA, all the answers just a phone call away – perfect for anyone without internet.

One address: Project Kedusha. All information in one place. Free, anonymous, any hour of the day or night.

What Is Actually in It

The Kedusha Guide runs close to 100 full-color pages, the first of its kind: close to 200 vetted resources from dozens of organizations, in one volume, across six Kedusha sections: Education, Chizuk, Struggles, Safeguards, Screen Addiction, and Kosher Isle (for kosher entertainment).

The Education section runs by age, from a book for a four-year-old to pre-marriage material, with school curriculums and resources for mechanchim.

The Chizuk section offers videos, Whatsapp groups, seforim and shul-wide programs to help you stay inspired.

The Struggles section offers GYE’s full gamut of resources and tools for those who struggle, including mentoring, spousal support and frum therapy referrals.

The largest section – and perhaps the most practical, is Kedusha Safeguards. Every filter in America and in Eretz Yisroel, compared. Every kosher phone, by region and community standard. Routers. Accountability software for the man who must keep the internet open. New Kosher AI tools. All the world-wide TAG offices plus Project STEP, for those that need help with setting up the filter or who can’t afford the price. Even the devices people forget, the Xbox, the smart TV, the Tesla. And call-in lines for rides, bills and email, for those with no internet at all.

Screen Addiction is the section nobody expects, because it isn’t about content at all: Offering community initiatives to help wean people from their screen addictions, software apps to help people cut down, lock boxes and timers to keep some distance.

And Kosher Isle offers safe alternatives for your bored children, kosher music, movies, phone hotlines to keep them entertained, and all the media devices to keep them protected and safe while they have fun.

Wherever you see the GYE badge, it’s ours. Everything else is the work of others. Because this isn’t about one organization; it is all of Klal Yisroel’s solutions in one place.

Rav Yosef Elefant of the Mir called the Kedusha Guide a “game changer”. Our goal is to have thousands of copies in shuls, schools and events across America and Eretz Yisroel, because when a book sits on a shelf at home, or a table in shul, it normalizes the conversation. No searching. No shame.

You Can Just Ask

But a printed book is fixed the day it leaves the press, and technology changes weekly. Which is why the same library is alive at Kedusha.com, updated continuously with our partners at Mishor in Lakewood. When a new filter launches or a new resource becomes available, Kedusha.com already knows.

In addition, the website does something no book has ever done.

The father in the kitchen types one sentence: “I found my son watching things he shouldn’t.” What answers him is not a page of search results. The website uses our proprietary AI database to answer with warmth, and it does what a wise mentor would. It asks his son’s age, and the type of device. Then it narrows down the resources to the few that fit the situation best, and even tells the father how the conversation with his son should sound.

So the father with the work laptop gets a different answer than the father with a smartphone, and the mother in Yerushalayim gets Israeli filters, not American ones. Anonymously. Instantly. Without judgment. And every conversation is reviewed by our team, so the site learns, and shapes the next edition of the Guide.

“A man will type at two in the morning what he could never say out loud at two in the afternoon. Our only job is to make sure that he gets the best resources possible in the most empathetic and Torahdik way, and that a human being is standing right behind it.”

Nineteen Years to Get Here

Guard Your Eyes is nineteen years old, founded by Yaakov Nadel and Yechezkel Stelzer with the guidance of Rabbi Dr. Abraham Twerski zt”l and, yibadel lechaim, Rav Aharon Feldman shlita. Tens of thousands have broken free using GYE’s three pillars: Motivation, Planning, Connection. Behind every number: marriages rebuilt, self-respect restored, fathers present again.

But prevention, stopping people from falling in in the first place, was never an afterthought either. GYE helped build the VCF filter in 2013, which became GenTech, built Webshadow for parent-child accountability, launched community Filterthon events, published books and prevention tips for parents. That work continues today with Project STEP, which finds a sponsor for a filter when cost is the obstacle. The 30 day Kedusha Challenge program for Rabbanim to run in their shuls with their kehilla. It has run in more than forty shuls in the past year; and the 50 day Esa Einai program has done the same for thousands of teens during the weeks of Shovavim.

Removing the Shame

GYE has always been identified with crisis, and crisis is hard to speak about; many never reach out at all because of stigma and shame. Project Kedusha changes the conversation. The Guide belongs on every Jewish bookshelf — not because of a crisis, but because every family is navigating technology today.

Elul is the season of kabbalos, but unfortunately our kabbalos rarely survive Cheshvan. But here is one that will. Pick one thing in the house: a conversation you keep putting off, a device with no real filter on it, or one new chizuk program to get stronger. The Guide will tell you how.

Every family deserves a trusted place to turn. Project Kedusha: “One small step for countless individuals. One giant upgrade to Klal Yisroel’s Kedusha.”

How to get the Kedusha Guide

The Guide is available now. Shuls, schools and organizations can request copies in quantity; an individual copy can be ordered at kedusha.com/order. Everything in it, kept current, lives at kedusha.com. And the hotline 866-KEDUSHA is available to answer any question.
