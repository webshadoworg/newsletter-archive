# 03 Sources

## Before a source goes in

- **Is it a re-run?** Check `newsletter-archive/campaigns/` (151 issues) before proposing anything.
  A previously sent piece is out however good it is. The archive has gaps; say so rather than
  claiming a clean check.
- **Is it שווה לכל נפש?** Can this reader be the person in it, and can he make that move this week?
  An extreme case is out however well cited. See [01-sensitivities.md](01-sensitivities.md).
- **Does it share the point, or only the topic?** A source that shares the subject but not the
  argument is an extra piece. Flag every one.
- **Write the sentence the excerpt argues before proposing it.** A mood is not a point.
- **Never judge a source from a free sample.** The publisher picks the free chapter. Get the part the
  sender actually pointed at.

## Citing

- **Give the year on anything written annually.** "The Sfas Emes says" is unverifiable and Mendy
  checks. `Sfas Emes, Re'eh 5633`.
- **Read the actual text before writing from it.** Verify on Sefaria. Strip nikud before comparing;
  Sefaria's Hebrew is vocalized and a plain-text match will call a verbatim quote missing.
- **Quote the earliest source.** A famous line in circulation is often a later writer's, merged in by
  a printing error.
- **Pick the most accepted mekor**, not the sharpest, for a claim the reader wants to reject.
- **Cite the sefer the piece is built on, once.** Not every pasuk. Verify the rest into the issue's
  `decisions.md`.
- **A trusted author carries his own sources.** We cite him by name, so his citations ride with his
  name. Do not re-check them.
- **Verify attributions inside supplied copy.** A source named in copy a human sent is still
  unchecked. Flag it; do not fix it.
- **Readers go looking for the sefer.** Asked for twice in the feedback log. Print the sources.

## Handling a mekor

- **Take the sharper reading.** Softening a mekor to make it more comforting weakens it, and it asks
  the reader to pretend his falls were free.
- **Translate in the reader's register.** A mekor's verdict on a person gets softened, because he is
  that person.
- **Keep the loshon kodesh.** Print יראת כבוד, not "the awe of honour". A gloss is often less clear
  than the term. See [07-hebrew.md](07-hebrew.md).
- **Print the mekor, do not perform it.** One line of setup, the quote, stop. The "notice what he did
  here" paragraph always gets cut.
- **Describe the source in its own terms, then draw the parallel in our voice.** Do not smuggle the
  parallel into the description.
- **A parallel is "an idea we see", not "the same point".**
- **Land it in a concrete moment.** The abstract statement is not the dvar Torah. The dvar Torah is
  the moment the reader recognises himself in.
- **Don't stack transliterated Hebrew with dash-glosses.** Say the idea in English; keep one or two
  Hebrew terms the reader already owns. Paraphrase the mekor's move instead of quoting its lomdish
  hinge.

## Never

- **Never print an invented quote in a real person's name.** Label draft wording as draft.
- **Never put a teaching in a named rebbe's mouth from an anonymous story.** Print it as a chasidic
  story and let a verified pasuk carry the idea.
